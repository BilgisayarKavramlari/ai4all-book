## Before the lab (about 10 minutes)
Choose a concept from your major that is new to you and a course source with keyed questions (textbook end-of-section or practice questions). Copy three questions, answer them without notes ("don't know" is fine) and rate your confidence (0–100%) for each. Write your tutor-mode setup: role, task, context (course, level, source), constraints (attempt first; one hint at a time; no full answer unless you ask after two attempts; say so if my answer is wrong), output format (one question or hint per turn; ask me to explain it back at the end). If your source has no keyed questions, write three questions from its headings or learning objectives only and look up the answers after step 3.

## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. **Briefing (10 min).** Review the five steps of a session (Figure 7.11) and the codes: **S** (you recalled or produced the answer, or got a hint after an attempt) and **C** (the tool recalled or produced what you needed to). Check your setup against Figure 7.10. Privacy: no personal, confidential or patient data; you will remove names and identifiers from the exported transcript.
2. **Dialogue (25 min).** Run your tutor-mode session alone, in a fresh, temporary chat. Attempt before every hint. Note the turn numbers where you slid toward answers. Aim for at least 10 of your own turns.
3. **Closed-book check (10 min).** Devices and notes away. Write a predicted result for each question (0–100%), then answer. Students with testing accommodations get extended time; the step can move to the end of the period.
4. **Score and verify (10 min).** Score your answers against the key. A teammate may check your scoring; you may keep your answers private and score them yourself. Check one of the tool's explanations against your source, not against the tool's own verdict. If the tool was wrong, or gave the answer despite your setup, **save this for your Failure Diary (Chapter 4).**
5. **Start the annotation (15 min).** Code your first 4–6 turns S or C with a one-line reason. Each team brings one scaffold turn and one crutch turn to the class discussion.
6. **Exit (5 min).** Write one change you will make before your delayed check.

At home (by Monday 11:59 p.m.): delayed closed-book check at least two days after the lab (predict first), finish the annotation (at least 6 coded turns), verify at least two explanations, write the 300–400-word reflection and the AI Use Declaration (A4).

## Expected observations
Most students slide into answer mode at least once, often when tired or stuck, and many tools give away the answer despite the setup at some point. Immediate closed-book scores are usually lower than students predict after a smooth dialogue; delayed scores are often lower again, but predictions get closer. Some students find that the tool marked a wrong attempt as correct; the key catches it.

## If it does not work
If the tool ignores your setup, record that as a result (it belongs in the annotation and possibly the Failure Diary) and restate the constraint once. If a tool refuses or errors, try a smaller concept. If you cannot use a tool, follow the alternative assignment's route with a classmate (peer-tutor script) or by self-explaining a textbook worked example. If you miss the lab, run the same steps at home and score yourself.

## Debrief (3–5)
1. Which turn did the most for your learning, and who recalled or produced the answer in it? (analyze)
2. Where did your prediction differ most from your result, and why? (evaluate)
3. Did the tool ever say a wrong answer was right? How did you find out? (evaluate)
4. What would you change in your setup next time? (create)

## Deliverable and rubric
A4 (Application Assignment, Chapter 7): setup, keyed questions with baseline, dialogue transcript, annotation, closed-book record (immediate and delayed), verification, reflection (a)–(e), AI Use Declaration. 10-point rubric in the chapter. Closed-book scores are not graded.

## Accessibility and alternatives
No-device or opt-out route: the alternative assignment (human or text scaffold plus annotation of the dated transcript pair). Screen-reader-friendly record sheet (a plain table). Extended time for closed-book steps. The dialogue may be in any language, with English glosses of annotated turns. Self-scoring keeps answers private.

## Safety and privacy
No personal, confidential or patient data in prompts. Use a fresh, temporary chat where the tool allows it. Remove names and identifiers from exported transcripts before submitting. Marcus's rule applies to everyone in clinical programs: no patient information in any AI tool.
