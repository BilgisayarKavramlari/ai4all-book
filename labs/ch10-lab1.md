## Steps (tool-neutral)
Never put personal or confidential information, yours or anyone else's, into a public AI tool. Replace your own and other people's details with placeholders ([PHONE], [ADDRESS], [NAME]) or use the university-provided tool. No step uses an AI detector.

1. **Briefing (10 min).** The voice and ownership spectrum (F10.3) and the coursework zones (section 10.2; A7 itself allows co-drafting); keep, rewrite or reject with the five reason tags (F10.7); the Verification Funnel for facts in a draft (F10.8); the privacy rule.
2. **Frame and draft (15 min, individual).** From v0, write a prompt with the parts of Chapter 5's anatomy of a prompt labeled (role, task, context with the reader and your own points, constraints, output format). Produce v1 and at least one iteration (a second tone or a tighter constraint). Record the tool, the model if shown, and the date. No-AI route: take the provided dated draft or the suggested edits as v1.
3. **Annotate (20 min, individual).** Code every sentence of v1 K, R or X with a tag and a few words. If you used the tool only as a reader, code each of its comments instead. If you write in a second language, compare each v1 sentence with your own v0 note: is this what you meant? List the factual claims (names, dates, numbers, policies, quotations) and mark the three with the highest stakes.
4. **Optional pair read (10 min).** Opt-in only. If you choose to, a partner reads v0 and v1 without your codes and marks two places where v1 "doesn't sound like you" or "promises something." Compare with your codes. If you prefer not to share your document, read the provided sample draft and mark it instead.
5. **Share and critique (15 min).** Each team shows the class one sentence rewritten back into its writer's voice, and one disclosure decision with its reason (the three questions of section 10.7).
6. **Exit (5 min).** One sentence of v1 you rejected, and its tag.

[COMPASS: ch10-lab1 — the A7 sheet, the provided dated drafts, current notes on version history and placeholders]

## Expected observations
Tool drafts of short professional documents often add stock phrases ("passionate", "I am writing to inform you"), stronger commitments than the writer made, and generic replacements for specific examples; facts the writer supplied usually survive, but invented specifics (a lab's method, a date) appear in some drafts. Many students keep most sentences after small rewrites; "kept almost everything, with reasons" and "rejected most of v1" are both valid results.

## Debrief
1. Which reason tag did you use most, and what does that say about where the tool pulled your writing? (analyze)
2. Which claim was highest-stakes, and what verdict did it get? (evaluate)
3. Would you disclose AI use to this document's reader? Why? (evaluate)

## Deliverable and rubric
Individual: A7 items 1–4 started in class; the rest at home, about 50 minutes (graded with the A7 rubric in Chapter 10). Pair notes are not submitted.

## Accessibility and alternatives
No-device route: printed provided drafts (alternative assignment). Screen-reader-friendly A7 sheet; codes are letters, never colors. Documents may be drafted in any language; v2 is in the language its reader needs, with an English gloss if that is not English.

## Safety and privacy
Placeholders for all personal details, yours included; the university-provided tool by default; no confidential workplace documents; no detector use; the pair read is opt-in.
