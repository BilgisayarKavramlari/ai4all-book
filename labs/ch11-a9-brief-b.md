# A9 Scenario B′ — Spring Fundraiser (one-page brief)

*Constructed scenario for Chapter 11's A9. File: data/ch11_scenario_b.csv (on your course site as a spreadsheet and a screen-reader-friendly table).*

**The decision.** A student association can run **one** fundraiser this spring. The board votes on March 31; the treasurer prepares the analysis and owns the recommendation. The money pays for members' professional-exam study materials.

**The three options.**
- **Trivia night** (indoors): the association ran one last fall. Net proceeds $620; about 40 volunteer hours.
- **Graduation photo booth** (partly outdoors): a partner club ran one last spring and reported net proceeds of $900 with about 55 volunteer hours; the association has never run it.
- **Plant sale** (outdoors): never run by anyone on campus; a local garden club would donate the plants. Volunteer hours estimated at 30.

**The five criteria.**

| Criterion | Source | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Expected net proceeds ($) | Last run of the same event (trivia: own records; photo booth: the partner club's figure) | higher is better |
| Volunteer hours needed | Estimates from the events' organizers | **lower is better** |
| Weather risk (1 low – 3 high) | Board's judgment: indoors 1, partly outdoors 2, outdoors 3 | **lower is better** |
| Members who said they would help | A members' survey: **14 of about 60 members answered** | higher is better |
| Fit with the association's mission (1–5) | Average of the board's ratings | higher is better |

**Why two cells are blank.** The **plant sale** has no proceeds figure because it has never been run. The **photo booth** has no survey count because it was added to the list after the survey closed.

**What to ask the AI tool.** "Build a decision matrix from this file: scale each criterion to 0–10, reverse any criterion where lower is better, propose weights, compute totals and recommend one option. Show your scaling rule and how you handled the blanks." (Use this prompt or your own; record the tool, model if shown, and date.)

**Your job (A9).** Audit the matrix: for each criterion, what it means and measures, whether it is a proxy (for whom is the gap bigger?), how blanks were handled and whether a real value can be obtained before March 31; check that "lower is better" criteria were reversed; recalculate the top two; run two sensitivity tests; write the memo to the board.
