## Steps (tool-neutral)
Never put personal, confidential, patient, student or client information into a workflow you test. On the pre-lab sheet, write your guide (your "field" for pairing) and bring two or three inputs inside your scope for your peer.

1. **Briefing (5 min).** The five-part frame and the use map (section 19.1), the four safeguards (section 19.6) and the peer-test rules: run only within the stated scope; no personal data; record, do not fix. The instructor reads out the pairs by guide.
2. **Build and run (30 min, individual, in mixed-field teams of 3–4).** Build your prototype (template, chain or configured tool). Run your typical, different and edge inputs. Record each output, tool kind, model name if shown, and date. Mark anything reused from A11. *Checkpoint at minute 20:* at least one run recorded.
3. **Peer test (20 min, in pairs from different guides).** Your partner picks one of the inputs you brought and runs your prototype with only your written instructions. You watch and record with the sentence frame: "With input ___, at step ___, I expected ___ but saw ___. I was confused by ___." Swap at minute 10. If no partner from another guide is available, the instructor confirms it on your sheet and a same-guide peer counts in full.
4. **Share and critique (10 min).** Place each member's cell on the class map poster; discuss one cell where two fields disagreed and the rule or evidence behind each view.
5. **Exit (10 min).** Write one change you will make, and one use your card will exclude. Photograph your sheet: it counts as A13's record of the lab runs and the peer test.

**Missed the lab:** ask any classmate from another guide to run your prototype before A13 is due, or use the instructor's office hours as your peer test; record which. **Alternative route:** use your provided scenario (posted by your instructor before the lab session) and its inputs.

In Compass (optional): ch19-lab1, with the A13 template and the peer-test card; also on your course site.

## Expected observations
Most first builds pass the typical input. The edge input is where field knowledge shows: look-alike names in health, units in engineering, sarcasm or negation in survey text, missing values in lab data. Peer testers from other fields most often stumble on unexplained abbreviations and on outputs that give no reason. Several students find that a fixed template or "no AI" fits one of their parts best; that is a valid result.

## Debrief
1. Which of your three inputs failed, and what does that tell you about your field's hard cases? (analyze)
2. What did your peer tester see that you did not? (evaluate)
3. Which cell on the class poster did two fields disagree about, and which rule decides it? (evaluate)
