# Lab 23.1 — Map It

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Studio |
| Objectives | O2 (LO5) · tags 4D:DISC, UNESCO:ETH-U |
| Time | About 25 minutes including reading this sheet and the debrief; optional; with a partner, in a discussion group or alone (solo route below) |
| Materials | A constructed op-ed paragraph arguing that "AI tutors should replace office hours" (the instructor package may supply a longer one; the sample below works on its own); paper or the argument-map template from section 23.1 |

## Sample op-ed paragraph (constructed for this lab)
"Office hours are a relic. Most students never go: the times clash with work and other classes, and a line outside a professor's door wastes everyone's afternoon. An AI tutor answers at 2 a.m., never runs out of patience and can explain the same idea five different ways. Universities are already stretched thin, and every hour a professor spends repeating last week's lecture is an hour not spent on research. It is time to replace office hours with AI tutors and let professors do what only they can do."

## Steps (tool-neutral; no AI tool needed)
1. **Read (3 min).** Underline the claim. Number every stated reason.
2. **Map (8 min).** Draw the claim at the top, reasons on the left, objections on the right, using the section 23.1 grammar: arrowhead = supports, flat bar = opposes. Add at least one unstated premise, drawn dashed and labeled "inferred." Add two objections, at least one aimed at a premise rather than at the claim. At most nine boxes.
3. **Swap (6 min).** Exchange maps with a partner. Each of you finds one premise the other missed or stated unfairly. **Solo route:** compare your map with the model map at the end of this sheet instead, and find one premise you missed or one the model map missed.
4. **Redraw (4 min).** Redraw one link and write one sentence: "The disagreement sits in …"

## Expected observations
The paragraph's stated reasons are about cost and availability; its unstated premises are usually about what office hours are for (answers only, or also relationships and judgment). Most pairs find that their objections target that unstated premise.

## If it does not work
If you cannot find an unstated premise, ask: "What would have to be true for this reason to support the claim?"

## Debrief (3)
1. Which premise did your partner find that you missed, and why did you miss it? (analyze)
2. Is your best objection aimed at the claim or at a premise? (evaluate)
3. State the op-ed's side in a form its author would accept. (create)

## Deliverable and rubric
The redrawn map and one sentence. Ungraded.

## Model map (for the solo route; one fair map among several)
- **Claim:** AI tutors should replace office hours.
- **+ Reason 1:** students rarely use office hours (times clash; lines waste time).
- **+ Reason 2:** AI tutors are always available, patient and can explain an idea many ways.
- **+ Reason 3:** professors' time is better spent on research than on repeating lectures.
- **+ Unstated premise (dashed, inferred):** office hours exist only to answer content questions.
- **+ Unstated premise (dashed, inferred):** an AI tutor's explanations are accurate enough to rely on without a check.
- **− Objection 1 (denies the first unstated premise):** office hours also build relationships, mentoring and judgment that a tutor does not supply.
- **− Objection 2 (opposes the claim):** low attendance argues for moving or changing office hours, not for replacing them.

## Accessibility and alternatives
The map can be written as an indented outline (claim; + reasons; − objections, with "denies premise n"). Plain-text version of the paragraph.
