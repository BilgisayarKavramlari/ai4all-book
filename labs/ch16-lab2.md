## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. Open the file and count the rows (400; the first line is a note, not a question).
2. Compute the share of correct answers overall.
3. Compute it by language (English, Spanish), then by year (first-year, other years).
4. Compute it for the four language × year subgroups, with each subgroup's size.
5. Which subgroup does the overall figure hide? How many questions does it contain, and is that enough for a stable rate?
6. Write two sentences for the help desk: what to report every month, and one change to test.

## Expected observations
Overall 364 of 400 (91.0%); English 262 of 280 (93.6%), Spanish 102 of 120 (85.0%); first-year 140 of 160 (87.5%), other years 224 of 240 (93.3%). Subgroups: English first-year 112 of 120 (93.3%), English other years 150 of 160 (93.8%), Spanish first-year 28 of 40 (70.0%), Spanish other years 74 of 80 (92.5%). The intersection (Spanish, first-year) is far below every single-characteristic rate; with 40 questions it is a clear gap but still worth a larger sample.

## Debrief
1. Why did neither single characteristic show the size of the gap? (analyze)
2. What would you add to the monthly report? (apply)
