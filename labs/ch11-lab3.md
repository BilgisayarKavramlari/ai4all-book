# Lab 11.3 — Proxy Hunt

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Check It (optional) |
| Objectives | O3 (LO5) · tags 4D:DISC |
| Time | 20 minutes, at home |
| Materials | The three proxy questions (F11.8); a library database or search engine for step 3 |

## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. Choose five everyday metrics: one from campus (for example, attendance), one from health (step counts), one from work (sales calls per day), one from city services (complaints filed) and one from online ratings (star averages).
2. For each, answer the three questions: What do we mean? What do we measure? For whom is the gap bigger?
3. Find one documented case, with a source you opened, in which a metric stood in badly for its goal: a library database or a news search for the metric's name with "unintended" or "gaming" is a good start. Cite it (Chapter 9).
4. Mark the proxy's place: in the inputs or the label (section 11.3).

You may ask an AI tool for candidate cases; treat its answer as a lead and confirm each case in a source you opened.

*In Compass (optional): worked answers for the five sample metrics.*

## Expected observations
The third question is the hardest: students often name who scores low, not who scores low for reasons unrelated to the goal. Cases are easy to find in health, education and policing; check that the source documents the gap rather than asserting it.

## Debrief
1. For which metric was the gap hardest to name, and why? (evaluate)
2. Was your documented case a proxy in the inputs or a proxy label? (analyze)

## Deliverable and rubric
Ungraded. Your answers can feed A9's criteria audit.

## Accessibility and alternatives
Text-only; works without any AI tool.

## Safety and privacy
Public sources only; no personal data.
