# Lab 10.2 — One Message, Three Tones

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Explore |
| Objectives | O2 (LO2) · tags 4D:DESC |
| Time | 20 minutes, at home (optional) |
| Materials | A real message you need to send this week; any AI tool, or pen and paper |

## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. Write your own five points for the message first (what you want, why, what you propose, what the reader should do, anything you must not promise).
2. Produce three versions in named tones (for example, formal, warm and brief), with a tool or by hand. If you use a tool, name the reader and the tone in the prompt, and replace personal details with placeholders.
3. Ask one person which version they would want to receive, and why.
4. Write one sentence on what you learned about your own tone.

This mirrors the concept session's demonstration (section 10.3).

## Expected observations
The same points read very differently across tones; writers often predict their own tone better than readers perceive it (compare Kruger et al., 2005). The version a reader prefers depends on the relationship.

## Debrief
1. Which tone did your reader prefer, and did it match your guess? (evaluate)
2. Which words carried the tone most? (analyze)

## Deliverable and rubric
Ungraded. The three versions can serve as A7's "at least one iteration."

## Accessibility and alternatives
By-hand route needs no device; the demonstration email is in the companion files in print and screen-reader formats.

## Safety and privacy
Placeholders for names and contact details; no confidential workplace content.
