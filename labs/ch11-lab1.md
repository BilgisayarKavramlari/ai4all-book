## Steps (tool-neutral)
Never put personal or confidential information into a prompt. Scenario C uses only your own data, and only in a tool your institution provides or approves (for example, an enterprise chat assistant with data protection), or no AI at all. Scenario C compares options, never people.

1. **Briefing (10 min).** Decision roles (F11.3), the three proxy questions (F11.8), the six places to look (F11.12), matrix anatomy (F11.14); the privacy rule.
2. **Draft (10 min).** Teams of 3–4 on A′ or B′ give an AI tool the scenario file with the prompt in the brief (or your own), or use the provided dated output. Save the prompt, tool, model (if shown) and date. Scenario C students work alone from their own table.
3. **Criteria audit (15 min, individual).** For each criterion: what it is meant to capture, what the data measure, whether it is a proxy and for whom the gap is bigger, how blanks were handled and whether the real value can be obtained (A9 item 3). The brief gives the reason for every blank.
4. **Recalculate and test (20 min, individual).** Enter the raw values, ranges and the tool's weights in the spreadsheet template and compare its totals for the top two options with the tool's, giving each a verdict on Chapter 8's scale (supported, partly supported, unsupported, contradicted, cannot verify). Change one weight or one blank and record the new top option, the margin and the shortlist (A9 items 4–5).
5. **Share and critique (15 min).** Each team or solo student reports whether its top option survived and one hidden assumption it found.
6. **Exit (5 min).** Name one assumption you would not have seen without checking.

*In Compass (optional): scenario briefs, A9 template, current notes on giving a tool a file and asking it to show its formulas or code.*

## Expected observations
AI-drafted matrices often score a blank as the lowest value or drop it without a note, add "lower is better" criteria without reversing them, and present one winner without margins. Scenario-specific observations are in the instructor key (private/ch11-a9-key.md), so that the scenarios stay unsolved for students. "The top option survived" and "the arithmetic was correct" are valid results.

## Instructor notes
- **Setup:** post both briefs, both CSV files and the template a week ahead; print packs for the no-device route. Have the dated AI outputs for A′ and B′ ready for teams whose tool fails or who prefer not to use one.
- **Timing:** step 4 is the bottleneck; the template does the scaling, so students check the tool's totals rather than computing six totals by hand. If time is short, recalculate one total in the session and the second afterward.
- **Solo route:** scenario C students and any student who prefers it work alone; pair them for step 5.
- **Key:** private/ch11-a9-key.md (not for students).

## Debrief
1. Which criterion in your matrix was the clearest proxy, and for whom was the gap bigger? (evaluate)
2. Did your sensitivity test change the top option, the margin or the shortlist? What should the owner take from that? (analyze)
3. For which blank could the real value be obtained, and how? (apply)

## Deliverable and rubric
Individual: A9 items 2–5 started in the lab session (graded with the A9 rubric in Chapter 11). Team drafts are shared but ungraded.

## Accessibility and alternatives
No-device route: printed scenario sheets and the provided matrix (alternative assignment). Screen-reader-friendly CSV files and A9 sheet in the companion files. Solo work is available for any student who prefers it.

## Safety and privacy
Constructed scenario data only; no real candidates, employees, patients or customers; for scenario C, options not people, your own data only, in a tool your institution provides or approves (Chapter 15).
