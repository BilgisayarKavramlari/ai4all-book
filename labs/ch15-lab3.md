# Lab 15.3 — A Call-Back Drill

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Practice (paper, optional; pairs) |
| Objectives | O4 (LO5) · tags 4D:DIL |
| Time | 15 minutes |
| Materials | Two role cards: an urgent caller asking to change a customer account's email (section 15.4 worked example) and a voice message asking a hotel desk to refund a card (faded example) |

## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. Round 1: one partner plays the urgent caller; the other applies the rule "nothing changes on a call alone": a code to the email on file, and a second approver. Swap roles.
2. Round 2: the hotel voice message. Pause, choose an out-of-band channel, name the second approver.
3. Write a verification plan for your family, club or team that says *how* you would check a request for money, logins or data (for example, "call back on the number already in my phone," "agree a phrase in person"). Never write a real phrase or phone number on the sheet.

No real people are called and no real accounts are touched.

## Expected observations
Urgency and politeness pressure the person who answers; a written rule makes it easier to say no.

## Debrief
1. Which part of the rule was hardest to follow under pressure? (analyze)

## Deliverable
Ungraded. The plan can support A12's social-engineering row.

## Accessibility and alternatives
The drill can be done in writing, one card at a time.
