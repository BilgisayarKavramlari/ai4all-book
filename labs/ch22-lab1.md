# Lab 22.1 — Tag a Launch Post

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Check It |
| Objectives | O1 (LO1, LO3) · tags 4D:DISC, UNESCO:TECH-U |
| Time | About 25 minutes including reading this sheet and the debrief, at home; optional |
| Materials | The constructed launch post and evaluation excerpt below (printed; no account or internet needed) |

## The launch post (constructed; fictional company and model; dated March 3, 2027)
"Today we release Lumen-4. (1) Lumen-4 scores 91% on a graduate science benchmark, up from 78% for Lumen-3. (2) It is our most accurate model ever. (3) It completes coding tasks that take engineers a full day. (4) Independent testers at an evaluation nonprofit found it solves 64% of their software tasks. (5) It uses 40% less energy per answer than Lumen-3. (6) By 2028 it will replace most customer-support work. (7) It was trained on licensed and public data only. (8) It never makes up citations."

## The evaluation excerpt (constructed; a fictional nonprofit; dated March 20, 2027)
"We tested Lumen-4 on 120 software tasks: 58% succeeded on the first try. We did not test science questions or energy use."

## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. **Tag (12 min).** For each of the eight claims, write its as-of date, its status (measured, reported, forecast; or "no source given" after you trace it) and who could check it. Every claim shares the post's date, March 3, 2027: write that date once at the top of the column, and note any claim that is about a different time (one claim is about 2028; one refers to a test whose date the post does not give).
2. **Compare (5 min).** Claim 4 and the excerpt disagree. Which number would you cite, with what date and status?
3. **Rewrite (5 min).** Rewrite claims 3 and 8 so that a reader sees their status.

## Expected observations
Most claims are reported (the developer's own post). Claim 4 is the developer's account of someone else's test: trace it to the excerpt, which is the outside source and gives a different figure (58%, measured, March 20, 2027). Claim 6 is a forecast. Claim 8 is an absolute claim that no finite test can show.

## If it does not work
If a claim seems to fit two statuses, write both and say what would decide it.

## Debrief (3–5)
1. Which claim would a journalist most likely repeat without a tag? (evaluate)
2. What does claim 2 even claim? (analyze)
3. What one outside test would you fund first, and why? (evaluate)

## Deliverable
The tag table and two rewrites. Ungraded; check your work against the answer notes below after you finish.

## Answer notes (read after you finish)
- Claims 1, 2, 3, 5, 7: reported (March 3, 2027); who could check: an outside evaluator running the named benchmark (1), a defined accuracy test (2), timed engineering tasks (3), an energy audit with a stated boundary (5), a training-data audit (7).
- Claim 4: reported as the developer's account; traced, the excerpt (March 20, 2027) measured 58% on 120 tasks. Cite the excerpt: "An evaluation nonprofit reported in March 2027 that Lumen-4 succeeded on 58% of 120 software tasks on the first try."
- Claim 6: forecast (about 2028); claim 8: reported, and no test can show "never".
- Sample rewrite of claim 3: "The developer says Lumen-4 completes coding tasks that take engineers a full day; it gives no test, and the one outside test found 58% success on software tasks."

## Accessibility and alternatives
Plain-text post and excerpt; the table as an accessible template; no chart reading required.
