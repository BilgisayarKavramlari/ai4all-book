### Starting a fresh chat (no Compass needed)
Most chat tools have a "new chat" button; a new chat does not include your earlier conversation. Many tools also offer a temporary or private chat that is not saved, and a memory option in their menus that you can switch off. If you cannot find these, note it in your probe record, or use the dated sample outputs. [COMPASS: ch03-lab1 — current steps for temporary chats, memory and search options by tool; tokenizer pages]

### Probes (tool-neutral)
1. **Tokens.** Ask the model to count a letter in a long word or name. Then ask it to spell the word letter by letter first and count again. Record whether the tool showed a reasoning step before answering. A correct answer is a valid result; explain why it might happen (section 3.1).
2. **Sampling.** One open prompt from your own field, five times, each in a new chat. Mark what stays and what changes (section 3.2).
3. **Context.** The provided long public-domain text with one fact near the start, one in the middle and one near the end. Ask about each fact in a new chat with the full text (section 3.4).
4. **Cutoff and search.** Questions about the provided dated events, with the search option off and, where the tool allows, with search on. Do not rely on the model's self-reported cutoff (sections 3.3, 3.5).

**Extension (if time).** Test the reversal curse with a less famous parent–child pair from the provided list: ask in both directions, in new chats. (The paper's best-known example is widely repeated online, so it is a weak test.)

Record for every probe: the tool, the model name if shown, the options used (search on or off, memory on or off) and the date.

## Instructor notes (not for students)

### Expected observations
Probe 1: results vary by tool and word; many current tools answer short words correctly, especially tools that show a reasoning step; longer words or letters that appear more than twice fail more often (Fu et al., 2024). Probe 2: the core content often repeats while wording, order and examples change; for questions with one right answer the variation is smaller. Probe 3: the middle fact is the most likely to be missed or confused, but newer tools may answer all three; either result is evidence. Probe 4: without search, events after the cutoff produce a statement of no information or a plausible but invented answer; with search, answers improve but sources must be checked. Extension: newer tools may answer both directions; a correct answer does not show the curse is gone in general.

### Further debrief questions (for the share-and-critique part of class or for homework)
1. What is one thing your probe does **not** show? (evaluate)
2. Which of your probes tested something that came from training, and which tested something in the context window? (analyze)
3. Turn one probe result into a labeled link between a Chapter 3 term and a Chapter 2 term. (create)

### Preparation
Prepare the long text (about 3,000 words, public domain) with three planted facts; 5–6 dated events from the last few months; a list of 10 less famous parent–child pairs with sources. Generate and date the sample outputs one week before the lab. Remind students on Tuesday to bring their A2 Part 1 draft. Probe results differ by tool; the point is the method, not a specific outcome.
