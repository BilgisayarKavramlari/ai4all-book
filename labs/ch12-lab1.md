# Lab 12.1 — Tool Bake-off

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Studio (lab session) |
| Objectives | O4, O5, O6 (LO2, LO5) · tags 4D:DISC, 4D:DESC, 4D:DIL |
| Time | 75 minutes in the lab session (briefing 10 · practice 45 · share 15 · exit 5), plus about 13 minutes before the session |
| Materials | Your A10 task split into two or three parts; for the part you test, the task frame with 3–4 success criteria and three test inputs (typical, different, edge); the data class of the input (Chapter 12, section 12.5 table); the team's dated terms cards (at least three tools, including one tool under institutional terms, such as a tool your institution provides or approves); the A10 sheet (F12.16); the provided dated outputs of a second tool in the companion files, for anyone who can reach only one tool or prefers not to create an account |

## Steps (tool-neutral)
Use only public information, your own non-sensitive work or the provided material; never put personal data about other people, internal or confidential information, or restricted or legally protected information into a prompt. You never need to create a new account for this lab.

1. **Briefing (10 min).** Fit belongs to a task and a tool together (F12.3); the data gate and the section 12.5 table (F12.9); the bake-off rules (F12.11); correctness and polish scored separately (F12.10), with polish on a 1–5 scale (1 rough · 3 usable after edits · 5 ready to send). The three A10 routes: two tools; one tool and no AI; the provided outputs.
2. **Terms jigsaw (12 min).** In a jigsaw, each member studies one piece and teaches it to the others. Each team member takes one tool's dated terms card and answers the three data-terms questions: (1) is input used for training, by default, and can you opt out? (2) how long is it kept, and who can see it? (3) consumer or institutional terms? The team fills one gate table and checks each member's input against it.
3. **Runs (33 min, individual).** Run the same prompt, word for word, in two tools (Route A) or one tool plus your own no-AI work (Route B) on your three inputs, each in a fresh chat with memory and standing instructions off. Route C students score the provided outputs. Record the tool, the model if shown and the date for each run. Score each output as you go: correctness per criterion, polish, time including checking. Check the typical input's correctness with an independent check (the source document or your own recalculation). If time runs short, finish the edge input at home.
4. **Share and critique (15 min).** Each team shows the class one output whose polish and correctness disagreed, and one gate decision (pass, pass after changing the input, or blocked).
5. **Exit (5 min).** Name one part of your task you will keep on the floor (no AI), and why.

In Compass (optional) and in the companion files: the terms cards and the A10 sheet. Current tool notes are in Compass and on your course site.

## Expected observations
Most outputs look finished, so polish scores cluster high while correctness varies. The edge input is where tools most often fill in something that was not there; tools with search sometimes fill a missing value from a page about something else. Some students find that no AI is faster once checking time is counted, and some find that the gate blocks the better tool. All are valid results.

## Debrief
1. Where did polish and correctness disagree, and what would a grader who looked only at polish have concluded? (evaluate)
2. Which data-terms answer surprised your team most? (analyze)
3. Change one choice, such as the stakes of a part or the time you allow: does any cell's decision flip (Chapter 11's sensitivity check)? Mark untested cells "predicted" and single-run cells "tested once" (apply)

## Deliverable and rubric
Individual: A10 items 3 and 4 started in the lab session (graded with the A10 rubric in Chapter 12). The team's gate table is shared but ungraded.

## Accessibility and alternatives
No-device or no-AI route: the alternative assignment's provided dated outputs of two tools and the printed terms cards. Screen-reader-friendly A10 sheet in the companion files. Inputs in any language are fine, with an English gloss of each criterion.

## Safety and privacy
No personal, confidential, patient or client data. Follow the section 12.5 table: no personal data about other people, internal or confidential information, or restricted or legally protected information in a consumer tool; no restricted or legally protected information in any tool for work in this book. Use placeholders when a part does not need real names (Chapter 15).
