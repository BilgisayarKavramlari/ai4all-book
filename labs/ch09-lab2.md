# Lab 9.2 — Lateral Reading Sprint

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Check It (optional) |
| Objectives | O2 (LO3) · tags 4D:DISC |
| Time | 20 minutes, at home or in Compass (optional, recommended) |
| Materials | The list of three websites on your course site, chosen from sites analyzed in published lateral-reading studies (for example, the minimum-wage site used by Wineburg & McGrew, 2019, and the climate site used by Breakstone et al., 2021; the instructor checks each site's current state and the papers' descriptions before release); a timer |

## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. Open the first site. Do not read beyond its name and front page heading.
2. Set a timer for two minutes. Leave the site: search for the organization's name with words such as "funding", "founded by" or "who runs".
3. Record who is behind the site, which outside sources told you, and how long it took.
4. Repeat for the other two sites.
5. Compare your answers with the key on your course site (also in Compass) and with the study that used each site.

You may ask an AI tool "who is behind this site?", but record its answer as a lead and confirm it in outside sources (Chapter 8: a tool's own answer is never a source).

[COMPASS: ch09-lab2 — answer key (also on the course site) with the published studies' descriptions of each site]

## Expected observations
Most students find the sponsor within two minutes once they leave the page; the site's own pages rarely state it plainly. In Wineburg & McGrew (2019), fact-checkers often left a page within about half a minute. Results depend on what the search engine shows on the day (as of October 2026).

## Debrief
1. Which outside source was most useful, and why could you trust it more than the site itself? (evaluate)
2. What on the site's own pages would have misled you if you had read vertically? (analyze)

## Deliverable and rubric
Ungraded. Your notes can serve as practice for A6's outside-source log. Lateral reading informs your use decision, not the source's rung.

## Accessibility and alternatives
Printed screenshots of each site's front page and of the provided search-result pages; screen-reader-friendly versions on the course site.

## Safety and privacy
No sign-ins, no downloads, no forms; read only.
