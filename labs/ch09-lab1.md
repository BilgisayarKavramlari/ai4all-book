# Lab 9.1 — Source Audit

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Studio (in class) |
| Objectives | O2, O3, O4, O6 (LO3; O6 also LO4) · tags 4D:DISC, 4D:DIL |
| Time | 75 minutes, Thursday, February 25, 2027 (briefing 10 · practice 45 · share 15 · exit 5), plus about 10 minutes before class |
| Materials | Your A6 question, purpose and claim; one AI summary (at least five sentences) of an abstract or an open-access article of at most 1,500 words, with tool, date and prompt (or the provided dated summary on eLearning); the team source pack (four sources on one shared question: a gray report, a preprint, a news story, a peer-reviewed article); the A6 sheet (F9.15) |

## Steps (tool-neutral)
Never put personal or confidential information into a prompt, and never upload a paywalled or confidential document to a public tool.

1. **Briefing (10 min).** The source ladder inside Chapter 8's funnel (F9.8a–b); the four SIFT moves; what counts as an outside source; the drift types (9.4, 9.5); the upload rule.
2. **Team jigsaw (15 min).** Each member takes one source from the pack and reads it laterally for five minutes, using outside sources only. Place it on the ladder for the claim printed on the pack. Teams of three share the fourth source. Compare rungs and note any rung that moved after lateral reading.
3. **Fidelity check (15 min, individual).** Check every sentence of your own summary against its original: passage, verdict, drift type. Swap with a teammate and check two of each other's verdicts.
4. **Trace (15 min, individual).** Trace one claim from your own A6 sources at least two hops, or to a dead end, running Chapter 8's three checks at each hop. Record the verdict and the sentence you would write instead (A6 item 3).
5. **Share and critique (15 min).** Each team shows the class one source whose rung moved after lateral reading and one summary sentence with its drift type.
6. **Exit (5 min).** Name one source you would not rely on for your A6 claim, and why.

[COMPASS: ch09-lab1 — the team source packs, the A6 sheet, current tool notes]

## Expected observations
Lateral reading often moves gray literature and polished websites down a rung, and sometimes moves a plain-looking source up. Summaries of short abstracts are often faithful in most sentences; broadened claims and dropped caveats are the drift types students find most often (compare Peters & Chin-Yee, 2025). Traces of statistics found through AI answers often end in a dead end. "Faithful" and "kept its rung" are valid results.

## Debrief
1. Which source moved most after lateral reading, and what did the outside source show? (evaluate)
2. Which drift type was most common in your class's summaries, and why might that be? (analyze)
3. Where did your trace end, and what verdict does Chapter 8's rule give? (apply)

## Deliverable and rubric
Individual: A6 items 3 and 4 started in class (graded with the A6 rubric in Chapter 9). The team's rung table is shared but ungraded.

## Accessibility and alternatives
No-device route: the printed source pack and the provided dated AI summary (alternative assignment). Screen-reader-friendly A6 sheet and source pack on eLearning. Sources in any language are fine, with an English gloss of each passage cited.

## Safety and privacy
No personal, confidential or patient data; open-access or library-licensed sources only; no uploads of paywalled PDFs to public tools (Chapters 15 and 18).
