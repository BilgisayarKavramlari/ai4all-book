## Steps (tool-neutral)
Never put personal or confidential information into a prompt, and never upload a paywalled or confidential document to a public tool.

1. **Briefing (10 min).** The source ladder and its two rules (a citation lifts a source only if the cited record holds this claim; opening the cited record does not raise the source's rung); lateral reading and the use decision ("rely on it" needs both a sufficient rung and nothing troubling about who is behind it); verdicts and drift types; the upload rule.
2. **Team jigsaw (15 min).** Each member takes one source from the pack. Open it to find the printed claim and place it on the ladder. Then read laterally for five minutes, using outside sources, and make a use decision. Teams of three share the fourth source. Compare rungs and use decisions, and note any use decision that changed after lateral reading.
3. **Summary check (15 min, individual).** Check every sentence of your summary against its original: passage, verdict, drift type if one fits; then look for an omitted key result. Swap with a teammate and check two of each other's verdicts.
4. **Trace (15 min, individual).** Trace one claim from one of your own sources at least two hops, or to a dead end, running Chapter 8's three checks at each hop. Record the verdict and the sentence you would write instead (A6 item 3).
5. **Share and critique (15 min).** Each team shows one source whose use decision changed after lateral reading, and one summary sentence with its verdict and drift type.
6. **Exit (5 min).** Name one source you would not rely on for your A6 claim, and why.

## Expected observations
Lateral reading often turns gray literature and polished websites from "rely on it" into "use with a caveat" or "only as a lead," without changing their rung. Summaries of short abstracts are often faithful in most sentences; broadened claims and dropped caveats are the drift types students find most often (compare Peters & Chin-Yee, 2025). Traces of statistics found through AI answers often end in a dead end. "Faithful" and "kept its use decision" are valid results.

## Debrief
1. Which source's use decision changed most after lateral reading, and what did the outside source show? (evaluate)
2. Which drift type was most common in your class's summaries, and why might that be? (analyze)
3. Where did your trace end, and what verdict does Chapter 8's rule give? (apply)

## Deliverable and rubric
Individual: A6 items 3 and 4 started in class (graded with the A6 rubric in Chapter 9). The team's table is shared but ungraded.

## Accessibility and alternatives
No-device route: the printed source pack and the provided dated AI summary (alternative assignment). Screen-reader-friendly A6 sheet and source pack on eLearning. Sources in any language are fine, with an English translation or explanation of each passage cited.

## Safety and privacy
No personal, confidential or patient data; open-access or library-licensed sources only; no uploads of paywalled PDFs to public tools (Chapters 15 and 18).
