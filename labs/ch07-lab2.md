# Lab 7.2 — Reread or Retrieve?

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Compare |
| Objectives | O2, O5 (LO4) · tags UNESCO:HCM-U, UNESCO:HCM-A |
| Time | 15 minutes, then a 5-minute check two days later; at home or in Compass (optional) |
| Materials | A short reading from a course you are taking this term (about two pages); paper or a blank document. No AI tool needed |

## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. Split the reading into two halves of similar length and difficulty: A and B.
2. **Make the counting list:** write down each half's main ideas (its headings or topic sentences). You will count recalled ideas against this list, so a main idea counts once if your recall states it in any words.
3. **Half A:** reread it twice, carefully.
4. **Half B:** read it once, then close it and write down everything you remember. Do not check against the text (checking would add an extra study of half B only).
5. **Predict:** for each half, write how many of its main ideas you expect to recall in two days.
6. **Two days later**, without looking, write everything you remember from each half. Count the main ideas recalled from each, using your list.

[COMPASS: ch07-lab2]

## Expected observations
Many students expect to remember the reread half better and in fact recall more of the half they tested themselves on, as in Roediger and Karpicke (2006). One small test on yourself is a signal, not proof; the halves may differ in difficulty.

## Debrief (3–5)
1. Which half did you expect to remember better, and which did you? (evaluate)
2. What was your prediction gap for each half (predicted minus recalled main ideas)? (apply)
3. What might make your two halves unequal, and how would you fix that next time? (analyze)
4. Which course could you study this way this week? (apply)

## Deliverable and rubric
Ungraded. The result can support A4's reflection item (b).

## Accessibility and alternatives
Recall can be spoken and recorded instead of written. Any reading format works.

## Safety and privacy
No data leaves your notes.

## Instructor notes
Good opener for Tuesday's review in week 5 (Chapters 4 and 8 week): ask a few students to share their predicted-versus-recalled pattern.
