# Lab 7.2 — Reread or Retrieve?

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Compare |
| Objectives | O2, O5 (LO4) · tags UNESCO:HCM-U, UNESCO:HCM-A |
| Time | 15 minutes, then a 5-minute check two days later; at home or in Compass (optional) |
| Materials | A short reading from a course you are taking (about two pages); paper or a blank document. No AI tool needed |

## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. Split the reading into two halves of similar length and difficulty: A and B.
2. **Half A:** read it twice, carefully.
3. **Half B:** read it once, then close it and write down everything you remember. Check against the text and note what you missed.
4. Rate how well you expect to remember each half in two days (1 = not well, 7 = very well).
5. Two days later, without looking, write everything you remember from each half. Count the main ideas recalled from each.

[COMPASS: ch07-lab2]

## Expected observations
Many students expect to remember the reread half better and in fact recall more of the half they tested themselves on, as in Roediger and Karpicke (2006). One small test on yourself is not proof; the halves may differ in difficulty.

## Debrief (3–5)
1. Which half did you expect to remember better, and which did you? (evaluate)
2. What might make your two halves unequal, and how would you fix that next time? (analyze)
3. Which course could you study this way this week? (apply)

## Deliverable and rubric
Ungraded. The result can support A4's reflection item (b).

## Accessibility and alternatives
Recall can be spoken and recorded instead of written. Any reading format works.

## Safety and privacy
No data leaves your notes.

## Instructor notes
Good warm-up for Tuesday's discussion of desirable difficulties; ask a few students to share the predicted-versus-actual pattern.
