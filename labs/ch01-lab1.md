# Lab 1.1 — Same Question, Two Answers

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Compare |
| Objectives | O1 (LO1) · tags UNESCO:TECH-U, LM:how-works |
| Time | 20 minutes, concept session, in class |
| Materials | Two free AI tools or tools your institution provides or approves, or one tool used in fresh conversations; no account needed with the sample outputs in the companion files |

## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. Choose three questions: one factual, one practical, one from your major.
2. Ask each question in two different tools, or twice in one tool using new conversations.
3. For each pair of answers, record: what is the same, what differs, whether either gives sources, and how certain each sounds.
4. Save the full outputs, not only your notes. You will reuse them in section 1.4 and in A1.

## Expected observations
Answers overlap but differ in wording, order and sometimes content. Few state their uncertainty. If two runs are identical, try a more open question; identical answers are possible and also worth noting.

## Debrief (3–5)
1. Were the differences in content or only in wording?
2. Which answer would you trust more, and what is your reason besides how it sounds?
3. Did any answer cite a source? Did you open it?
4. What would you need to do before using either answer in graded work? (decide/justify)

## Deliverable and rubric
Comparison table for three questions (feeds A1).

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Exceeds | Three complete pairs, differences classified (content vs. wording), sources checked |
| Meets | Three complete pairs with differences and certainty recorded |
| Approaching | Two pairs, or differences described vaguely |
| Not yet | One pair or no recording |

## Accessibility and alternatives
Screen-reader users can work from the plain-text sample outputs in the companion files. Students without a laptop can use a phone browser or the printed sample outputs. Students who prefer not to use AI compare the two provided outputs.

## Safety and privacy
No personal, confidential or institutional data in prompts.

## Instructor notes
Run it after the concept-session demo. Collect two striking pairs for discussion. Common problem: students compare only length; prompt them to compare claims.
