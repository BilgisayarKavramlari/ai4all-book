# Lab 13.1 — Describe It, Then Check

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Studio |
| Objectives | O3 (LO5) · tags 4D:DIL, 4D:DISC |
| Time | 25 minutes (10-minute version available), at home; optional |
| Materials | Your own drawing, chart, or photo of an object or a public place with **no people in it and not your home or anything that shows where you live**, or the provided image (course site: a constructed city-street drawing, dated); any AI tool that accepts images, chosen after the data gate; for the audio extension, the provided clip and script (recorded for this lab by a consenting speaker) |

## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. **Choose an image (2 min).** No person may be identifiable in it, including you, and it must not show your home or where you live.
2. **Data gate (5 min).** Read the tool's terms on whether uploads may be used for training and who can see them (Chapter 12); if you did Lab 12.2, reuse your notes. Remember that the uploaded file, with its metadata such as location, reaches the provider; remove location data if you can. Choose a tool and option that fit; if none fits your own image, use the provided one.
3. **Your alt text first (5 min).** Write one sentence of purpose (what must a reader who cannot see the image learn?), then your alt text.
4. **Tool draft and check (8 min).** Ask the tool for alt text. Mark each claim in its draft keep, fix or cut, checking it against the image yourself.
5. **Decide (5 min).** Which version should a screen-reader user get, and why? Record the tool, model if shown, and date.

**Audio extension (optional, 10 more minutes; not counted in the 25):** transcribe the provided clip with a tool; mark every word that differs from the provided script. Never upload a recording of another person's voice.

**10-minute version:** steps 1–3.

In Compass (optional): how to upload an image and switch off training in several tools.

## Expected observations
Drafts are usually fluent and often include details that are wrong (medium, colors, small text) or not needed for the purpose. Small text is the most common error after resizing (section 13.1). The best final version usually starts from the student's purpose sentence.

## If it does not work
If a tool cannot accept images, use the provided dated sample output (model name and date) and check it against the provided image.

## Debrief (3–5)
1. Which claims in the tool's draft were wrong, and which were true but unneeded? (analyze)
2. Who relies on this alt text, and could they have caught the errors? (evaluate)
3. What did your purpose sentence change about the final version? (evaluate)
4. Would you disclose the tool's help where you publish the image? Why? (evaluate)

## Deliverable and rubric
Purpose sentence, both versions, the marked draft and a two-sentence decision. Ungraded.

## Accessibility and alternatives
Students who are blind or have low vision may reverse the roles: describe the provided image from its long description and check a tool's draft against that description, or work with a partner. No-device route: the provided image and dated sample output on paper.

## Safety and privacy
No identifiable people and no home locations; data gate before any upload (the file and its metadata reach the provider); provided clip only for the audio extension.

## Instructor notes
Collect a few anonymized drafts with errors to show in class; the pattern (fluent, confident, partly wrong) connects to Chapter 4's hallucination and Chapter 8's checks.
