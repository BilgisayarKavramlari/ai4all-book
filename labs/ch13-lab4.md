# Lab 13.4 — The Ear Test

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Break It |
| Objectives | O5 (LO5) · tags 4D:DISC |
| Time | 15 minutes (10-minute version: five clips), at home; optional |
| Materials | Ten provided short clips (eLearning): recorded for the course by speakers who gave written consent, some replaced by synthetic versions made with their written consent; an answer sheet with a confidence column; the key |

## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. **Before each clip,** read nothing about it. Listen once.
2. **Record** your answer (real or synthetic) and your confidence (50–100%).
3. **Score** yourself with the key. Compare the share of synthetic clips you caught with Mai et al. (2023), whose listeners correctly identified 73% of the deepfake clips (overall accuracy on real and fake clips was about 70%). Then compare your average confidence with your accuracy (Chapter 7's calibration of your own confidence: how closely your confidence matches how often you are right).
4. **One sentence:** what does this mean for a phone call that asks you to act?

## Expected observations
Accuracy varies widely; confidence is often higher than accuracy. Some real clips will be judged synthetic.

## Debrief (3–5)
1. Was your confidence higher or lower than your accuracy? (analyze)
2. Which clips fooled you, and what did you listen for? (analyze)
3. What check would work on a phone call where listening does not? (apply)

## Deliverable and rubric
Answer sheet with confidence and the one-sentence conclusion. Ungraded.

## Accessibility and alternatives
Students who are deaf or hard of hearing, or who prefer not to listen: judge the provided transcript-and-spectrogram pairs (a spectrogram is a picture of a sound's pitches over time), or skip without penalty.

## Safety and privacy
Clips are made for the course with written consent; never clone or upload anyone's voice for this lab, including your own.

## Instructor notes
Commission the clip set before term (instructor package): consenting speakers, written consent covering synthetic versions and classroom use, documented tool and date for each synthetic clip. If consent cannot be obtained, run the non-audio route for everyone.
