# Lab 23.3 — The Imitation Game on Paper

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Explore |
| Objectives | O1, O3 (LO5, LO1) · tags 4D:DISC, UNESCO:TECH-U |
| Time | About 30 minutes including reading this sheet and the debrief; optional |
| Materials | Six provided transcripts of short text conversations, about 250 words each (instructor package): three with a person (written by volunteers who consented to their use; no personal details), three with AI systems (model names, dates and whether a persona prompt was used, on a separate answer sheet); a guess sheet. **Solo route without the package:** ask two people to each write a short chat transcript on a topic you give them, generate two with a tool (record model name, date and whether you used a persona prompt) and ask a third person to shuffle them and keep the key |

## Steps (no AI tool needed)
1. **Judge (12 min).** Read each transcript once. Write "person" or "system" and your confidence (0–100%). Note in a few words what you relied on (spelling, humor, knowledge, emotion, speed of topic changes).
2. **Score (4 min).** Read the answer sheet. Count your hits and compare your average confidence with your hit rate (Chapter 7's calibration idea). Six guesses are far too few to measure your calibration; treat the comparison as a lead about your cues, not a result.
3. **Compare (4 min).** Compare your cues with those Jones and Bergen (2026) reported: interrogators judged mostly by style and social and emotional cues.
4. **Write (4 min).** Two sentences: what did your guesses rely on, and what would your result show, and not show, about understanding?

## Expected observations
Hit rates near chance are common; cues are mostly about style. Check the answer sheet's persona column: in Jones and Bergen (2026), persona-prompted systems were judged human far more often than the same systems without the persona prompt (73% against 36% for GPT-4.5).

## If it does not work
If you recognize a transcript's source, mark it "known" and leave it out of your count.

## Debrief (3)
1. Which cue misled you most? (analyze)
2. Would Turing count your result as evidence of thinking? Would Searle? (evaluate)
3. Design one question that you think would separate person from system, and say what answering it would and would not show. (create)

## Deliverable and rubric
The guess sheet and two sentences. Ungraded.

## Accessibility and alternatives
Transcripts in plain text; may be read aloud by a partner.
