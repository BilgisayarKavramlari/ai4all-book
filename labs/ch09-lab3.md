## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. Choose one open-access abstract from your field and keep it open.
2. Ask a tool: "Summarize this abstract in three sentences." Record the tool, the model if shown, the date and the prompt.
3. If you can, ask a second tool, or the same tool in a new chat.
4. For every sentence, find the supporting passage and give a verdict on Chapter 8's scale; if the verdict is not "supported," name the drift type when one fits (dropped caveat, broadened claim, cause from correlation, changed number). Then check the whole summary for an omitted key result.
5. Write one sentence on what differed between the two summaries.

This mirrors the Worked Example in section 9.5. "Both summaries were faithful" is a valid result.

## Expected observations
Three-sentence summaries of short abstracts are often faithful in most sentences; when they drift, broadened claims and dropped caveats are the most common types, consistent with Peters & Chin-Yee (2025) for the models they tested. Two runs can drift in different places, because each run samples again (Chapter 3).

## Debrief
1. Which sentence was hardest to code, and why? (evaluate)
2. Would asking the tool to "be accurate" have helped? What did Peters & Chin-Yee (2025) find (Deep 9.B)? (explain)

## Deliverable and rubric
Ungraded. The coded summary can be used for A6 item 4 if the abstract belongs to one of your three sources.

## Safety and privacy
Open-access abstracts only; no paywalled or confidential documents uploaded to public tools.
