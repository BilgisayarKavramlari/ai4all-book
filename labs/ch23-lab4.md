## Scenario (constructed for this lab)
A hospital buys an AI tool from a vendor that drafts discharge summaries from the patient's record. The hospital's IT office set it up and its pharmacy committee approved it; the vendor's contract says the hospital is responsible for review. On a busy evening, the tool drafts a summary for a patient who is going home. The record lists a penicillin allergy, but the draft leaves it out. A resident physician, at the end of a twelve-hour shift with eight discharges still to sign, reads the summary quickly and signs it. The hospital's policy asks residents to check allergies, but the summary screen does not show the allergy list next to the draft. The community pharmacy fills a prescription based on the summary. The patient has an allergic reaction and recovers in the emergency department. Afterward, no one can say why the tool dropped the allergy; only the vendor can examine its logs.

## Two views you will use (from section 23.4)
- **Matthias (2004):** we hold makers and operators responsible because they control a system and can foresee what it will do; learning systems act in ways no one can predict, so a responsibility gap opens.
- **Tigard (2021):** there is no new gap; moral responsibility is a dynamic, flexible process, and our practices of holding people responsible can stretch to cover new technologies.

## Steps (no AI tool needed)
1. **List every actor (5 min).** Human and non-human, everyone and everything that played a part. (From the Field: actor-network theory counts non-human actors in the map; listing them is not assigning moral responsibility.)
2. **Chain (3 min).** Place the case on Chapter 4's failure-to-harm chain: which link failed?
3. **Four gaps (8 min).** For culpability, moral accountability, public accountability and active responsibility, write one forward-looking duty and a named owner by role.
4. **Judge (4 min).** One sentence: is anyone culpable on Matthias's view? On Tigard's? Use the two views printed above.

## Expected observations
A full actor list: the vendor, the tool, the hospital (its IT office and pharmacy committee), the resident, the summary screen and record system, the community pharmacy and the patient. Most groups find that the resident is the nearest person (a possible moral crumple zone) while the vendor alone can examine why the omission happened, and that the hospital owns the approval gate's design.

**A model judgment sentence (one fair answer among several):** "On Matthias's view the resident is not fully culpable, since no one could foresee or explain why the tool dropped the allergy, so part of the harm falls into a gap; on Tigard's view our practices can stretch: the resident answers for signing, the hospital for a screen that hid the allergy list, and the vendor for explaining the failure."

## If it does not work
If two duties have the same owner, ask whether that person can actually carry both.

## Debrief (3)
1. Which actor had the most control and the least visible role? (analyze)
2. Would blaming the resident alone be fair? Use Elish (2019). (evaluate)
3. Write one sentence for the hospital's policy that assigns a forward-looking duty. (create)

## Deliverable and rubric
The actor list and the four-gap table. Ungraded.

## Accessibility and alternatives
Table template in an accessible format; can be done orally in a group.
