# Lab 23.4 — Who Answers?

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Apply |
| Objectives | O4 (LO5) · tags 4D:DIL, UNESCO:ETH-A |
| Time | About 20 minutes including reading this sheet and the debrief; optional |
| Materials | A provided, constructed scenario (about 200 words, instructor package): a hospital's AI discharge-summary tool, bought from a vendor, omits a patient's allergy; a resident signs the summary at the end of a long shift; a pharmacy fills a prescription; the patient has a reaction and recovers. No real patient data. |

## Steps (no AI tool needed)
1. **List every actor (4 min).** Human and non-human: the vendor, the tool, the hospital that deployed it, the resident, the pharmacy, the patient, the record system. (From the Field: actor-network theory counts non-human actors in the map; listing them is not assigning moral responsibility.)
2. **Chain (3 min).** Place the case on Chapter 4's failure-to-harm chain: which link failed?
3. **Four gaps (6 min).** For culpability, moral accountability, public accountability and active responsibility, write one forward-looking duty and a named owner by role.
4. **Judge (3 min).** One sentence: is anyone culpable on Matthias's view? On Tigard's?

## Expected observations
Most groups find that the resident is the nearest person (a possible moral crumple zone) while the vendor alone can examine why the omission happened, and that the hospital owns the approval gate's design.

## If it does not work
If two duties have the same owner, ask whether that person can actually carry both.

## Debrief (3)
1. Which actor had the most control and the least visible role? (analyze)
2. Would blaming the resident alone be fair? Use Elish (2019). (evaluate)
3. Write one sentence for the hospital's policy that assigns a forward-looking duty. (create)

## Deliverable and rubric
The actor list and the four-gap table. Ungraded.

## Accessibility and alternatives
Table template in an accessible format; can be done orally in a group.
