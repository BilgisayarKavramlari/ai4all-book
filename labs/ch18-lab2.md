## The eight uses (constructed)
1. A student makes flashcards from her own textbook for personal study.
2. A comedy site publishes a parody of a famous poem that comments on the poem.
3. A start-up trains a model on a newspaper's articles to sell news summaries to the paper's readers.
4. A company trains a model on print books it bought and scanned; outputs are not shown to reproduce the books.
5. A company keeps a central library of pirated e-books, some never used for training.
6. A researcher computes word frequencies across a library's digitized collection and publishes only statistics.
7. A design agency trains an image model on one living illustrator's portfolio to sell images "in her style" to her clients.
8. A legal-tech firm trains a search tool on summaries written by a competitor whose license it was refused.

## Steps
For each use, mark each factor as leaning toward or against fair use, with one reason; then give your overall lean and compare with section 18.2's decisions.

## Expected observations (reasoning, not verdicts)
Uses 3, 7 and 8 share Ross's features (same purpose or market as the original; commercial); 4 resembles Bartz's training holding; 5 resembles Bartz's pirated library; 6 is a classic non-expressive analysis use (outputs are statistics); 1 and 2 are traditional study and commentary uses. Factor 4 (market) carries great weight. Several uses (7) are untested for generative AI as of October 2026.

## Debrief
1. Which factor changed your mind most often? (analyze)
2. Which use would you want a lawyer to see before going ahead, and why? (evaluate)
