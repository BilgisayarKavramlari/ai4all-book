| Materials | A free AI tool or one your institution provides or approves; your three tasks from the 1.3 Your Turn; the A1 template (Figure 1.14) |

## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. Bring the three tasks you listed in the 1.3 Your Turn (one you expect inside the frontier, one outside, one unsure).
2. Frame: write what a good result would look like for each, before using AI.
3. Run each task. Save what you asked and what came back.
4. Verify each output with a test: your notes, a source, a calculation, or a classmate who knows the topic.
5. Document at least one failure. If everything worked, make one task harder until something fails.
6. Optional (if time): share one surprise with your group.
7. Start your A1 draft.

## Expected observations
Inside-frontier tasks mostly pass with light checking; outside-frontier tasks produce confident errors. A "failed" test is a success for this lab: it shows where your judgment is needed.

## Debrief (3–5)
1. Which of your predictions about inside/outside was wrong?
2. Which test was most useful, and why?
3. What would you change in your Frame step next time? (decide/justify)

## Deliverable and rubric
Lab notes that become A1 parts 1–3. Scored as part of A1 (see the chapter's A1 rubric).

## Accessibility and alternatives
Students without AI access, or who prefer not to use it, use the provided dated outputs on the course site for three tasks and design the tests. Screen-reader-friendly template in Compass and on the course site.

## Safety and privacy
No personal, patient, client or institutional data. Use public or invented content.

## Instructor notes
Circulate during step 4; most students need help designing a test. Keep group share to 5 minutes or skip it.
