# Lab 21.1 — Code Peer Check (suggested within Lab 20.1)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Studio (in class), a suggested 15-minute activity that fits inside Chapter 20's portfolio workshop (the Ch. 21 peer check step of Lab 20.1) or runs on its own |
| Objectives | Ch. 21 O3, O5 (LO5, LO7) · tags 4D:DIL · feeds item 7 of the code's brief (trigger-test record and one revision) |
| Time | 15 minutes |
| Materials | Your workshop draft of the code (the section 21.2 and 21.3 Your Turns: three commitments and a boundaries line; the rest of the code is added after the workshop), on paper or on screen; the trigger-test strip (three questions, printed); a pen. No device or AI account is needed |

## Steps (tool-neutral)
Work in pairs inside your workshop team. Use your own draft only; no personal or confidential information about anyone else.

1. **Read aloud (2 min each, 4 min).** Partner A reads A's draft aloud; then B reads B's. Reading aloud shows quickly whether a moment is one you would notice.
2. **Test (3 min each, 6 min).** The listener marks each commitment against the three questions, with a tick or a cross: **Moment** (would you notice it then, from something you can see?), **Action** (could you or someone else trace afterward that you did it?), **Reason** (does the "because" point to your own evidence?). The listener then reads the boundaries line and asks: "Which rule is stricter here, and whom would you ask?"
3. **Name one line (1 min).** The listener names the one line that most needs work and says which question it failed.
4. **Revise and record (4 min).** Each writer rewrites that line and records it before and after, with the question it failed and the question it now passes. This is item 7 of the brief.

## Expected observations
Most first drafts have at least one line that fails "moment" ("when I use AI …"), "action" ("I am careful …") or "reason" (a rule with no evidence of why it matters to the writer). Boundaries lines often name the course and forget a job, a placement or a client; the question "which rule is stricter here?" usually brings these out. A line that passes all three questions on the first try is a valid result; record it unchanged and say so.

## Debrief (folded into Lab 20.1's share-and-critique block)
1. Which test question did your draft fail most often, and why? (analyze)
2. Did your boundaries line name a rule you had forgotten? (evaluate)

## Deliverable and rubric
None in class. The before-and-after line goes into item 7 of the code; if the instructor grades the portfolio, the code is scored with Chapter 21's suggested 15-point rubric, inside the portfolio rubric (Chapter 20).

## Accessibility and alternatives
Students who cannot attend may do the check with a classmate by message or with the instructor in office hours, or apply the test to their own draft with the printed strip and record one revision. Drafts may be shared on paper or read from a screen; the strip is available as an accessible document.
