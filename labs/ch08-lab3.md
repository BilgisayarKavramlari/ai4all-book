# Lab 8.3 — Signals versus Truth

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Break It |
| Objectives | O4 (LO3) · tags 4D:DISC, LM:how-works |
| Time | 35 minutes, home, optional |
| Materials | The keyed question pack for your field on your course site (business, health, engineering, humanities, social science, science; ten questions each); any AI tool; a spreadsheet or paper |

## Steps (tool-neutral)
1. Before asking, write your own predicted answer and confidence for each question (your calibration).
2. Ask the tool each question and ask how confident it is, as a percentage. Record both.
3. Ask three of the questions again in three temporary chats (memory off). Record whether the answers agree.
4. Score every answer against the key.
5. Group the tool's answers by stated confidence (50–69%, 70–89%, 90–100%) and compute each group's share correct. Do the same for yourself.
6. Write three sentences: which signal, stated confidence or disagreement across runs, went with the wrong answers? What is the calibration gap for the tool, and for you (average confidence of the answers rated 80% or higher minus the share of them correct)?

Ten questions give a rough picture, not a measurement. "The tool was well calibrated on my pack" is a valid result.

## Expected observations
In 2023 tests (Xiong et al., 2024), stated confidence clustered at 80–100%; record what your tool does. Disagreement across runs, when it happens, is a warning sign worth noting. Some packs produce no errors at all; that is a finding too.

## Debrief
1. Did the tool's stated confidence rise with its accuracy? (evaluate)
2. Was your own calibration gap larger or smaller than the tool's? (compare)

## Deliverable and rubric
Ungraded. The table and the three-sentence conclusion.

## Accessibility and alternatives
No-device route: dated sample transcripts with stated confidence on your course site. Screen-reader-friendly table template.
