## Course description

Artificial intelligence can now produce fluent writing, working code and confident answers in seconds. What it cannot do is decide what question to ask, judge whether an answer is true, protect the information it is given, or take responsibility for the result. This course teaches that part of the work. Students learn how generative and agentic AI systems behave and where they fail, then build a semester-long portfolio in which they frame tasks, design and iterate prompts, verify claims and sources, audit outputs for error and bias, address privacy and academic integrity, and document where their own judgment shaped the outcome. The emphasis is on practice and cases rather than tool demonstrations, and no programming background is assumed.

**Who it is for.** Every major. Students from business, the arts and humanities, social sciences, the natural sciences, education, engineering and computer science are equally at home. The final weeks ask each student to apply the method inside their own discipline.

## Learning outcomes (LO1–LO7)

By the end of the semester, students will be able to:

| Code | Outcome | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|
| LO1 | Explain, in correct terminology, what generative and agentic AI systems are, how they work in outline, and how and why they fail. | Exam terminology section; concept map |
| LO2 | Frame a task for an AI system and improve results through deliberate, documented iteration. | Prompt and iteration log; workflow blueprint |
| LO3 | Verify AI-generated claims, sources and outputs, and document remaining uncertainty. | Claim verification table; source review; case questions |
| LO4 | Distinguish responsible assistance from overreliance, undisclosed outsourcing and academic misconduct. | Tutor-mode reflection; AI Use Declaration; interpretive questions |
| LO5 | Apply privacy, security, fairness, transparency and human-oversight principles to AI-assisted work. | Risk review; decision matrix audit |
| LO6 | Design an AI-assisted workflow for a task in their own field, and defend its decisions to a non-specialist. | Domain workflow prototype; oral defense |
| LO7 | Present a portfolio of that work in terms an employer or a graduate program will recognize. | Career narrative; final portfolio |

## Weekly format

| | Tuesday: concept session | Thursday: practice lab |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | The week's concepts, terms and mechanisms | Apply them to a realistic case and produce the week's artifact |
| 75-minute flow | Opening case 10 · concept core 35 · one or two live demonstrations 20 · concept check 10 | Lab briefing 10 · group practice in teams of 3–4, 45 · share and critique 15 · exit step 5 |
| Output | Notes; ungraded concept check | Artifact draft, submitted on eLearning by Monday 11:59 p.m. |

Weeks 8, 14 and 15 break the pattern (midterm, portfolio workshop and oral defenses, final). Group work happens in class; every graded artifact is individual. In weeks 4, 9 and 10 the chapter's Case Study is read together in Tuesday's class.

## Use of AI in this course

Principle: AI use is not prohibited; unacknowledged, unverified or intellectually outsourced AI use is not acceptable.

Under UT Dallas policy UTDSP5017 (March 2025), which lets each instructor permit, partly permit or prohibit AI use in the syllabus, this course permits AI use as set out in the three zones below. [proposed — author to confirm]

| Permitted | Permitted with disclosure and verification | Not acceptable |
|---|---|---|
| Brainstorming, explanation of concepts, study help, feedback on your own draft, formatting, testing prompts | Drafting, coding support, source synthesis, case analysis, data and decision work — provided you disclose, verify and can explain the result | Undisclosed outsourcing, fabricated sources, unsupported claims, submitting work you do not understand, AI use during examinations, entering personal or confidential data (yours or anyone else's) into a public AI tool (course guidance) |

Every major artifact carries an AI Use Declaration: tools used, purpose, outputs accepted, rejected or revised, how they were verified, and where the student's own judgment shaped the work.

**Writing in coursework (textbook section 10.2).** [proposed — author to confirm] In coursework that builds your own writing or reasoning skill: feedback on your draft and grammar-and-spelling-only edits are permitted and listed briefly in the declaration; rewording or translating your own text needs the instructor's permission first; AI co-drafting is outsourcing unless the instructor explicitly allows it; AI as author (sent as is) is not acceptable. In other coursework: feedback and grammar-only edits are permitted and listed briefly; rewording, translating your own text and co-drafting are permitted with disclosure and verification; AI as author is permitted with disclosure and verification only if you can explain every sentence and its readers would accept it. A7 is the explicit permission for co-drafting. For the personal AI code of practice (Chapter 21), drafting help is permitted with disclosure and verification, and every commitment must be your own and defensible orally without a tool.

**Which data may enter which tool (textbook section 12.5; course guidance).** [proposed — author to confirm] Classes follow UTD's names in UTDBP3096: Public Data may go into consumer and institutional tools; your own non-sensitive work, course material and de-identified data may go into consumer tools if you accept the tool's training and retention terms, and into institutional tools; personal data about other people and Controlled Data never go into consumer tools, and into institutional tools only if the task needs it and the university's rules allow it (ask OIT if unsure); Confidential Data, including FERPA and HIPAA records, go into no tool in this course. The university's own pages decide any case the table does not cover. Lab and assignment items contain no identifiable person, including the student's own face or voice; voice clips only when recorded for the course with written consent.

**Privacy gate.** [proposed — author to confirm] A12 or A13 submitted with real personal, confidential, patient, student or client data is returned ungraded, to resubmit without it. In the final portfolio, an entry the instructor flags for such data is withheld from grading; the student has 48 hours from the notice to upload a redacted version, with no further penalty; an entry still not fixed when the 48 hours end scores 0 on its rows, and nothing else is lost.

Other policies: 2–4 guest practitioner sessions (Tuesdays, March 30 and April 13); voluntary, anonymous start- and end-of-term surveys under an approved IRB protocol with no effect on grades; university policies incorporated by reference (go.utdallas.edu/syllabus-policies). Compass, the book's digital companion, is optional, free and ungraded for UTD students; grades and submissions stay in eLearning.
