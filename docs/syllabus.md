## Course description

Artificial intelligence can now produce fluent writing, working code and confident answers in seconds. What it cannot do is decide what question to ask, judge whether an answer is true, protect the information it is given, or take responsibility for the result. This course teaches that part of the work. Students learn how generative and agentic AI systems behave and where they fail, then build a semester-long portfolio in which they frame tasks, design and iterate prompts, verify claims and sources, audit outputs for error and bias, address privacy and academic integrity, and document where their own judgment shaped the outcome. The emphasis is on practice and cases rather than tool demonstrations, and no programming background is assumed.

**Who it is for.** Every major. Students from business, the arts and humanities, social sciences, the natural sciences, education, engineering and computer science are equally at home. The final weeks ask each student to apply the method inside their own discipline.

## Learning outcomes (LO1–LO7)

By the end of the semester, students will be able to:

| Code | Outcome | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|
| LO1 | Explain, in correct terminology, what generative and agentic AI systems are, how they work in outline, and how and why they fail. | Exam terminology section; concept map |
| LO2 | Frame a task for an AI system and improve results through deliberate, documented iteration. | Prompt and iteration log; workflow blueprint |
| LO3 | Verify AI-generated claims, sources and outputs, and document remaining uncertainty. | Claim verification table; source review; case questions |
| LO4 | Distinguish responsible assistance from overreliance, undisclosed outsourcing and academic misconduct. | Tutor-mode reflection; AI Use Declaration; interpretive questions |
| LO5 | Apply privacy, security, fairness, transparency and human-oversight principles to AI-assisted work. | Risk review; decision matrix audit |
| LO6 | Design an AI-assisted workflow for a task in their own field, and defend its decisions to a non-specialist. | Domain workflow prototype; oral defense |
| LO7 | Present a portfolio of that work in terms an employer or a graduate program will recognize. | Career narrative; final portfolio |

## Use of AI in this course

Principle: AI use is not prohibited; unacknowledged, unverified or intellectually outsourced AI use is not acceptable.

| Permitted | Permitted with disclosure and verification | Not acceptable |
|---|---|---|
| Brainstorming, explanation of concepts, study help, feedback on your own draft, formatting, testing prompts | Drafting, coding support, source synthesis, case analysis, data and decision work — provided you disclose, verify and can explain the result | Undisclosed outsourcing, fabricated sources, unsupported claims, submitting work you do not understand, AI use during examinations |

Every major artifact carries an AI Use Declaration: tools used, purpose, outputs accepted, rejected or revised, how they were verified, and where the student's own judgment shaped the work.

Other policies: 2–4 guest practitioner sessions (Tuesdays, March 30 and April 13); voluntary, anonymous start- and end-of-term surveys under an approved IRB protocol with no effect on grades; university policies incorporated by reference (go.utdallas.edu/syllabus-policies). Compass, the book's digital companion, is optional, free and ungraded for UTD students; grades and submissions stay in eLearning.
