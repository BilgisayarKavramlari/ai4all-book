## 5. Concept sequence

**Session question:** *Can a message still be yours if an AI tool helped write it?* Answer (hedged): yes, when you decided what it says, you can explain and stand behind every sentence, the help is the kind its readers would accept, and you disclose where your course, employer or profession expects it. Either way, a disclosure line does not change who answers for the message.

**Content note** (one line, in the book and before Tuesday's slot): the case concerns a message sent after a campus shooting in which students died; no victim is named.

**Opening Case (≤ 550 words): Peabody's message after the Michigan State shooting (C07).** Attribution tiers: **confirmed** = the email and the dean's email as reproduced or quoted in the reporting (the email's ChatGPT line, quoted by CNN; the dean's statement, quoted by the *Hustler*); **reported** = the *Hustler* and CNN; **inferred** = readers' expectations, the origin of the "shootings" error, how much the office changed the output.
- Feb 13, 2023: three students killed, five wounded at Michigan State University (reported: NPR). No names.
- Feb 16, 2023: Peabody College's Office of Equity, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI) emailed the Peabody community about the shooting (reported: *Hustler*, Feb 17, Apr 12); the message spoke of community and inclusion and referred to "recent Michigan shootings", though there had been one (reported: *Hustler*; CNN). Its last line said it was a paraphrase of ChatGPT output, in a form like a personal-communication citation (confirmed, as quoted by CNN; the one CNN quotation).
- Feb 17: the office's associate dean apologized and called the use of ChatGPT "poor judgment" (reported: *Hustler*, Feb 17; one quotation). Students criticized a machine-written message about community (reported; paraphrased; no names). A university spokesperson said Vanderbilt does not use AI systems to generate its messages (reported).
- Feb 18: Dean Camilla Benbow wrote that the message had not followed Peabody's normal processes "providing for multiple layers of review" (confirmed, as quoted by the *Hustler*, Feb 19; one quotation), that administrators including herself had not known of it, and that her office would review what happened; the office's associate and assistant deans stepped back temporarily (reported). They returned on Apr 11, 2023 (reported: *Hustler*, Apr 12).
- Puzzle: *The message said ChatGPT had helped. It was fluent and on topic. Why did readers, and the office's own dean, treat it as a failure?* Answered in 10.1, 10.2, 10.5, 10.7.
- Naming (V3): institution, office, roles and the dean only. Never "Vanderbilt apologized"; the apologies came from the office and the dean.

**Prediction before evidence (10.6):** "Will AI detectors flag essays by non-native English writers more often, less often or as often as essays by U.S. eighth graders?" (Liang et al., 2023.)
