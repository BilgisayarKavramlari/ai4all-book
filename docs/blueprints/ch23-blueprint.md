## 5. Concept sequence

**Session question:** *When informed people disagree about whether AI understands, feels, creates or endangers us, how do you take a position you can defend, and what does any answer change about who answers for AI's work?* Hedged answer (Summary), as method only: state each side at its strongest from its own texts; find the premise in dispute; keep evidence about behavior apart from claims about minds; in coursework and at work the practice rule is that you answer for your own use (Ch. 10), while whether responsibility can always be fairly assigned remains an open question (23.4); a labeled, dated, revisable view is the goal.

**Case, one job per appearance.** Opening = facts and puzzle: Turing proposed replacing "Can machines think?" with an imitation game; in two preregistered three-party tests (*PNAS*, May 19, 2026), participants judged GPT-4.5 with a humanlike persona prompt to be the human 73% of the time, significantly more often than the real human; LLaMa-3.1 with the same prompt 56%; without the persona prompt 38% and 36%; ELIZA 23%, GPT-4o 21%; a third study with 15-minute games: 56% and 59%; interrogators' reasons focused on style and social-emotional cues (Jones & Bergen, 2026). "Pass" is the authors' word, attributed. Puzzle: *the authors say these systems pass Turing's test; has anything about understanding been settled?* 23.2 = each text's argument, one paragraph each. Case Study = the texts as primary sources and labels. Lab 23.3 = the game on paper. **Tiers:** confirmed = the published texts; reported = news coverage (only "widely reported"); inferred = the book's reading. Not used: contested accounts of an author's 2020 departure from Google (X9).

## 9. Optional artifact: position paper

**Brief (~450 words in the chapter).** One debate from 23.2–23.7 or the Ethics Lens. Submit: (1) the question in one sentence; (2) an argument map (≤ 9 boxes; claim, ≥ 2 reasons, ≥ 2 objections; unstated premises dashed, labeled "inferred"); (3) 600–900 words with each side at its strongest, each from at least one primary source you opened, attributed exactly, one short quotation per source at most; (4) the hardest objection and your reply; (5) **your view in a separately labeled paragraph** ("undecided, because…" accepted) and what would change it; (6) a five-element AI Use Declaration (tools, purpose, accepted/rejected/revised, how verified, your judgment). Side is carried by position, label, glyph and line end, never by color. **Dashed outline and link = inferred only** (an unstated premise or the book's reading; legend says so). `highlight` = "claim", "pro:N", "con:N" or a list: figlib's highlight role plus a "▶ this section" tag (survives gray). `note` = footer (default "A map shows the structure of an argument, not who is right").
