**Chapter 8 definitions frozen here** (from the Chapter 8 blueprint v0.2, approved; Chapter 8's text is not yet written, so the drafter copies these exact words and re-checks them against chapters/08-*.qmd when it exists):
- **Claim:** one statement in an output that you might rely on; marked *checkable* (it can be true or false) or *not checkable as true or false* (a judgment, a recommendation, a prediction).
- **Independent check:** evidence that no AI tool produced: a source with an author or publisher you can name, or your own recalculation or reasoning from known inputs.
- **Primary source:** the original record a claim comes from (the study, dataset, law, court record, official page).
- **Fabricated citation:** a citation to a work that does not exist (failure 1 only).
- **Verdict scale (book-wide):** supported · partly supported · unsupported · contradicted · cannot verify.
- **Verdict rule for citations:** an authoritative record shows the work does not exist or its details belong to another work → contradicted; not found in two indexes no AI tool produced → unsupported (the uncertainty line names the indexes); found, but the passage does not support the claim → unsupported (contradicted if it says the opposite); found and the passage supports it → supported.
- **Signature 8:** `verification_funnel(highlight=None, verdict=None, tag=None, note=None, notes=None)` in figures/scripts/ch08_figures.py (not yet in the repository): output → Claims → 1 Independent check → 2 Primary source → 3 Expert, a stop rail, Verdict, dashed Remaining uncertainty (7 boxes).
- An AI tool's own "yes" is never a source; an AI search tool may help find a source, but the verdict rests on the source you opened (Ch. 8 Decision 15). Chapter 8's L8.2 record (five references) may feed A6.

## 5. Concept sequence

**Session question, answered in the Summary:** *Can you trust what an AI tool tells you a source says?* Answer (hedged): not without opening the source. AI tools can find and condense sources quickly, but in tests from 2023 to 2025 their citations often did not support their sentences and their summaries often stated broader conclusions than the originals (each finding dated and model-specific). How directly a source records the claim you need is a separate judgment that you make by reading laterally and tracing claims to their origin. Both judgments stay with you, and your reference list should show what you actually read.
