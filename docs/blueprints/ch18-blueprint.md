## 4. Concept sequence (≤ 5 must-teach facts per section)

**Session question:** *Who sets the rules for AI — on training data, on outputs, on mistakes and on risky uses — and how do you find the ones that apply to you?* Answered in the Summary (hedged, dated).

**Opening Case (≤ 320): C15.** Ross, a start-up, asked to license Westlaw content and was refused (district op. p. 2); it had training memos written using Westlaw headnotes and trained a search tool that returned passages from judicial opinions; Thomson Reuters sued (2020); Ross shut down (reported, 2020–21); the district court (Feb 11, 2025) and the Third Circuit (Sept 29, 2026) held that copying 2,243 headnotes to train the tool was not fair use. Puzzle: in 2025 two other federal judges held that training models on books was fair use — how can both be right, and which rule applies to the tools you use?

**C15 tiers.** Confirmed (opinions): license refused; ~25,000 memos; 2,243 headnotes infringed (of 2,830 compared); not generative; district factors 1 TR, 2 Ross, 3 Ross, 4 TR; appeals court factors 1 against fair use ("minimally transformative, at best" — the one quotation), 2 slightly for, **3 against (reversed)**, 4 against; footnote 7 distinguishes generative models (a narrowing, **not a holding**). Reported: shutdown (Dec 2020 announcement, Jan 31, 2021 offline); plans to seek rehearing or Supreme Court review. Inferred: what it means for generative AI; it binds courts in the Third Circuit only.
