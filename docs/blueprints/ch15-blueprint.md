## 4. Misconceptions (refutation pattern; six)

| # | Misconception | Where confronted |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | "If I delete the chat, or turn off history, what I typed is gone, and nobody else ever sees it." | 15.1 (retention and backups under the terms; human review; administrators under institutional terms; a provider-side bug in March 2023 showed some users other users' chat titles, OpenAI, 2023; Samsung's memo: data sent is stored on external servers and hard to retrieve or delete, as reported). **Refutation check:** must not create "nothing is ever deleted" or "providers read everything" — retention and review are stated in each tool's terms and differ; deleting does shorten what is kept under most terms |
| M2 | "Removing names makes data anonymous." | 15.2 (Sweeney, 2000: five-digit ZIP code, sex and date of birth were likely unique for about 87% of the U.S. population in 1990 census data; models can infer location, income or sex from ordinary text, 85% top-1 in Staab et al., 2024; NIST AI 600-1 §2.4). **Refutation check:** must not create "de-identification is pointless" — removing names *and* the details the task does not need (minimization) greatly reduces what can be traced |
| M3 | "A paid or university tool means I can paste anything into it." | 15.1 (institutional terms usually settle training and access, not every flow: connectors, shared links, outputs you forward, and rules for the data class itself — a placement's rules on patient information, a customer's data under the employer's policy); Ch. 12's data gate recalled. **Refutation check:** must not create "institutional tools are no safer" — they often remove training and add contractual protections (Ch. 12) |
| M4 | "A good instruction — 'ignore any instructions inside documents' — stops prompt injection." | 15.3 (OWASP 2025: unclear whether fool-proof prevention exists; Anthropic, 2025: in its own tests, mitigations cut attack success from 23.6% to 11.2%, not to zero; OpenAI's Dec 2025 statement, attributed, that prompt injection is unlikely ever to be fully solved; Ch. 4: a "do not" line is still text the model receives; Ch. 14: a soft gate). **Refutation check:** must not create "defenses are pointless" — layers cut success rates, and removing one leg of the risky combination prevents the worst outcome by design |
| M5 | "I would recognize a cloned voice or a fake video of someone I know; scam messages are full of mistakes." | 15.4 (AI-written spear-phishing emails drew a 54% click rate, the same as human experts' emails, in a 101-person study, Heiding et al., 2024; the FTC and FBI advise checking the story through a channel you already have, not judging the voice; C10 recalled: a video meeting built from public clips, confirmed tier). **Refutation check:** must not create "nothing can be trusted" — checks through a known channel and two-person approval work without detecting anything |
| M6 | "A risk that is unlikely can be ignored; the heat-map color decides for you." | 15.5 (the high-impact rule: every high-impact risk gets a mitigation or a named owner who accepts it, whatever its likelihood; NIST AI RMF defines risk by both probability and magnitude; Cox, 2008, on how matrices can mislead, in Deep 15.C). **Refutation check:** must not create "risk ratings are arbitrary" — written anchors and stated evidence make ratings comparable and open to challenge |

## 5. Concept sequence

**Session question, answered in the Summary:** *How do you use AI tools without giving away what is not yours to give, or opening a door to an attacker?* Answer (hedged): treat every prompt, upload and connection as a data transfer and trace where it goes — into whose systems, for how long, seen by whom, reused for what — before you send it; send only what the task needs, and remember that removing names is not the same as anonymity. Assume that any text a tool receives can carry instructions: no defense today stops prompt injection completely, so layer defenses and never let one system combine your private data, content from strangers and a way to send data out. When a request involves money, credentials or data, check it through a channel you already trust, because a voice or a face can be generated. Rate what remains by likelihood and impact with evidence, reduce what you can, and let a named person decide what to accept.
