# Recurring characters (story bible)

Status: APPROVED by delegation (decision D11, 2026-10-18). The author asked the editor to choose the characters; the author may revise any detail. ai4all-chapter-writer must follow this file exactly and log any new fixed detail here before using it.

## The premise

Five students meet in week 1 of a fictional section of an "AI for All" course and are assigned to the same lab table for the Thursday labs. They are **fictional composites**: no character is based on a real student, and their university is never named. The front-matter section "Meet the Students" (front/meet-the-students.qmd) tells readers this.

Why they exist: recurring people make examples personal and memorable (Mayer's personalization principle), let the book show the same method across five fields, and give each chapter a human thread. They do not replace real, cited cases; they are clearly labeled teaching examples.

## Rules for using them

- Each chapter features one or two of them, not all five. Rotate so every character appears in roughly the same number of chapters.
- They are never presented as real people, never quoted as sources, and never used to make factual claims about the world. Anything factual they encounter is cited like any other claim.
- Their AI outputs in examples are labeled "constructed example" or carry a real model name and date.
- Their growth is gradual and believable; each makes mistakes and fixes them. No one is the "smart one" or the "bad example".
- Avoid stereotypes: majors, skills and attitudes do not follow ethnicity, gender or nationality.
- Pronouns as listed; never change them.
- Other people in examples (a manager, a teacher, a patient) are unnamed one-off figures, unless added here first.
