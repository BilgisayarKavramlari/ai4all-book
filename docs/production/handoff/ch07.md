## Glossary rows

New Key Terms (Chapter 7 is the defining chapter). Sense labels follow the brief: "calibration (of your own confidence)" is defined here; a model's calibration is Chapter 8's row.

| Term | Definition | Türkçe |
|---|---|---|
| Performance (during practice) | How well you do a task while practicing, often with help; it can differ from what you have learned. | Performans (alıştırma sırasında) |
| Learning (durable) | A lasting change in what you can do without help, shown later and in new situations. | Kalıcı öğrenme |
| Overreliance | Relying on an AI tool's output more than the task or the evidence warrants, so that your own checking or skill-building drops out. | Aşırı bağımlılık |
| Retrieval practice (learning) | Recalling information from your own memory, for example by self-testing, which strengthens it more than restudying. Not the same as retrieval in an AI system (Ch. 3). | Geri çağırma alıştırması |
| Desirable difficulty | A condition that makes learning feel harder and slower but improves long-term memory and use, provided you can meet it with effort. | İstenen zorluk |
| Generation effect (learning) | The finding that information you produce yourself is remembered better than information you only read. | Üretme etkisi |
| Spacing (spaced practice) | Spreading study of the same material over separate sessions instead of massing it in one. | Aralıklı çalışma |
| Metacognition | Knowing about and monitoring your own thinking and learning. | Üstbiliş |
| Calibration (of your own confidence) | How closely your confidence in your answers matches how often they are actually correct. | Kalibrasyon (kendi güveninin) |
| Scaffold | Temporary support that lets you do what you cannot yet do alone, withdrawn as you improve. | İskele (destek) |
| Crutch (in learning with AI) | Help that does the part of the work you needed to do yourself, and that you keep depending on. | Koltuk değneği |
| Tutor mode | A way of using an AI tool in which it is set up to respond with questions and hints and to wait for your attempts, instead of giving answers. | Öğretmen kipi |
| Answer mode | The default way of using an AI tool, in which it produces the answer or finished work you ask for. | Cevap kipi |
| Socratic questioning | Teaching by asking questions that lead the learner to reason the answer out. | Sokratik sorgulama |
| Cognitive offloading (Deep) | Using a tool or action to reduce the mental work a task requires, such as writing a note or using a calculator. | Bilişsel yük aktarımı |

## Chapter 7

### Review Questions A
1. *Performance:* how well you do a task while practicing, often with help. *Learning:* a lasting change in what you can do without help, shown later and in new situations.
3. +10 percentage points (from 20 to 30); a relative increase of 50% (10 ÷ 20).
5. **(b)** After a week, the group that read once and recalled three times remembered the most (61%, vs. 40% for rereading). (a) is M2: rereading won only at 5 minutes; (c) is false; (d) confuses exposure with recall.
7. **(b)** A difficulty is desirable only if you can meet it with effort (Bjork & Bjork, 2011). (a) overgeneralizes; (c) is M2; (d) is backward: desirable difficulties often lower performance during practice.
9. **(b)** Students learned more in active classes but rated their learning lower; the authors suggest they took the extra effort as a sign of poor learning (Deslauriers et al., 2019).
11. Ask of each turn: who recalled and produced the answer in this turn, you or the tool? If you did (or got a hint after your attempt), code S; if the tool did the part you needed to do, code C.
13. GPT Tutor's instructions also contained the teachers' worked solutions and an instruction to give hints instead of the answer. The label "tutor" alone did not keep GPT Base students working; withholding answers kept the recalling and producing with the student, and the supplied solutions made its feedback accurate.

### Review Questions B (model answers)
1. The claim shows performance with the helper: homework done while the tool was available. It does not show learning, which needs a later check without the tool. Ask for: closed-book test scores taken later, a comparison group that did the same homework without the tool, random assignment, and who measured the scores.
3. (1) **S** — the student attempted (friction direction) and asks for a check; (2) **S** — the tool asks a question that makes the student reason; (3) **S** — the student produces the fix; (4) **C** — asks for a solution with no attempt; (5) **C** — the tool produces the whole solution the student needed to produce.
5. Right after: calibrated (predicted 3, scored 3), but an immediate score measures short-term performance. Two days later: overconfident (predicted 3, scored 1); she forgot what she had not retrieved since. Change: add spaced retrieval (self-test after one and three days), predict more cautiously, and check again before the exam.

### Review Questions C (scoring guidance)
1. Strong answers take a position and use evidence: GPT Tutor avoided the harm but did not raise exam scores (Bastani et al., 2025); structured tutors helped in short studies (Kestin et al., 2025; De Simone et al., 2025). They name at least one limit of tutor mode (answer mode is one request away; the tool can be wrong or agree with wrong attempts; good scaffolds need expert material). Strong "disagree" answers note that some tasks are not about building a skill (Deep 7.B, Ch. 5's third question).
3. Strong answers use what each study measured and cannot tell: short studies of single subjects; designs that depended on teachers' or experts' solutions (labor that is part of teaching); a working paper not yet peer-reviewed. A strong position separates "AI can do parts of tutoring well in some conditions" from "replace teachers." Weak answers cite one study as proof.

### Spaced Review
1. Example: "After the session, I can answer all three keyed questions closed-book, scored against the textbook key." It is checkable because the key decides each answer.
3. The model produces each next token from scores that reflect patterns in its training text; nothing in that process checks whether the result is true, so fluent text can be false (Chapter 3, section 3.2).

### Case Study Questions
1. The control group practiced the same problems with their course books and notes, without devices; the exam was the third part of the same 90-minute session, closed-book and closed-laptop. The first detail means the comparison is with normal, active practice, not with no practice; the second means the study measured short-term learning only.
3. Accept reasoned rankings. A common strong answer: the Türkiye study is the strongest design (randomized classrooms, preregistered outcome, about 1,000 students, peer-reviewed), but none measures long-term learning; Harvard is randomized and peer-reviewed but covers two lessons with immediate tests; Nigeria runs longest (six weeks) but is a working paper and bundles the tool with a guided program.
5. GPT Base's wrong answers are a property of the output: on the tree, a hallucination of the wrong-calculation kind, documented by the researchers' tests. The harm to learning came from what students did with the outputs (copying instead of attempting): a human use failure, labeled on the failure-to-harm chain's reliance link, documented by the message analysis and the exam result.
