| Term | Definition | Türkçe |
|---|---|---|
| Decision support | AI output, such as a summary, an analysis, a score or a recommendation, that goes to a named person who decides and answers for the result. | Karar desteği |
| Decision owner | The named person or body who makes a decision and answers for its result. | Karar sahibi |
| Automated decision | A decision in which an AI output triggers an action with no person in between. | Otomatik karar |
| Hidden assumption | A choice built into data or an analysis that can change the result but is not stated. | Örtük varsayım |
| Missing value | An empty cell or field where a value should be. | Eksik değer |
| Listwise deletion | Deleting every row that has a missing value, so the analysis uses complete rows only. | Liste boyunca silme |
| Imputation | Filling a missing value with an estimate, such as the average of the other values. | Eksik veri tamamlama (töhmet) |
| Proxy label | A recorded outcome used as the label because it is easy to measure, standing in for the outcome you actually care about. | Vekil etiket |
| Simpson's paradox | A pattern in a total that weakens or even reverses inside the groups that make it up. | Simpson paradoksu |
| Decision matrix | A table that lists options against the criteria they are judged on, each criterion with a scale and a weight. | Karar matrisi |
| Criterion (in a decision matrix) | One of the things the options are judged on, with its own scale. | Ölçüt |
| Weight (in a decision matrix) | How much a criterion counts toward the total. | Ağırlık |
| Sensitivity check | Changing one choice in an analysis, such as a weight or the handling of a blank, to see whether the result changes. | Duyarlılık kontrolü |
| Automation bias | The tendency to over-rely on automated output, following it when it is wrong or missing what it did not flag. | Otomasyon yanlılığı |
| Commission error | Following an automated output when it is wrong. | Yapma hatası |
| Omission error | Missing a problem because an automated system did not flag it. | İhmal hatası |
| Missing completely at random, MCAR (Deep) | Values are missing for a reason that has nothing to do with any data. | Tamamen rastgele eksiklik |
| Missing at random, MAR (Deep) | Values are missing for a reason that depends only on things you did observe. | Rastgele eksiklik |
| Missing not at random, MNAR (Deep) | Values are missing for a reason that depends on the missing value itself. | Rastgele olmayan eksiklik |
| Multiple imputation (Deep) | Filling each missing value several times with plausible values, analyzing each completed dataset and pooling the results with their extra uncertainty. | Çoklu tamamlama (çoklu töhmet) |
| Sensitivity analysis (Deep, missing data) | Assuming the missing values were somewhat higher or lower than the fill and checking whether the conclusion survives. | Duyarlılık analizi |

### Faded examples and Your Turn 11.4 (self-check)
- **11.1 A Maintenance Score:** Below the threshold the list **recommends**: it proposes an order that the supervisor accepts or changes. The supervisor owns those decisions. Above it, the plant's managers who switched on the automatic work orders own the rule and answer for its results.

Report it with that caveat, or by wait group.
- **11.3 Tickets Closed per Hour:** **Measure:** the number of tickets an agent closes per hour, whatever their difficulty or outcome. **Gap:** agents who take the long, hard tickets close fewer per hour while solving the hardest problems, so they score low for a reason unrelated to helpfulness. (Agents who close tickets without solving them may also score high.)
- **11.4 A Campus Survey:** **Check 4:** this year's survey ran in the first week of a new hall, when novelty may raise ratings; the two periods are not comparable. **Check 5:** this year's responses come mostly from one hall, so the "rise" may reflect who answered rather than a change for everyone; compare the same halls across years. **Verdict for "satisfaction rose":** unsupported as stated.

History 2 ÷ 10 × 10 = 2.0 × 10% = 0.20. **Total 6.70.** Exhibition history stands in for quality or reliability; the gap is bigger for new or self-taught artists who have had fewer chances to show. ### Review Questions A
1. **(b)** A fraud score that blocks a payment automatically, with no one reviewing it. (a), (c) and (d) inform or recommend; a person decides.
3. **(d)** Those shifts' proceeds count but their hours do not, so the rate rises. (a) is M2: skipping a blank in the hours is not neutral; (b) the formula does not fill anything; (c) the error goes the other way.
5. **(a)** Agreement is not evidence they are right; find out why the values are missing and get them if you can. (b) is M2; (c) picks a method by its result; (d) throws away the column without asking why it is blank.
7. **(c)** Its label, next year's cost, was lower for Black patients with the same needs, because less was spent on their care. (a) race and close stand-ins in the inputs were not the mechanism here (M3; that is Chapter 2's proxy in the inputs); (b) the algorithm predicted cost well (M4); (d) doctors did not change the scores.
9. **(b)** A pattern in a total can weaken or reverse within the groups that make it up. (a) is about sample size, (c) about tool defaults (check 3), (d) about the time window (check 4).
11. **(d)** To see whether the top option and shortlist change when a defensible choice changes. (a) a check tests, it does not confirm; (b) removing proxies is a criteria-audit decision; (c) equal weights are a choice too (M5).
13. **(a)** An omission error: a problem missed because the system did not flag it. (b) a commission error would be following a wrong flag; (c) and (d) name data problems, not the reviewer's error.

### Review Questions B
1. (i) The summary informs: the committee member reading it owns the decision. (ii) The score recommends: the committee owns which applications to fund; a check is needed on the scoring criteria (proxies) and on the cutoff at 50. (iii) Automatic rejection triggers an action with no person in between: an automated decision; the owner is whoever switched it on (the office), and the check is most needed here, before use, on what counts as "incomplete", with a route for appeals and regular audits. Full credit: three roles, owners, and the check placed at (iii).
3. What do we mean? Roads that most need repair. What do we measure? Complaints filed through an app. For whom is the gap bigger? Areas where fewer residents use the app or file complaints (for example, because of language, phone access or time), whose roads may be just as bad. Better measure or check: a road-condition survey or sensor data, or comparing complaint counts with inspections in a sample of areas. Full credit: three answers plus one measure or check.
5. Any defensible test earns credit if it is reported with the new top, the margin and the shortlist: for example, move 10 points from image quality to price, or change the price scale's ends (a scale choice changes the effective weight). With a 0.1-point gap, a small change will likely reorder the top two. Advice: treat the top two as tied, check that price was reversed (lower is better), and decide on stated reasons such as a trial with both microscopes; record them.

### Review Questions C
1. Strong answers take a position and use the evidence: cost was a reasonable choice for a program partly meant to reduce costs, and it reflected the industry's standard approach (the article); but the program's purpose was to help patients with the greatest needs, and cost stood in badly for need for Black patients ($1,801 less spending at the same health, through barriers to care and direct effects of race, in the authors' account); a health-based label nearly doubled their share among the top 3%. Many conclude that the choice was understandable but not acceptable without group-by-group checks against health. Weak answers call the algorithm "racist" or ignore the business reason.
3. Strong answers say a sign-off alone is not enough: automation bias (Goddard et al., 2012; Dratsch et al., 2023), and in the Opening Case doctors saw only patients the score referred and corrected only a small part of the gap in the authors' simulations. They design a review step with its four elements: who reviews; what they see first (the record before the score); what counts as a reason to disagree; where overrides are recorded and reviewed, plus a sample check of unflagged patients and group-by-group audits. Weak answers say "any human check is enough" or "humans are useless."

### Spaced Review
1. One line saying which AI tool was used and for what, and how the numbers were checked, for example: "A chat tool ran the first analysis; I checked every number against the sign-in sheets." Disclosure tells the board how the analysis was made (Chapter 10); it does not make the analysis right: only checking does.
3. In Chapter 8's scale, "partly supported" means part of the claim holds and part does not; you have not yet established any part. "Cannot verify" means the evidence exists or may exist but you cannot reach it: here, which 240 of the 252 orders were used. Once you know, the verdict can change to supported, partly supported or contradicted.
