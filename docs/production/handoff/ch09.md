|---|---|---|
| Secondary source | A source that reports on or interprets primary sources, such as a review article, a textbook or a news story about a study. | İkincil kaynak |
| Tertiary source | A source that collects and condenses secondary ones, such as an encyclopedia, an index or a study guide. | Üçüncül kaynak |
| Gray literature | Material published outside journals and books, such as reports by governments, companies, charities and consulting firms. | Gri literatür |
| Peer review | The checking of a research manuscript by a few experts in the field, at a journal editor's request, before publication. | Hakem değerlendirmesi |
| Preprint | A research manuscript posted publicly before peer review. | Ön baskı (hakem öncesi makale) |
| Correction (of an article) | A notice that fixes part of a published article. | Düzeltme |
| Retraction | The withdrawal of a published article because of serious error or misconduct. | Geri çekme |
| Vertical reading | Judging a page by its own look and its own claims. | Dikey okuma |
| Lateral reading | Leaving a page early to see what outside sources say about who is behind it. | Yanal okuma |
| Outside source | A source not written or paid for by whoever is behind the page you are checking. | Dış kaynak |
| Source ladder | The book's four-rung picture of how directly a source records a claim: it states the claim with no traceable origin; it reports the claim and names a source you have not checked; it cites the record specifically; it is the record itself. | Kaynak merdiveni |

| AI search | A tool that retrieves pages and then produces an answer with links (the search-then-generate lane). | Yapay zekâ destekli arama |
| Research-report tool | A system that runs many searches, retrieves many pages and produces a long report with citations; often marketed as "deep research." | Araştırma raporu aracı |
| Systematic review (Deep) | A review that searches for every study on a question using a written plan, judges each one's quality and summarizes them. | Sistematik derleme |
| Meta-analysis (Deep) | A study that combines the results of many studies statistically into one estimate. | Meta-analiz |
| GRADE (Deep) | A system that rates the certainty of a body of health evidence, starting from study design and moving the rating down or up. | GRADE (kanıt kesinliği derecelendirmesi) |

### Review Questions B
1. A manufacturer's white paper is gray literature, not peer-reviewed, written by a party with a stake in the answer. It is a primary record of what the manufacturer claims or specifies (for example, its stated lifetime under its own test conditions); it is not a good record of how the battery performs in independent tests or real use, which needs an independent test report or study. Full credit: type, status, who paid, one claim it records and one it does not.
3. Hop 1, blog → exhibition catalogue: does the catalogue exist, do the details match, does it say $2 million in 1910? Hop 2, catalogue → auction record: does the record exist (the auction house's archive or a published sale record), and what does it say: price, currency, date, which painting? Drift is most likely as a changed number (a price converted to today's dollars or another currency, or a wrong year) or a misattributed painting. Verdict follows Chapter 8's rule; if the auction record is not found, the claim is unsupported.
5. Types: three company blogs (gray literature, low rungs, paid for by interested parties), two news stories (rung 2), one survey by the tool's own provider (gray literature with a conflict of interest). Support: check that each cited page says what its sentence claims. Missing evidence: peer-reviewed studies, primary datasets, counter-evidence, dates. Faithfulness: compare the report's conclusions with what the sources say. Strong answers conclude that the report is a starting list, not evidence.
