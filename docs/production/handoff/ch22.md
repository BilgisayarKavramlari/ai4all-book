| Term | Definition | Türkçe |
|---|---|---|
| Capability claim | A statement that a system can now do something. | Yetenek iddiası |
| As-of date | The date on which a claim was made or measured, not the date on which you read it. | Geçerlilik tarihi |
| Evidence status | What a claim's checking amounts to so far, which says who checked it: measured, reported or forecast, with the book's own inference kept separate; it does not say whether the claim is true. | Kanıt durumu |
| Measured (claim) | Measured or checked by an outside source, with a method you can read. | Ölçülmüş (iddia) |
| Reported (claim) | Stated by the developer, or by a news outlet citing the developer. | Bildirilmiş (iddia) |
| Forecast | A statement about the future, whoever makes it. | Öngörü |
| Outside evaluation | A test of a system run by an outside source. | Dış değerlendirme |
| Reasoning model | The providers' name for a model trained to produce many intermediate tokens, often hidden, before its final answer; a provider term, not a claim that the model reasons as a person does. | Akıl yürütme modeli |
| Test-time compute | Computation spent at the moment of answering a question, rather than only during training. | Yanıt anı hesaplaması (test zamanı hesaplama) |
| Time horizon (of an agent) | The length of task, measured in how long skilled people take, that an agent is predicted to complete half the time (the 50% time horizon); the 80% time horizon is the length it completes four times out of five. | Zaman ufku (ajanın) |
| Computer-use agent | An agent whose observations are screenshots of a computer screen and whose actions are clicks, keystrokes and typed text. | Bilgisayar kullanan ajan |
| Tool protocol | A published format that lets a model's requests reach many tools in the same way. | Araç protokolü |
| Training compute | The total amount of computation used to train a model, counted in FLOP. | Eğitim hesaplaması |
| FLOP | Floating-point operations: the individual arithmetic steps a computer performs, used to count training compute. | FLOP (kayan noktalı işlem) |
| Scaling (of AI models) | The finding that language models predicted text better, in a smooth and predictable way, as their size, their training data and the computing used to train them grew. | Ölçekleme |
| System boundary (of an energy estimate) | Which parts of a system an energy estimate counts. | Sistem sınırı (enerji tahmininin) |
| Aging check | A check of a dated claim against the best current source: what that source says today, a verdict on the old claim as a description of today, and what changed (the facts, the status or only the names). | Eskime kontrolü |

## Chapter 22

### Check Yourself
**22.1** (1) The as-of date, the evidence status (measured, reported, forecast; the book's inference kept separate) and who could check it (§22.1). (2) It is the original record of what the developer claims, and no AI tool produced it, but the developer is behind it, so it is not an outside source (§22.1).
**22.2** (1) Computation spent at the moment of answering, such as producing many tokens before the final answer; every token is computed and usually billed, so it takes time and money (§22.2). (2) The step text is generated like any other text; it can include unsupported steps, and in one developer's test a newer reasoning model invented facts more often than an older one (§22.2; Chapter 17).
**22.3** (1) On tasks that take skilled people four hours, the agent is predicted to succeed about half the time; it does not mean it succeeds reliably (the 80% horizon is shorter) or on other kinds of tasks (§22.3). (2) Points are measurements; a fitted line is a trend, and the part beyond the last point is an extrapolation, a forecast; this book draws trend lines dotted and labels them "trend, not data" (§22.3).
**22.4** (1) It describes how the video looks, as stated by its maker; a realistic video is not evidence that an event happened (§22.4; Chapter 13). (2) When looks and sound are less useful as clues, checking source, channel and earliest version is what still works (§22.4).
**22.5** (1) A crowd-vote leaderboard gap (people pick the better of two anonymous answers) and a time gap on Epoch's index (months until an open-weight model reaches a closed model's level) (§22.5). (2) Do: run the model on your own hardware or adapt it. Not shown: the training data (§22.5; Chapter 12).
**22.6** (1) Measured: the growth of training compute and of data centers' electricity use; forecast: whether more scale will keep improving models, and future compute, data and power (§22.6). (2) They can count different parts of the system (their boundary), such as only active chips or also idle capacity and cooling (§22.6).
**22.7** (1) Replication, critique and peer review need other specialists, data and time; an announcement needs none of these (§22.7). (2) That the formal proof is valid step by step; specialists still judge whether the formal statement says what the original problem meant (§22.7).
**22.8** (1) The facts, the status, or only the names (§22.8). (2) Because claims keep aging; a set date or trigger makes the next check happen (§22.8).

### Review Questions A
1. As-of date: the date on which a claim was made or measured, not when you read it. Evidence status: who checked a claim so far (measured, reported, forecast; the book's inference separate) (§22.1).
3. A case tier asks whether a fact is in the primary record; the evidence status asks who checked the capability. DeepMind's post is the confirmed record of what DeepMind says; the score it reports was checked by the IMO's coordinators, so the claim is measured (§22.1).
5. Where answers can be checked automatically (math, single-answer science questions, code with tests); a training procedure that rewards checkable right answers can improve most where checking is easy (§22.2; the "why" is this book's reading of Guo et al.).
7. The length of task (in skilled people's time) that the agent completes half the time; the 80% horizon is several times shorter, and a person who relies on an agent needs it to succeed most of the time (§22.3).
9. Do: run it on the institution's own hardware so inputs stay inside; study and adapt it. Does not reveal: the training data (§22.5).
11. That a formal proof is valid step by step; whether the formal statement matches the original problem (§22.7).

### Review Questions B
1. Date: the announcement's date; status: reported (a company's own announcement; one paper accepted at a workshop is a narrower fact than "writes publishable papers"); who could check: the workshop's reviewers and the paper itself. Rewrite: "In [month year], [company] said a paper produced with its system was accepted at a workshop; the claim that AI writes publishable research papers in general has not been tested."
3. 1 → 2 → 4 hours in about 10 months: two doublings, about one every five months. The 64-hour point is not data: it is an extrapolation, a forecast (§22.3).

### Review Questions C
1. Strong answers take a position and weigh the measured gap (months; §22.5) against things scores do not show: data terms, support, features, the license, local control and lock-in (Chapter 12); they name a task where each choice fits.

### Spaced Review
1. A tool's answer is not evidence that something is true; the verdict rests on a source you opened (Chapter 8).
3. Each step is another chance for error: under the book's calculation, 0.95^20 ≈ 36% of runs have no error (Chapter 14).
5. For example, a new tool or a change in a tool's terms or capabilities (such as an agent that can act on your files), which should trigger a review of the code (Chapter 21).

### Case Study Questions
1. Competition coordinators: the proofs, by student criteria; not the system, computing or human involvement. Developer-chosen graders: the proofs; not independent of the developer. Proof checker: each step of the formal proof; not whether the formalization matched the problem.
3. The olympiad case shows no wrong output; the Erdős post was a claim about an output published before it was checked, a workflow failure on chain link 2.
5. The Erdős claim was settled within days, because the problem list's maintainer could check his own records and the papers existed; the materials claim required specialists to examine compounds, which takes longer.
