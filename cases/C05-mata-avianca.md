# Case: Six Cases That Did Not Exist (*Mata v. Avianca, Inc.*, S.D.N.Y. 2023)

## Context
Roberto Mata says that on August 27, 2019, on Avianca Flight 670 from El Salvador to New York (JFK), an airline employee struck his left knee with a metal serving cart (ECF 55, p. 1). He sued on February 2, 2022, in New York state court; Avianca removed the case to federal court under the Montreal Convention (¶ 1). Steven A. Schwartz of Levidow, Levidow & Oberman P.C. did the substantive work; Peter LoDuca, admitted in that court, appeared as counsel of record (¶ 2). On January 13, 2023, Avianca moved to dismiss the claim as time-barred under the Convention's two-year limit (¶ 3).

## Timeline
- March 1, 2023: LoDuca files an "Affirmation in Opposition" citing and quoting purported decisions (¶ 5). Schwartz researched and wrote it; LoDuca reviewed it for style and read none of the cited decisions (¶ 6).
- March 15, 2023: Avianca's reply says it cannot locate many of the cases (¶ 7).
- April 11–12, 2023: the court orders an affidavit annexing copies of eight cited decisions (¶ 13).
- April 25, 2023: an affidavit annexes excerpts, not full decisions; it says one case ("Zicherman") could not be found (¶¶ 20–22).
- May 4, 2023: Order to Show Cause why LoDuca should not be sanctioned (¶ 42).
- May 25, 2023: Schwartz's affidavit first acknowledges that ChatGPT produced the cases (¶ 44; p. 2).
- June 8, 2023: sanctions hearing (p. 3).
- June 22, 2023: sanctions opinion (ECF 54); separate opinion dismissing the claim as time-barred (ECF 55).

## What the AI did
ChatGPT produced descriptions, summaries and excerpts of decisions that do not exist. The respondents acknowledged that six ("Varghese", "Miller", "Petersen", "Shaboon", "Martinez", "Durden") were generated by ChatGPT and do not exist (¶ 36). Screenshots show Schwartz asking ChatGPT "Is Varghese a real case" and whether the other cases were fake; its replies said it had supplied real authorities that could be found through Westlaw, LexisNexis and the Federal Reporter (¶ 45). The fake "Varghese" opinion: no such case in the Eleventh Circuit's records, per its Clerk (¶ 26); legal analysis the court described as nonsensical, with internal inconsistencies (¶ 27); docket number belonging to *Cornea v. U.S. Attorney General* and reporter citation associated with *J.D. v. Azar* (¶ 28); internal citations that do not exist, point to other cases, or do not contain the quoted language (¶ 29).

## What humans did
Schwartz testified that he did not think the tool could fabricate cases (¶ 11); he searched for "Varghese" on a free site, could not find it, and cited it anyway (¶ 12). LoDuca signed the filings without reviewing the authorities (¶¶ 6, 21) and gave a false reason when seeking an extension (Conclusions of Law ¶ 23). The court found bad faith based on conscious avoidance and false and misleading statements (p. 2).

## Failure label (Signature 5 and the failure-to-harm chain; source figures F8.11, F8.12; funnel F8.4d)
- **Tree — hallucination, documented** (leaf: invented facts or sources; ¶ 36).
- **Tree — overconfidence, documented** (leaf: certainty beyond the evidence). Chapter 4's rule: label overconfidence only with evidence about expressed certainty. Asked directly whether the cases were real, the replies said they were and named databases where they could be found (¶ 45). Contrast C03 (Moffatt): plain instructions, no such statement, no overconfidence label.
- **Chain — link 2 failed, documented** (¶¶ 6, 12). Link 3: filed with and relied on in court submissions; defended after orders (pp. 1–2). Link 4: penalty, letters, costs to the other side and the court (pp. 1–2, 33–34).
- **Where the funnel would have stopped it (F8.4d):** stage 1, the lawyer's own search (¶ 12); stage 2, the reporter citation and docket number (¶ 28); the authority, the Clerk's confirmation (¶ 26).

## Consequences (documented only)
Sanctions under Rule 11 or the court's inherent power: within 14 days, letters to Mata and to each judge falsely identified as author of the six fake opinions, with copies filed; a $5,000 penalty imposed jointly and severally on Schwartz, LoDuca and the firm (ECF 54, pp. 33–34). The claim was dismissed as time-barred in a separate opinion (ECF 55); the fake cases were not the reason. No appeal of the sanctions order found as of 2026-10-18 [VERIFY].

## A better workflow (AI Work Loop, for the firm)
Frame: a chatbot may help brainstorm arguments; it is not a source of law. Verify: open every citation in a legal research database before filing. Decide: the lawyer who signs reads the cited authorities. Disclose: follow the court's rules on AI use where they exist (Chapters 10, 18).
