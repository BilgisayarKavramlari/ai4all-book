# Case: A Message to a Grieving Campus (Vanderbilt Peabody College, 2023)

Chapter: 10 · Field: education / institutional communication · Failure label: **chain link 2, "checked before use?" — inappropriate delegation and skipped review (primary, documented** by the dean's statement); **tree: hallucination, recorded as "a wrong detail; AI origin inferred" (secondary, inferred**: the wrong detail is reported, its AI origin is not on record) · Outcome: negative (apology; two deans stepped back temporarily; returned)

## Context
On February 13, 2023, a gunman killed three students and wounded five at Michigan State University (reported: NPR, 2023). Peabody College, Vanderbilt University's college of education and human development, has an Office of Equity, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI), whose work is the college's equity and inclusion efforts. Vanderbilt's dean of students had emailed students on February 14; that message carried no note about ChatGPT (reported: Perrotta, 2023).

## Timeline
- February 13, 2023: shooting at Michigan State (reported).
- February 16, 2023: the EDI office emails the Peabody community about the shooting. The message spoke of safe, inclusive environments and of respect and understanding, and referred to shootings in Michigan in the plural, although there had been one (reported: Perrotta, 2023; Korn, 2023). Its last line, in smaller type, said it was a paraphrase of ChatGPT output, formatted like a personal-communication citation dated February 15, 2023 (confirmed, as quoted by Korn, 2023). The image's alt text and the AI line give February 15; the visible caption says the screenshot was taken February 17; the newspaper's text and the later reports give February 16 for the email.
- February 17: *The Vanderbilt Hustler* reports the email with students' reactions; the office's associate dean apologizes, calling the use of ChatGPT "poor judgment" (reported: Perrotta, 2023). A university spokesperson says Vanderbilt does not use AI systems to generate its messages (reported).
- February 18: the dean of Peabody College, Camilla Benbow, writes to the community (date: Lele, 2023). Her statement says the message did not follow Peabody's normal processes "providing for multiple layers of review", that administrators including herself were unaware of it before it was sent, that she was deeply troubled that a communication from her administration had missed the need for personal connection and empathy, and that her office will review what led to it; she apologizes (confirmed: Benbow, 2023, archived; also reported by Korn, 2023). The *Hustler* summarizes the review point in its own words (Lele, 2023). The office's associate and assistant deans step back temporarily (reported: Lele, 2023).
- April 11, 2023: the two deans return to their roles, per the dean's email (reported: Lele & Faquih, 2023).

## What the AI did
ChatGPT produced text that the office paraphrased (confirmed: the email's own line). How much of the output survived in the sent message is not reported. ChatGPT in February 2023 drew on training text that ended before 2022 and could not search the web, so the shooting's details must have come from the office's own prompt (this book's reading of how the tool worked then). Whether the plural "shootings" came from the tool or from the paraphrase is **inferred** at most.

## What humans did
The office drafted the message with ChatGPT and sent it without the college's usual layers of review (confirmed: the dean's statement). The office disclosed the tool in the message's last line. After students and the student newspaper reacted, the office apologized and the dean opened a review. The *Hustler* also ran the email through OpenAI's own AI-text classifier, noting OpenAI's warning that the tool identified only 26% of AI-written text (reported: Perrotta, 2023); the chapter does not treat that classifier's output as evidence (section 10.6).

## Failure label (figures F10.13 and F10.14)
- **Chain — link 2 "checked before use?" failed, documented:** an office's message to a grieving community was handed to a tool and sent without the normal review. Both parts are documented by the dean's statement: the skipped review layers, and the missed need for personal connection and empathy, which marks the delegation itself as the failure. Link 3: the message was sent to the community (confirmed). Link 4: harm to trust and to readers; the office's deans stepped back (reported).
- **Tree — secondary, hallucination, recorded as "a wrong detail; AI origin inferred" (dashed):** the inaccurate detail is reported by two outlets; that the tool produced it is not on record, and "invented facts" would overstate a wrong plural. This refines Chapter 4's handoff proposal ("none on the tree"), because an inaccurate detail is documented.
- **No overconfidence label:** no evidence about expressed certainty.

## Consequences (confirmed or reported, as marked)
Apology from the office (reported); dean's apology and review (confirmed, as quoted); two deans step back and return about seven and a half weeks later, February 18 to April 11 (reported). No outcome of the review is reported beyond the dean's statement that the issues had been addressed (reported: Lele & Faquih, 2023).

## A better workflow (AI Work Loop)
Frame: this is a message whose readers expect the office's own words. Prompt: at most, ask a tool for a checklist of what such messages usually include (support services, how to reach them). Verify: the facts (one shooting; the right services and hours). Decide: the normal review layers. Disclose: no line repairs a delegation the readers would reject.
