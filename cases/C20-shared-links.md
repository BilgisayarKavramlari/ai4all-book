# Case: Shared Chats in Search Results (ChatGPT, 2025)

Chapter: 15 (short case 2; Case Study only) · Field: consumer apps / privacy · Failure label: **none on the tree (input-side harm); data-flow map** ("seen by" box widened to anyone with a search engine) and **Risk Heat Map** (high impact for some users) · Outcome: negative (personal details made findable for some users; option removed)

## Context
In 2025 ChatGPT let users share a conversation by link and, with an extra opt-in box, make the shared chat findable by search engines.

## Timeline
- July 2025: *Fast Company* reports finding over 4,500 shared chats indexed by Google, some with personal details such as names and resumes (reported, as described by Southern, 2025; the book says "thousands").
- July 31, 2025: OpenAI's chief information security officer, Dane Stuckey, announces on X that the option, "a short-lived experiment," is removed because it introduced too many chances to share things by accident; OpenAI works to remove indexed content from search engines (reported: Abdullahi, 2025, eWeek, which names Stuckey; Southern, 2025, which quotes the statement).

## What the AI did
Nothing wrong: no output was false. The harm came from a sharing option.

## What humans did
Some users ticked the opt-in box, probably without expecting search engines to list the chats (inferred). The company removed the option.

## Labels
Tree does not apply (no output was wrong). Map: the "seen by" box widened (the shared-links data-flow map in the Case Study). Heat map: high impact for users whose personal details became findable; an opt-in box did not make the likelihood low (inferred).

## Consequences (documented only)
Option removed; removal of indexed chats under way, as reported.

## A better workflow
Before sharing a chat, check its "seen by" box: who can open the link, and can a search engine find it? Remove other people's details before sharing.
