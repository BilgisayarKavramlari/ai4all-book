# Case: Generative AI at Work — The Support Center (2025)

Chapter: 1 · Field: business (customer support) · Failure label: none — uneven benefit · Outcome: positive / mixed

## Context
A Fortune 500 company that sells business-process software gave its customer-support agents a generative AI chat assistant. The study does not name the firm; the book does not name or guess it (Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, 2025).

## Timeline
The assistant was introduced to groups of agents at different times (a staggered rollout). Researchers followed 5,172 agents and compared those who had received the assistant with those who had not yet, over the same weeks.

## What the AI did
It generated suggested replies during live chats, which agents could use, edit or ignore. It had been trained on past chat transcripts, with greater weight on chats handled by top performers [see fact-check log for the exact description].

## What humans did
Agents decided what to send and stayed responsible for the conversation. Managers decided who received the tool and when.

## Consequences (documented)
Issues resolved per hour rose 15% on average and about 30% for less experienced and lower-skilled agents, who also improved quality; the most experienced and highest-skilled agents saw small speed gains and small quality declines. Customers were more polite and less likely to ask for a manager; customer satisfaction scores did not change significantly. Gains were largest for moderately rare problems. The authors report evidence of worker learning and improved English writing, especially among international agents.

## A better workflow (AI Work Loop)
Frame: decide which problems benefit (moderately rare ones). Prompt: n/a (the tool proposes). Verify: newer agents check suggestions against documentation. Decide: the agent owns each reply. Disclose: follow the company's disclosure rule to customers.
