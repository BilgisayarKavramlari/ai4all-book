## Context

Employers that use AI, and some that build it, have published or circulated rules on how job applicants may use AI. The case centers on one AI company's published guidance (confirmed) and its earlier rule (reported), with two other large employers as a short comparison (reported). Status as of October 2026.

## What the AI did

Nothing in this case. The rules govern what applicants may ask AI tools to do: refine their own drafts (allowed by Anthropic), invent experience (not allowed), assist in take-home tasks (only when allowed) and assist in interviews (not allowed by any of the three rules as published or reported).

## What humans did

- **Anthropic (confirmed):** applicants write the first draft of resumes and application answers, then may refine with its model; never invent experience; no AI in take-home assessments unless stated; AI encouraged for interview preparation; no AI in live interviews unless indicated. The page says the company does not use candidates' data to train its model or let the model make hiring decisions, asks candidates to be transparent about AI use as the company is about its own use in hiring, and asks candidates who need accommodations for their interviews to tell the recruiter early.
- **Amazon (reported, Business Insider, Feb 27, 2025):** disqualification possible for generative AI use in interviews unless explicitly permitted; reasons reported as unfair advantage and difficulty of assessing a candidate's own skills.
- **Goldman Sachs (reported, Fortune, Jun 11, 2025):** no outside sources, including ChatGPT or a search engine, in its interview process (HireVue); the email went to EMEA private investing academy applicants, and the spokesperson said the same language goes to all campus applicants.

## Consequences (documented only)

No outcome data. The documented consequence is the rule change itself (ban reported in February and May 2025 → July 2025 guidance by stage, confirmed).

## Labels

None on the Failure Taxonomy Tree and no failure-to-harm chain: no AI output was wrong. Inferred (this book's reading, for Anthropic only; Amazon and Goldman Sachs are reported only for interviews): the rules share one line — AI may help applicants prepare and polish; the evidence of the person (experience, first thinking, live answers) must be the applicant's own. Three firms are not a survey.

## A better workflow (AI Work Loop: Frame → Prompt → Verify → Decide → Disclose)

For an applicant: **Frame** the application yourself (your experience, your reasons); **Prompt** only for refinement where the employer allows it; **Verify** that every sentence describes something you did; **Decide** what to keep in your own voice; **Disclose** AI use in the form the employer asks for, and use no tool where the employer forbids it (interviews).
