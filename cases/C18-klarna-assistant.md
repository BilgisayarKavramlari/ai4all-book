## Context
Klarna, a Swedish payments company, offers "buy now, pay later" installment plans. Its customers contact customer service about payments, refunds and returns. In early 2024 it launched an AI assistant built with OpenAI's technology to answer customer-service chats. The case is known from the company's own press release, from statements its chief executive made to Bloomberg (reported by other outlets) and on an earnings call. Every figure below is a company statement, not an independent measurement; the book marks its own reading as such.

## Timeline
- February 27, 2024: Klarna's press release on the assistant's first month (Klarna, 2024).
- May 8, 2025: Bloomberg interview with chief executive Sebastian Siemiatkowski (paywalled; reported by Entrepreneur on May 9, 2025) [VERIFY wording against Bloomberg].
- November 2025: third-quarter earnings call; reported by CX Dive on November 20, 2025 (Doerer, 2025).

## What the AI did (as the company described it)
- Handled 2.3 million conversations in its first month, two-thirds of Klarna's customer-service chats; the company equated this to the work of 700 full-time agents (Klarna, 2024).
- Customers resolved errands in under 2 minutes instead of 11; satisfaction on par with human agents; 25% fewer repeat inquiries; available in 23 markets, in more than 35 languages; an estimated $40 million profit improvement in 2024 (Klarna, 2024).
- November 2025: the chief executive said the assistant did the work of more than 853 full-time agents and had saved about $60 million; the company's presentation said satisfaction was on par with human agents (Doerer, 2025).

## What humans did
- Configured the assistant to answer customers directly; customers could choose a live agent (Klarna, 2024).
- May 2025: the chief executive said cost had weighed too heavily and the result was lower quality, and that customers would always be able to reach a person; Klarna recruited people for customer service on a flexible, remote ("Uber-type") model and said it remained committed to AI (Entrepreneur, 2025, reporting Bloomberg).
- An analyst quoted by CX Dive (Kate Leggett, Forrester) said Klarna had over-pivoted to cost containment (Doerer, 2025). One analyst's view.

## Mapped to the Delegation Ladder (Signature 7)
From the company's side: **agency** (configured to carry out future tasks for others; Dakan & Feller, 2025). Contrast with C01 (Chapter 1): also agency from the company's side, but a human agent checked each suggestion before the customer saw it. The difference is where the human check sits: before the output (C01) vs. an option after it (C18). No layoff or headcount claims are made in the book.

## Consequences (documented only)
Company-reported: speed, volume, cost savings, satisfaction on par. Company-stated in 2025: quality had suffered when cost dominated; a guaranteed human option and new human hiring. No independent study of outcomes is cited.

## A better workflow (AI Work Loop, for the team setting it up)
Frame: write quality and choice criteria next to cost and speed before launch. Prompt/configure: route question types that are costly to get wrong to people. Verify: sample and score conversations against the quality criteria. Decide: put a human check before high-cost outputs (Chapter 14 approval gates). Disclose: tell customers they are talking to an AI and how to reach a person.
