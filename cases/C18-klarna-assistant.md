# Case: Klarna's AI customer-service assistant — by the company's own measures (2024–2025)

Chapter: 5 (Opening Case and main Case Study) · Field: business / customer service (fintech) · Failure label: **none on the tree** (no specific wrong answers are documented in the sources used; one analyst reports customer complaints about generic answers to complex questions, Doerer, 2025). ## Context
Klarna, a Swedish payments company, offers "buy now, pay later" installment plans (Swedish: reported, secondary outlets; the press release calls Klarna a global digital bank and flexible payments provider). Its customers contact customer service about payments, refunds and returns. In February 2024 it launched an AI assistant built with OpenAI's technology to answer customer-service chats (confirmed: Klarna, 2024; Klarna Group, 2025). Before and alongside the assistant, human agents, many employed through outside service providers, handled chats (reported: Fisher, 2025; Engelking, 2025).

## Timeline
- February 27, 2024 (confirmed): press release on the assistant's first month (Klarna, 2024).
- January 24, 2025 (confirmed): draft registration statement (Form DRS/A) filed with the SEC (Klarna Group, 2025).
- May 8, 2025 (reported): Bloomberg interview with chief executive Sebastian Siemiatkowski; Bloomberg not read (paywall); statements attributed to Entrepreneur (Shibu, May 9), heise online (Engelking, May 11) and CX Today (Fisher, May 12), which were read.
- November 2025 (reported): CX Dive reports the CEO's statements; only one quotation is explicitly tied to the Q3 2025 earnings call (Doerer, 2025).

## What the AI did (as the company described it)
- Confirmed (Klarna, 2024): 2.3 million conversations in the first month, two-thirds of customer-service chats; the company equated this to the work of 700 full-time agents; resolution time under 2 minutes instead of 11; satisfaction on par with human agents; "more accurate in errand resolution," which the company said led to a 25% drop in repeat inquiries (a company claim; no method published); 23 markets, more than 35 languages; an estimated $40 million profit improvement in 2024; customers could choose live agents.
- Confirmed (Klarna Group, 2025): the assistant "has handled 60% of customer service chats since its launch," with no drop in consumer satisfaction according to internal surveys.
- Reported (Doerer, 2025): the CEO said the assistant could do the work of more than 853 full-time agents and had saved about $60 million; the company described satisfaction as on par with human agents.

## What humans did
- Confirmed: configured the assistant to answer customers directly, with live agents on request (Klarna, 2024).
- Reported (Fisher, 2025, quoting Bloomberg): the CEO said cost had been "a too predominant evaluation factor," resulting in lower quality. Sources for the 2024 quality claim: the press release ("more accurate in errand resolution") and the DRS/A ("more accurately resolved issues"; "resolved issues more accurately than human agents"); neither gives a method for accuracy; the DRS/A says the 25% compares repeat inquiries before and after launch.
- Reported (Shibu, 2025): customers must know there will always be a human if they want one; Klarna recruiting for fully remote customer-service roles.
- Reported (Fisher, 2025; Doerer, 2025): an "Uber-type" setup for in-house agents; described as a small pilot (Engelking, 2025), moving from outsourcing to insourcing.
- Reported (Engelking, 2025): the CEO remained enthusiastic about AI.
- Reported (Doerer, 2025): an analyst (Kate Leggett, Forrester) said Klarna over-pivoted to cost containment and reported customer complaints about generic answers. One analyst's view.

## Mapped to the Delegation Ladder (Signature 7)
Inferred: by the AI Fluency framework's definition (Dakan & Feller, 2025), **agency** (configured to carry out future tasks for others). C01 (Chapter 1) is also agency by the same definition; the designs differ in whether a human check sat before each output (C01: an agent reviewed each suggestion; C18: no check before each answer reached the customer, with a person available on request). An approval gate (Chapter 14) would add a check without changing the rung. No layoff or headcount claims are made.

## Consequences (documented only)
Company-reported: speed, volume, cost savings, satisfaction on par, claimed more accurate resolution (25% fewer repeat inquiries), 60% of chats handled since launch. Reported in 2025: the CEO's statement that quality had suffered when cost dominated; a stated commitment that a person is always available; a small in-house hiring pilot. No independent study of outcomes is cited.

## A better workflow (AI Work Loop, for the team setting it up)
Frame: write quality and choice criteria next to cost and speed before launch. Prompt/configure: route question types that are costly to get wrong to people. Verify: sample and score conversations against the quality criteria. Decide: put a human check before high-cost outputs (Chapter 14 approval gates). Disclose: tell customers they are talking to an AI and how to reach a person.
