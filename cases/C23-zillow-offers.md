# Case: Zillow Offers winds down after it cannot forecast home prices (2021)

Chapter: 19 (business guide) · Field: business (real estate, forecasting) · Failure label: other (a forecast used outside the conditions it was built for), inferred; chain link 4 loss documented · Outcome: negative

## Context
Zillow Offers bought homes for cash and resold them. From Feb 25, 2021, Zillow said its Zestimate, which it described as using AI including a deep-learning neural network, became the *initial* cash offer for a limited subset of homes in more than 20 markets (Zillow, 2021a; confirmed).

## Timeline
- Feb 25, 2021: Zestimate as initial cash offer for eligible homes (Zillow, 2021a).
- Oct 2021: Zillow paused new purchases through the end of 2021 (reported; Veiga, 2021).
- Nov 2, 2021: Q3 results and the plan to wind down Zillow Offers (Zillow, 2021b, 2021c).

## What the AI did
Zillow's own Q3 documents do not attribute the losses to an algorithm or a model; they say Zillow had been unable to forecast home prices accurately, with misses in both directions, larger than it had modeled (confirmed). That an AI model's forecasts were at fault is press framing: **inferred**.

## What humans did
Zillow bought 9,680 homes in Q3 and sold 3,032, ending the quarter with 9,790 in inventory; it decided to wind down the business (Zillow, 2021b; confirmed).

## Consequences (documented only)
A Q3 inventory write-down of about $304 million; expected Q4 losses of $240–265 million; a workforce reduction of about 25% over several quarters (Zillow, 2021b, 2021c; confirmed). AP reported the write-down as "about $340 million," which conflicts with Zillow's filing (use $304 million).

## A better workflow (AI Work Loop)
Frame: what conditions was the forecast built for? Verify: test forecasts on a period unlike the training period. Decide: approval gates on purchase volume when conditions change. Disclose: state the forecast's conditions on its card.
