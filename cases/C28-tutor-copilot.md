# Case: Tutor CoPilot — AI suggestions to tutors, not to students (2024–2025)

Chapter: 19 (education guide) · Field: education (K–12 tutoring) · Failure label: none on the tree (positive trial); chain: the tutor is the gate (design, documented) · Outcome: positive (mixed on long-term tests)

## Context
Tutor CoPilot, built by Stanford researchers, showed a human tutor three suggested replies, generated by GPT-4, during chat-based math tutoring; the tutor could edit or ignore them. The tool was tutor-facing only: students did not use or see it, though they may have read wording a tutor took from a suggestion (Wang et al., 2025; confirmed).

## Timeline
- Mar 27 – May 17, 2024: preregistered randomized controlled trial in grades 3–6 in one Southern U.S. school district.
- Oct 3, 2024: first arXiv version; later versions Jan 2025 and Oct 10, 2026; EdWorkingPaper No. 24-1054 (Nov 2025).
- As of October 2026: not peer reviewed (no journal version found).

## What the AI did
Generated three suggestions per moment for the tutor; the authors report that some did not fit the grade level.

## What humans did
About 900 tutors were randomized; 783 tutored in the study, with 1,013 students in the analysis (latest version; v1's "1,800 students" is superseded). Tutors chose whether to use each suggestion.

## Consequences (documented only)
Students of tutors with access to the tool (used in about 29% of those tutors' sessions) were 4 percentage points more likely to master the lesson's topic on an exit ticket (62% to 66%); for students of lower-rated tutors, 9 points (56% to 65%). The study did not show an effect on end-of-year tests. Cost: about $20 per tutor per year. The authors disclose that one author had worked part-time with the tutoring provider (confirmed).

## A better workflow (AI Work Loop)
Frame: help the tutor, not replace them. Verify: measure learning (exit tickets, later tests), not only use. Decide: the tutor chooses. Disclose: conflicts of interest and the short study period.
