# Case: A Government Report and Its References (Deloitte–DEWR, 2025)

Chapter: 9 (also 10, for disclosure) · Field: consulting / government · Failure label: hallucination (primary, **inferred**: the published references were incorrect, but no record shows they were generated) · chain link 2 "checked before use?" failed (documented: the errors were published; what checks ran: inferred) · Outcome: negative (corrections; final installment of the fee repaid)

Facts are stated in three attribution tiers (book-wide rule): **confirmed** = the Department of Employment and Workplace Relations' (DEWR) own pages and report PDFs, the AusTender notice and Senate Hansard; **reported** = a named outlet or person; **inferred** = this book's reading. ## Context
The Targeted Compliance Framework is the set of rules under which the Australian government can suspend or reduce welfare payments when people do not meet "mutual obligation" requirements. The department said it had identified examples where the IT system operating the framework did not operate in line with the law and policies, including in ways that affected people's payments, and that the government commissioned an independent review of that system (confirmed: DEWR, 2025a). Deloitte undertook the review from December 2024 to June 2025 (confirmed: DEWR, 2026). The review covered the department's IT system, not partner agencies' systems or human decision-making (confirmed: DEWR, 2025a). Contract value A$439,142 (about A$440,000; about US$290,000 as the AP converted it) under contract notice CN4118426, contract period December 23, 2024 to July 4, 2025 (confirmed: AusTender; reported: McGuirk, 2025). The report says the work was advisory and was not conducted under the Australian Auditing and Assurance Standards Board's standards, so it expresses no opinions under those standards (confirmed: report PDF, p. 2); "independent assurance review" is the department's name for it.

## Timeline
- December 2024 – June 2025: review conducted (confirmed).
- August 14, 2025: the department publishes the final report, dated July 4, 2025 on its cover, and a statement of assurance (confirmed: DEWR, 2025a, 2025b; the report PDF). The Associated Press wrote that the report was originally published on the department's website in July; the department's own statements give August 14. The publisher settles the publication date; the cover settles the report's own date.
- August 22, 2025: the *Australian Financial Review* reports citation errors in the report after Chris Rudge, a University of Sydney researcher in health and welfare law, raised them (reported: Karp, 2025).
- October 3, 2025: the department's statement notes media reports about citation accuracy, says Deloitte "has confirmed some footnotes and references were incorrect" and that a corrected version of the statement of assurance and final report has been released (confirmed: DEWR, 2025b). The corrected report is dated September 26, 2025 and was published October 3, 2025 (confirmed: DEWR, 2026).
- October 8, 2025: the final installment, A$97,587.11 (GST inclusive), is repaid, per the Department of Finance at Senate estimates that day (confirmed: Senate Hansard) and the AusTender notice (confirmed). The department had said Deloitte agreed to repay the final installment (reported: McGuirk, 2025); a department spokesperson confirmed "over A$97,000" on October 21 (reported: Alexis, 2025).
- February 3, 2026: the department updates the report again "to address identified corrections"; the update keeps the September 26 cover date. A text comparison of the October 2025 and February 2026 PDFs shows changes to a handful of footnote citations to one book (Ayres and Braithwaite, *Responsive Regulation*: author spelling, year, an added author), not to findings or recommendations (confirmed: DEWR, 2026; fact-checker's comparison; layout and images not compared).

## What the AI did
The corrected report added a disclosure in its methodology: a generative AI large language model (Azure OpenAI GPT-4o) based tool chain, licensed by the department and hosted on its Azure tenancy, had been used in the technical workstream to assess whether the system's code could be mapped to business requirements (confirmed: report PDF, pp. 48, 147). The July version has no such disclosure. The Associated Press summarized this as AI having been used in writing the report (reported: McGuirk, 2025). No public source says the tool produced the incorrect references or any report text; that is **inferred**. Deloitte did not answer the AP's question about whether AI produced the errors (reported).

## What humans did
Before publication: two checkpoints existed, the firm's own review of its report and the department's acceptance and publication of it; what either checked is not on the public record (inferred). After publication: Rudge reported up to 20 errors, including a book attributed to Professor Lisa Burton Crawford that does not exist (he said he knew at once it was either invented by AI or the best-kept secret in his field), and a quotation attributed to a Federal Court judge in a real case, *Amato v Commonwealth*, that the reporting said was made up; the corrected report replaced that passage and describes the case as resolved by consent orders (reported: McGuirk, 2025; visible in the two PDF versions). He said colleagues' work was cited as "tokens of legitimacy" (reported). Deloitte confirmed some footnotes and references were incorrect (confirmed via the department: DEWR, 2025b) and told the AP the matter had been "resolved directly with the client" (reported). The department said the report's substance and recommendations were unchanged (reported: McGuirk, 2025; the report's own update note says the same of the changes, p. 2). Senator Barbara Pocock (Australian Greens) said the full fee should be repaid (reported: McGuirk, 2025).

## Consequences (confirmed or reported, as marked)
Corrected report (confirmed); a further correction in 2026 (confirmed); the final installment of A$97,587.11 repaid (confirmed); public criticism, including a call for a full refund (reported). The department says the recommendations stand (reported). Scope: one commissioned report; no finding by any court or regulator is reported here.

## A better workflow (AI Work Loop, for the firm and the client)
Frame: decide which parts of a report an AI tool may draft and which require a named expert. Verify: open every reference and check that it exists and says what is claimed (Chapter 8's checks), and trace every legal quotation to the court's own record (Chapter 9, 9.4). Decide: the client accepts the report only with a reference check on record. Disclose: state AI use where readers will see it (Chapter 10 discusses whether a methodology note is enough).
