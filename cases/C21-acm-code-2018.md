## Context
The Association for Computing Machinery (ACM) is a professional society for people who work in computing. It publishes a Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct for its members. Chapter 21 uses the 2018 update as a model of how a code is built, what it says it cannot do, and how codes age, not as a story of failure.

## Timeline
- **1992:** the code's previous version; the ACM president's 2018 preface says it was last updated then (confirmed).
- **About 2016–2018:** the update was drafted by the ACM Code 2018 Task Force, organized by the ACM Committee on Professional Ethics, with contributions from the international membership (confirmed: booklet); ACM's press release of July 17, 2018 describes a two-year process, with drafts published for comment (confirmed: archived capture of the release).
- **June 22, 2018:** the ACM Council adopted the code and its guidelines (confirmed).
- **As of October 2026:** the 2018 code is the current version (confirmed by ACM's code page as archived July 9, 2026; the booklet PDF was still served on October 18, 2026).

## What the AI did
Nothing: no AI system is involved. The 2018 code addresses machine-learning systems in principle 2.5 (extraordinary care with their risks) and asks that a system whose future risks cannot be reliably predicted be reassessed often as it evolves in use, or not be deployed (confirmed).

## What humans did
- The president's preface explains the need for an update: in 1992 many people saw computing work as purely technical and the web was in its infancy; by 2018 computing work could affect lives and livelihoods in ways that may not be intended or even predictable (confirmed).
- The co-chairs' preface says the principles are taken as a whole and applying them requires judgment; it gives the example of a reading app that meets a requirement to enlarge text for readers with impaired vision but prints the instructions in tiny type (confirmed).
- The Preamble names its audience (current and aspiring practitioners, instructors, students, and anyone who uses computing in an impactful way), says principles are statements of responsibility based on the public good, and says the code is not an algorithm for solving ethical problems but a basis for ethical decision-making (confirmed; the book quotes only this one phrase).
- Structure: Section 1 general ethical principles; Section 2 professional responsibilities (2.3 know and respect existing rules; rules judged unethical should be challenged; consider challenging a rule through existing channels before violating it; whoever violates a rule must consider the consequences and accept responsibility; 2.5 evaluations and risks; 2.6 competence and disclosure of its limits); Section 3 leadership (3.6 care when modifying or retiring systems; 3.7 systems that become infrastructure); Section 4 compliance (4.1 uphold the code; 4.2 violations inconsistent with membership, may be reported) (confirmed). ACM also publishes an Enforcement Policy (confirmed by the archived code page; the policy's content is not described in the book).

## Consequences (documented only)
A revised code adopted by the ACM Council; the 2018 version remains current as of October 2026. No harm or failure is involved.
