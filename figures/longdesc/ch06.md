## Long descriptions (complex figures)

**F6.3 — Context layers (new reusable diagram; also F6.5, F6.8, F6.13 and the summary).** A large shaded frame labeled "Context window — everything the model receives this turn" holds six stacked boxes, with a column "Who sets it" to their right. From top to bottom: "System prompt — set by the tool's maker," drawn with a dashed outline because it is usually not visible to you; who sets it: the provider, usually unseen. "Standing instructions — your rules for every chat in a scope"; who sets it: you, once, in options or a project; marked "new in Chapter 6." "Project files and memory notes — added automatically"; who sets it: you, or the product; marked "new in Chapter 6." "Conversation so far — earlier prompts and responses"; who sets it: you and the model, earlier. "Pasted or retrieved text — documents, search results"; who sets it: you, or a search tool. "Your prompt — the newest message," a rounded box; who sets it: you, now. An arrow leads from the frame to a hexagon, "The model — receives all layers as one input." A legend explains: dashed = usually not visible to you; rounded = you, now; hexagon = AI component; dark or thick = the layer a section discusses. A note says these are Chapter 3's four layers, with two that persistent features fill before you type. Variants highlight one or two layers: pasted or retrieved text (F6.5, section 6.2); standing instructions and project files and memory notes (F6.8, section 6.5); your prompt, with a side note "Plus a workflow: 5 runs, answer options shuffled, majority vote" (F6.13, Medprompt).

**F6.6 — Before and after: building Sam's parts-order context.** Two panels separated by a dashed line. Left, "Before: everything pasted": a rounded box "Draft a parts order for the club — task first, with no criteria"; a large box with a thick border (orange in color, hatched in print) "Supplier catalog, all 120 pages — motors, wheels, sensors, tools, clothing, returns policy, 2026 and 2027 price lists mixed"; a box "Club budget rules, all 8 pages — travel, food, parts, fundraising"; notes: "The rule that applies is on page 6. Two price lists disagree." Right, "After: selected, labeled, task last": six stacked boxes: "TASK IN BRIEF — a parts order for the club (details at the end)"; "INSTRUCTIONS — Use only the material below; if a part is missing, say so"; three shaded boxes "CATALOG p. 14 · 2027 price list — drive motors (four models)", "CATALOG p. 52 · 2027 price list — wheels and hubs", "BUDGET RULE 3 · Jan 2027 — parts orders over $250 need two officers"; and a rounded box with a thick border, "TASK (full, last) — Draft an order for two motors and four wheels; list total and who must approve." The figure is labeled as a constructed example.

**F6.9 — A template built on the Anatomy of a Prompt (Signature 6, imported from Chapter 5).** The Chapter 5 diagram: a shaded box "Your prompt" with six rounded parts (role; context; in-context examples; task, thick, always needed; constraints; output format), an arrow to a hexagon "The AI tool — receives only what is written," and an arrow down to "Output — scored against your criteria." Only the context part is highlighted; a note says that in a template the highlighted part becomes a placeholder filled each time. Below a rule, "Where the parts land in the context layers": context goes to pasted text, project files or the prompt itself (sections 6.2, 6.5); in-context examples are a fixed part of the template, kept only if a with-and-without test shows they help (6.4); task, constraints and format are the fixed part of the template (6.6).

**F6.10 — A library entry card (constructed example).** A card headed "Library entry: Club update email · v1.1 · 2027-02-03" lists seven fields: Purpose — turn my meeting notes into the weekly update for members; Use when / not when — weekly meeting notes, not for budget or safety decisions; Template — {NOTES} and {DATE} first, then the fixed task, audience and format; Context to attach — my notes, names replaced by roles, never members' contact details; In-context examples — not used, the test with and without showed no gain; Tested on — tool and model name and date, in the file; What changed — v1.1 adds: if no decisions were made, write "No decisions this week." Below, a test record with four rows: typical (normal meeting, 3 decisions): 3 of 3 criteria, keep; different (two meetings merged): 3 of 3, keep; edge, v1.0 (no decisions; the draft invented one): 2 of 3, criterion (b) failed, fix to v1.1; edge, v1.1 (same notes, rerun): 3 of 3, keep. Pass criteria are listed underneath: every decision in the notes appears; nothing appears that is not in the notes; each task has an owner and a date.

**F6.11 — Sam's three-step chain with a check.** Left to right: a rectangle "Meeting notes — names replaced by roles" → a hexagon "Step 1: extract decisions and action items" → a rounded box with a thick border "Sam checks names, dates and owners against his notes" → a diamond "All correct?" A dashed arrow labeled "no: fix the list" returns from the diamond to Sam's check. From the diamond, an arrow labeled "yes" goes down to a hexagon "Step 2: draft the update email," and from there left to a rounded box "Sam reads, edits and sends." A line beneath reads: each step's output becomes the next step's input; the check sits where errors start. Legend: rectangle = material, hexagon = AI step, rounded = Sam (thick = the check), diamond = decision, dashed = loop back. The figure notes that Sam fixes the steps and runs them; the model does not choose them.

**F6.12 — Medprompt's steps on MedQA (published values).** A bar chart with the vertical axis from 79% to 92% (the axis does not start at zero, as its label says). Five bars, cumulative in the authors' order: no examples (zero-shot, simple prompt), 81.7%; plus five random solved questions, 83.9% (+2.2); plus reasoning GPT-4 wrote for them, 87.3% (+3.4); examples chosen by similarity, 88.4% (+1.1); plus five shuffled runs and a vote, 90.2% (+1.8). The source line cites Nori et al. (2023), Figure 4 (the paper's step-by-step chart), notes that the authors call their order subjective, and that the paper's Figure 6 gives +0.8 and +2.1 for the last two steps.

**F6.4 — Irrelevant context (published values).** Three bars for code-davinci-002, an older OpenAI model, with step-by-step prompting: original 100 base problems, 95.0%; the same problems with one irrelevant sentence added (4,000 problems tested, from 58,052 built), 72.4%; with an added instruction to ignore irrelevant information, 77.8%. Source: Shi et al. (2023), Tables 2 and 3. In print, the distracted bar is hatched.
