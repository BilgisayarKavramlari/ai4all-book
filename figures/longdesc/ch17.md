## Long descriptions (complex figures)

**F17.3 (f17-03-layers), the explanation-layers signature.** At the left, a hexagon labeled "The model: inputs in, output out; workings not visible." Thin lines fan out from it to six stacked boxes, each phrased as a question: 1 Notice, "Was an AI system used in this decision?"; 2 Documentation, "Purpose, data, tests and known limits?"; 3 Global logic, "What drives its outputs overall?"; 4 Local reasons, "Why this output for this case?"; 5 What would change it, "What would give a different result?"; 6 Contest (a rounded box, a person), "Who can review and change it, and how?" A column at the right, headed "Who usually asks," lists: the people affected and the public; deployers, auditors, journalists; builders and oversight bodies; the reviewer and the person affected; the person affected; the person affected and a reviewer with power to change it. A line below says: layers, not steps; a decision can need any of them, in any order. The legend explains the states: light fill = present; an empty box with the word "missing" = missing; a dotted frame with the word "partly" = partly present; thick border = focus; dashed border = inferred.

**F17.4 (f17-04-layers-c14), the benefits case.** The same diagram for the parents in the Dutch case. Layers 1 to 5 are empty boxes labeled "missing," with notes: use hidden from the public for years; no public information on the model; workings not visible to users (these three reported, Amnesty 2021); reviewers could not see which indicators drove a score (confirmed, AP 2020); no basis to say what would change it (reported). Layer 6, Contest, has a dotted frame labeled "partly," with the note: objection and appeal existed; the inquiry faults the courts too (confirmed).

**F17.8 (f17-08-occlusion), Sam's replica sorter.** Two 12 × 12 grids of squares for one constructed bolt image; the bolt's pixels (a vertical bar two pixels wide) are outlined. Darker squares pushed the model's score harder toward "bolt." Each pixel was covered with a fixed average-gray value. Left, the old model trained on dark-desk bolts: almost every square is pale, a few bolt squares are mid-gray; the panel title says background pixels supplied 79% of the evidence for "bolt" and 43% of bolts were sorted right on a gray workbench. Right, the model retrained on mixed backgrounds: darker squares on the bolt and on the columns beside it, where a nut's or washer's ring would be; 66% of the evidence from background pixels; 100% of bolts right. A note says the occlusion maps matched the exact contributions of this glass-box model (correlation 1.00 and 0.99), and that against other baselines the old model's background share runs from 13.5% to 84%.

**F17.14 (f17-14-funnel-self-account).** Chapter 8's Verification Funnel, with two stages highlighted: Claims (note: the model's account of its own steps) and 2 Primary source (note: the system's record: its rule, its logs, a traced computation). The independent check's note: rerun without the hint; does the answer change? Subtitle: a model's account of its reasoning is a claim to check, not a record of what the system did.

**F17.19 (f17-19-chain-c14).** Chapter 4's failure-to-harm chain. Link 1, output: risk score with a citizenship indicator (confirmed: AP). Link 2, checked before use?, thick border: reviewer could not see which indicators drove the score (confirmed: AP). Link 3, someone relies on it: high score, payment delayed, evidence requested (confirmed: AP). Link 4, loss: investigations, suspended payments, reclaims (reported: Amnesty). Tag: confirmed = the AP's report; reported = Amnesty (2021); opacity is not a tree branch: it disabled link 2 (the book's reading).

**F17.20 (f17-20-lifecycle-c14).** Chapter 16's Bias Lifecycle. Data (highlighted): historic correct and incorrect applications; citizenship indicator. Model: a "self-learning" risk model (the office's description, per the AP). Use (highlighted): reviewer could not see which indicators drove a score (AP). Feedback (dashed, inferred): decisions as new examples? (the book's reading). Tag: the AP finding enters at data; the missing reasons fail at use.

**F17.21 (f17-21-heat-sam).** Chapter 15's Risk Heat Map. One risk, marked I (inferred, dashed outline): members told an AI summary "explains" their score. Before mitigation (hollow marker) at medium likelihood, medium impact; after (filled) at low likelihood, medium impact, with an arrow. Note: reduce, quote the rule and the officers' notes; avoid, no AI-written reasons; inferred because no pooled test was run on the club's own tool.

**F17.9 (f17-09-counterfactual).** An applicant box, "Not approved: income 61.8k, debt ratio 0.285, 0 late payments, age band 1," with two arrows. Upper path (filled, actionable): debt ratio 0.285 → 0.276, "pay down a little debt," leading to "Approve (0.51), model output." Lower path: age band 1 → 2, "cannot be changed soon: only by waiting years," leading to "Approve (0.59): true of the model; not advice." A note: both searches in the simulation found the same smallest change, debt ratio down by 0.009; a one-band age change also flips it, at a higher cost.

**F17.10 (f17-10-fidelity).** Bars for decision trees of depth 1 to 4 that imitate the loan model: agreement on 500 new cases 73.4%, 82.4%, 84.2% and 85.2%. A solid horizontal line at 73.4% is labeled "a rule that rejects everyone." Under each bar, the share of the model's approvals the tree also approves: 0%, 50%, 61%, 64%. Title: a rule that rejects everyone already agrees with the loan model on 73.4% of new cases; a two-question summary reaches 82.4% and matches only half of its approvals.
