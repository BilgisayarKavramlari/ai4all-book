## Long descriptions (complex figures)

**F10.2 (f10-02-storyboard).** A horizontal timeline with five events alternating above and below the line. February 13, 2023 (thick border, hatched in print): a shooting at Michigan State University killed three students (reported). February 16 (yellow card): Peabody College's equity office emailed the Peabody community, and the last line credited ChatGPT (confirmed, as quoted). February 17 (rounded): the student newspaper reported the email and the associate dean apologized (reported). February 18 (rounded): the dean wrote that the usual layers of review were skipped, opened a review, and two deans stepped back. April 11 (light): the two deans returned (reported). Notes below give the sources and say that readers' expectations and the extent of the office's editing are inferred.

**F10.3 (f10-03-voice-spectrum), the voice and ownership spectrum.** An arrow across the top reads "More of the wording supplied by a tool", with the note "so more of your judgment is needed to keep the message yours." Below it, five rounded boxes from left to right: 1, your words only (no AI); 2, AI as reader (comments; you change it); 3, AI as editor (grammar, rewording or translation); 4, AI as co-drafter (drafts from your points; you rewrite); 5, AI as author (sent as is). Inside each box a small bar shows the tool's share of the wording, from empty at position 1 to almost full at position 5; the box labels say the same in words. Under the boxes, a line reads "Which position fits depends on what these readers expect", above two wide bands separated by a dashed line: "Own words expected" (apology, message after a loss, personal statement, reference letter, reflection essay) and "Accuracy and clarity first" (meeting summary, FAQ answer, routine notice, status update). A legend explains the shapes.

**F10.3b (f10-03b-spectrum-peabody).** The same spectrum with the subtitle "Readers expected the office's own words (inferred)". A dashed bracket over positions 4 and 5 is labeled "Peabody email (2023): position 4 or 5, inferred", and the band "Own words expected" is highlighted.

**F10.7 (f10-07-keep-rewrite-reject).** A flow for one sentence of a draft (hexagon at top). Four diamonds in a column ask, in order: "True? Facts go through the funnel"; "Says what I mean, in my voice?"; "Right for this reader?"; "Can I stand behind it? Safe to share?". A "no" from each leads right: to "Reject or fix" (tag: accuracy; thick border), "Rewrite" (tag: voice), "Rewrite" (tag: audience and tone), and "Reject" (tags: commitment, privacy; thick border). "Yes" to all four leads down to "Keep" (green). A note says to record the code and the tag in the annotated draft.

**F10.10 (f10-10-liang-essays), data chart.** A grid of 91 squares, one per human-written essay by a non-native English speaker for the TOEFL. Eighteen squares are dark (hatched in print): flagged as AI-generated by all seven detectors. Seventy-one are medium: flagged by one to six detectors. Two are white: flagged by none. A side note says the 88 U.S. eighth-grade essays were classified as human with near-perfect accuracy. The source line gives Liang et al. (2023), detectors as of early 2023, essays from one forum, counts derived from the paper's 97.8% and 19.8%, and the average false-positive rate of 61.3%.

**F10.12 (f10-12-disclosure).** Three diamonds in a column: "Does a rule require it? (course, employer, client, profession)", "Do these readers expect your own words?", "Would a reasonable reader feel misled later?". A "yes" from any leads to a green box: "Disclose, where readers will see it" (a line in the message, a note to your manager, a project log, the course declaration, a citation for quoted AI output, Chapter 9). "No to all" leads down to "No line needed; keep your record." A thick-bordered box at right reads "The sender answers either way: a label tells readers how a message was made, not who checks it (Moffatt, paras. 27–28; this book's reading)."

**F10.13 (f10-13-chain-peabody).** Chapter 4's failure-to-harm chain with four stages and notes above each: 1, the output is wrong ("One wrong detail: 'shootings' for one", reported); 2, checked before use? (thick border: "Inappropriate delegation; review skipped", documented); 3, someone relies on it ("Sent to the Peabody community"); 4, loss or harm ("Hurt and lost trust; deans step back", reported). A "caught" path below stage 2 is not taken. The tag names the primary label: chain link 2, documented by the dean's statement.

**F10.15 (f10-15-base-rates), computed chart (Deep).** Three horizontal bars show the share of flagged texts that were written by a person, if a detector flags 26% of AI-written texts and 9% of human texts (the rates OpenAI reported for its 2023 classifier): 87% when 5% of texts are AI-written, 76% at 10%, and 51% at 25%. A dashed line marks 50%. The note says this is a computed illustration with assumed shares, not a study.

**F10.17 (f10-17-midterm-map).** The five AI Work Loop steps in a row (Frame, Prompt, Verify, Decide, Disclose), Decide and Disclose thick for this chapter. Under each step, as text: Frame — Chapter 1 (Work Loop; Jagged Frontier), Chapter 5 (Delegation Ladder), Chapter 7 (scaffold versus crutch); Prompt — Chapter 3 (Anatomy of an LLM Response), Chapter 5 (Anatomy of a Prompt), Chapter 6 (context layers; Deep, not examined); Verify — Chapter 2 (Data-to-Prediction Pipeline), Chapter 4 (Failure Tree and chain), Chapter 7 (closed-book check), Chapter 8 (Verification Funnel), Chapter 9 (source ladder); Decide — Chapter 10 (keep, rewrite or reject); Disclose — Chapter 10 (voice spectrum; disclosure), Chapter 9 (cite what you read, second). A bottom line says the midterm covers the Core sections of Chapters 1–10 and that Chapter 6 is Deep and not examined. The same content appears as a table in the chapter.
