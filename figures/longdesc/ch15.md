## Long descriptions (complex figures)

**F15.2 (f15-02-storyboard).** Seven boxes left to right: Mar 11, 2023 chip division allows a chat tool, staff told to take care; Paste 1 source code of a chip-data program to fix an error; Paste 2 source code of a yield and defect program to optimize it; Paste 3 a meeting recording turned into text to draft minutes (the three pastes thick); Notice: once sent, stored on outside servers, cannot be retrieved; Emergency rule: uploads limited to 1,024 bytes per question; May 2, 2023: generative AI restricted on company devices (confirmed by a spokesperson). Labels: Mar 30 The Economist Korea reports the pastes; Bloomberg, citing the memo. A shaded box: no public Samsung statement confirms the pastes; March vs. April discrepancy. Sources: Jeong (2023); Gurman (2023) via Sharma (2023); Park (2023).

**F15.3 (f15-03-data-flow-map).** Nine shapes. Left of a shaded vertical band labeled 'Trust boundary' ('your side'): a rounded box 'You (or your team)' and a rectangle 'Input (typed, pasted, uploaded)'. Right of the line ('someone else's rules'): a hexagon 'AI tool (the model, at the provider)' with 'Connectors (other apps it reads or sends to)' above it, joined by arrows both ways; arrows lead on to 'Output (the response)' and a rounded box 'Recipients (anyone it is forwarded to)'. Below the tool: 'Storage (history, memory, logs, backups)', then a rounded box 'Seen by (reviewers, admins, people with a link)', then a shaded box 'Reuse (training future models?)', shaded because it depends on the terms. Note: ask of every box what is there, for how long and under whose rules.

**F15.4 (f15-04-flow-samsung).** The data-flow map (f15-03-data-flow-map) with Input and Storage drawn thick (risk). Notes: input 'source code; a meeting transcript'; storage 'outside servers; could not be retrieved (reported)'; reuse 'depended on the account's terms'. Bottom note: this book reads the pastes as made under consumer terms; the reports do not say which account was used.

**F15.5 (f15-05-flow-terms).** The data-flow map with Seen by drawn thick. Notes: input 'the same input travels the same way'; storage 'kept for the period the contract sets'; seen by 'institutional: your school's or employer's admins'; reuse 'institutional terms: often no training'. Bottom note: institutional terms change the reuse and seen-by boxes; the data still crosses the boundary.

**F15.6 (f15-06-reid).** Horizontal bar chart from Sweeney (2000), estimates from 1990 U.S. Census summary data: share of the U.S. population likely unique on five-digit ZIP code, sex and date of birth 87% (about 216 of 248 million people); on city or town, sex and date of birth 53%; on county, sex and date of birth 18%. Source line: not a count of people identified.

**F15.8 (f15-08-layers).** Six boxes in a row: Label untrusted content; Trained priority; Filters; Least privilege; Approval gate (rounded, a person); Log. Below them a solid line for injected text runs left to right and gets thinner after each layer, ending in a small arrow. A wide box below: each layer lets less through; none has been shown to stop every attack outside its tests (as of October 2026).

**F15.9 (f15-09-loop-injection).** Signature 9, the agent loop from Chapter 14 (goal, plan, approval gate, tool call, observation, check point, stop decision, hand back, log strip), with Observation drawn thick and annotated 'a retrieved page carries an instruction'. Note: constructed example; a browsing agent compares two public pages; the next tool call acts with the agent's permissions.

**F15.10 (f15-10-three-legs).** A hexagon 'One AI system' with three thick boxes pointing into or out of it: 'Private data (it can read your mail, files or records)', 'Untrusted content (it receives text a stranger can write)', 'A way to send data out (it can send messages or load links)'. Text: remove any one leg and this route for stealing data closes; injected text can still change the outputs, so they still need a check. Credit: after Willison (2025).

**F15.12 (f15-12-channel-check).** Flow: 'A request (money, logins or data)' → 'Pause (urgency is a cue)' → diamond 'Confirmed out of band?' → yes → 'Second approver (dual authorization)' → 'Act'; no → thick box 'Stop; report it'. Above, a shaded box 'How real the voice or face seems', crossed out and labeled 'not a check: a voice or a face can be generated'.

**F15.13 (f15-13-account-change).** Flow: 'Caller asks to change the account email' → 'Code sent to the email already on file' → diamond 'Code confirmed?' → yes → rounded 'Second agent (approves login or payment changes)' → 'Change'; no → thick 'No change; call logged'. Text: nothing changes on a call alone (constructed example).

**F15.14 (f15-14-heat-map).** Signature 10. A 3-by-3 grid: likelihood across (low, medium, high), impact up (low, medium, high). Each cell is labeled likelihood · impact in words. The top row (high impact) is a band with a thick border, tinted in color and hatched in print, labeled 'High-impact band: mitigate, or name an owner who accepts the risk'. Legend: circle = one risk; D with solid outline = documented; I with dashed outline = inferred; hollow = the current rating.

**F15.15 (f15-15-heat-lucia).** The heat map with five hollow markers: 1 (D) and 2 (D) in medium likelihood, high impact, inside the band; 3 (I) in medium, medium; 4 (I) and 5 (D) in low, medium. Note: constructed example; risks listed in section 15.5.

**F15.16 (f15-16-heat-moves).** The heat map with before-markers hollow and after-markers filled, joined by arrows: 1 and 2 move from medium to low likelihood and stay in the high-impact band; 3 moves from medium to low likelihood at medium impact; 4 and 5 stay at low, medium. Note: risks 1 and 2 stay in the band, so the team lead owns them; risk 3's owner is Lucía.

**F15.18 (f15-18-heat-samsung).** The heat map with one documented risk: 'confidential data pasted into an outside tool', hollow at high likelihood, high impact, with an arrow to a filled marker at low likelihood, high impact. Note: data already sent did not move.

**F15.19 (f15-19-a12-sheet).** Eight stacked rows of the A12 record: 1 scope; 2 data-flow map with trust boundary (drawn in the lab); 3 register of five risks, four drafted in the lab (privacy, security, fairness, rules and rights); 4 red-team record (injection three runs each way, canary trace, bias probe); 5 heat map, hollow before and filled after with arrows; 6 plan for each qualifying risk (kind, change, owner, new rating); 7 note for the decision owner (60–80 words); 8 AI Use Declaration. Footer: fake data only; real data returned for resubmission.

**F15.22 (f15-22-defense-families).** Three columns. Train: the model is trained to favor privileged instructions; leaves open: attacks still succeed, less often. Detect: classifiers flag likely injections; leaves open: new attacks can slip past a filter. Design: the plan comes only from the user's request, tools' data cannot steer it; leaves open: fewer tasks completed, and a summary can still be changed.

**F15.23 (f15-23-same-cell).** The heat map with two inferred (dashed) markers in the medium-likelihood, high-impact cell. Note: 1 = a mislabeled reply to one customer; 2 = every customer's record exposed; same cell, very different harm.

**F15.24 (f15-24-tree-echoleak).** Signature 5, the Failure Taxonomy Tree from Chapter 4, with the security vulnerability branch drawn thick (solid, documented). Tag: C19 EchoLeak (2025), security vulnerability, indirect prompt injection, documented; record: CVE-2025-32711, fixed by the vendor, no known exploitation.

**F15.25 (f15-25-flow-echoleak).** The data-flow map with Input and Output drawn thick. Notes: input 'an email from outside, with hidden instructions'; connectors 'the user's mail and files, retrieved to answer'; output 'a link or image address that could carry data out'. Note: the assistant retrieved the attacker's email with the user's own data; fixed by the vendor.

**F15.26 (f15-26-flow-links).** The data-flow map with Seen by drawn thick and annotated 'anyone using a search engine, once a shared chat was made findable'; recipients 'people with the link'. Note: an opt-in sharing option widened who could see a chat; the option was removed.
