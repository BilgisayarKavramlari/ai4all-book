## Long descriptions (complex figures)

**Figure 1.2 — AI Work Loop.** Five rounded boxes, the book's shape for human decisions, are arranged clockwise in a circle: 1 Frame (what is the task; should AI be in it), 2 Prompt (give task, context, limits, format), 3 Verify (check against something other than the tool), 4 Decide (keep, change or reject; own the result), 5 Disclose (say what you used and how). Curved arrows connect each step to the next and step 5 back to step 1. In the center, a hexagon (the AI component) reads "The AI tool — produces drafts; you make every decision." In the chapter-opening version, Frame is filled dark with white text and a thick border.

**Figure 1.6 — Frontier results.** Left panel, inside the frontier (18 tasks, 385 consultants): three bars showing change against the control group — tasks completed +12.2%, speed +25.1%, human-graded quality more than +40%. Right panel, outside the frontier (one task, 373 consultants): one hatched bar below zero at −19 percentage points for correct solutions (about 84% correct without AI vs. about 60–70% with AI). Source: Dell'Acqua et al. (2023).

**Figure 1.7 — Jagged Frontier Map.** The horizontal axis orders tasks by how hard they look to a person; the vertical axis shows how hard they are for the AI tool. A jagged line rises unevenly from left to right; the shaded area below it is inside the frontier. Filled numbered circles (usually inside): 1 rewrite a paragraph in another tone, 3 summarize an article you paste in, 5 generate many ideas quickly. Hollow numbered circles (usually outside): 2 exact quotes with page numbers from a book not given, 4 exact letter counts in a long text without a code or calculator feature, 6 facts about a small local organization without web search. A note says the examples are illustrative as of October 2026 and readers should test their own tasks.

**Figure 1.16 — Staggered rollout.** A grid with three rows (wave 1, wave 2, wave 3 of agents) and eight columns (months 1–8). Cells marked "AI" begin in month 3 for wave 1, month 5 for wave 2 and month 7 for wave 3. A note explains that in month 5, wave 2 has just received the assistant while wave 3 has not, so comparing their changes in the same weeks helps separate the tool's effect, if the groups were on similar paths before. The figure is schematic.

**Figure 1.3 — Two answers (full transcript).** Prompt to both: "What are three good study strategies for a chemistry exam?" Both models were asked to answer a university student in at most 120 words, in plain text. Collected October 17, 2026.

*Answer A, Claude Haiku 5.5:* Practice problems by hand instead of just rereading notes. Chemistry is mostly applied skill, so work through stoichiometry, equilibrium, and gas law problems without looking at the solutions first. Then check your answers and redo the ones you missed. Make a one-page sheet of key equations, units, and constants, and review it often. Also explain each reaction type or periodic trend in your own words. Where you can't, you've found a gap to study. Do past exams or old quizzes under timed conditions, showing every step and unit. This builds speed and gets you used to exam pressure. Afterward, label each mistake as a concept error or a calculation slip, since they need different fixes.

*Answer B, Claude Sonnet 5.5:* 1. Practice with problems, not just rereading. Work through past exams, textbook problems, and problem sets without looking at the solution first, then check where you went wrong. Chemistry is a skill, so stoichiometry, equilibrium, and mechanisms only stick when you do them yourself. 2. Use active recall and spaced repetition. Make flashcards for reactions, definitions, periodic trends, and formulas. Quiz yourself from memory, and review a little each day instead of cramming the night before. 3. Understand the concepts behind the formulas. Be able to explain why something happens, such as why bond polarity affects solubility. Study with a friend and teach topics to each other. If you can explain it simply, you know it. Also write out a one-page summary of key equations and concepts from memory.
