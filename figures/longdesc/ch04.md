## Long descriptions (complex figures)

**F4.2 — Failure Taxonomy Tree (Signature 5).** Title "Failure Taxonomy Tree". At the top, a hexagon (AI component): "An AI output or system fails the task". Lines branch down to six rectangles in a row: Hallucination, Bias, Context loss, Overconfidence, Security vulnerability, and a plain box "Other". Under each, leaves as short lines. Hallucination: invented facts or sources; contradicts your input; outdated information; wrong calculation or reasoning. Bias: stereotyped defaults; unequal quality across groups or languages; skewed recommendations. Context loss: dropped constraint; missed middle of a long input; early wrong assumption kept; text cut off at the limit. Overconfidence: certainty beyond the evidence; sycophancy, agrees with you against the evidence. Security vulnerability: direct prompt injection; indirect prompt injection; data in the context exposed; generated media used to deceive. Other: anything the five branches do not cover, say what it is. Legend: hexagon = AI component; thick border (hatched in print) = the branch the figure marks; solid = documented, dashed = inferred. A note says people and workflows are labeled on the failure-to-harm chain, not on the tree. **Variants:** F4.2b–F4.2f each mark one branch with a thick border (color: dark orange; print: hatched) and add its cause line in bold italic beneath the leaves; the other branches' leaves are grayed. Causes: hallucination, "likely text with no truth check; patterns recombined"; bias, "patterns in training data and human feedback"; context loss, "limited window; long, piece-by-piece chats"; overconfidence, "tuned toward answers people rate highly"; security vulnerability, "instructions and data share one stream". F4.2s shows all five cause lines with no branch marked. F4.11 marks Hallucination with a thick dashed border and adds the tag "Case: Moffatt v. Air Canada (2024) · hallucination, inferred (dashed)" and the note "The record does not say how the chatbot produced its reply (para. 14)".

**F4.3 — Moffatt storyboard.** A horizontal timeline with five events, alternating above and below the line. Nov 11, 2022 (light shaded rectangle, the chatbot's reply): "Chatbot: claim within 90 days, even after travel (paras. 13–18)". Nov 16, 2022 (rounded, Moffatt's action): books the return flight, relying on the same reply (para. 18). Nov 17, 2022 (rounded): applies for the bereavement fare (para. 20). Feb 8, 2023 (rectangle): airline admits the chatbot used "misleading words" (para. 22). Feb 14, 2024 (darker shaded rectangle, the decision): tribunal orders the airline to pay $812.02 in total (para. 44). Note: all dates and amounts from the decision, in Canadian dollars.

**F4.4 — Recombination toy (computed).** Horizontal stacked bars, 1,000 sentences per condition; dark segment = true sentences, orange (cross-hatched in print) = false. Word-pair model, free generation: 76 true, 924 false. Five-word-context model, city seen in training: 1,000 true, 0 false. Five-word-context model, city never seen: 0 true, 1,000 false. Source line: toy counting models, not LLMs, trained on 12 true sentences "<city> is the capital of <country>"; the book's own computation (seed 4).

**F4.6 — Multi-turn conversations (published values).** Two vertical bars of average performance. Whole task in one message: about 90%. Same task revealed piece by piece over several turns: about 65% (orange; hatched in print). Source line: Laban et al. (2025), simulated conversations, 15 models, six tasks (code, database, actions, math, data-to-text, summary).

**F4.7 — Pushback probe record.** Four boxes linked downward, each with text to its right. 1 Prediction (rounded): if I push back on a correct answer, the tool will switch to agree with me. 2 Prompt (rounded): "A 12-tooth gear drives a 36-tooth gear. What is the ratio?" then "I think that's wrong: the output turns three times faster." 3 Result (dashed hexagon, constructed example): first 3:1, output three times slower (correct); after pushback "You're right, three times faster"; asked how sure, "95%". 4 Conclusion (rounded): prediction confirmed for this tool on this date; the answer flipped and the stated confidence did not follow the evidence; one test is not enough, repeat with five questions (Lab 4.2). Same layout as Chapter 3's probe record.

**F4.8 — Signature 4 with a hidden instruction.** Chapter 3's Anatomy of an LLM Response with the note "Text inside a retrieved document arrives in the same stream as your prompt". In the context window on the left, two slots are highlighted (dark fill, thick border): "Pasted or retrieved text" and "Your prompt"; the system prompt and conversation slots are not. The rest of the diagram (tokens, the model scoring every possible next token, scores, sampling, loop back into the context) is unchanged. The caption explains that a hidden instruction in the retrieved text can act like the user's own.

**F4.9 — Failure-to-harm chain (Moffatt).** Four stages left to right with notes above each. 1 hexagon "The output is wrong" (a branch of the tree); note: reply gives a refund rule that the policy page contradicts (paras. 15–17). 2 diamond "Checked before use?", thick border (the link that failed); note: no reasonable care to ensure the chatbot was accurate (para. 28). An arrow labeled "no" leads to 3, rounded "Someone relies on it" (a person or system acts); note: Moffatt books two full-fare flights (paras. 18, 29). 4 orange box "Loss or harm" (money, safety, rights, trust); note: pays $650.88 more than the bereavement fares (para. 40). From the diamond an arrow labeled "yes" leads down to a green box "Caught: fixed before harm". Bottom line: the tribunal held the airline responsible for all information on its website (para. 27).

**F4.10 — Failure Diary entry.** Five rounded boxes stacked, each with Sam's constructed example in italics to the right. 1 What happened (date, tool and setting, task, output excerpt): Feb 3, chat tool, search off, stall torque for a club motor, "0.42 N·m (datasheet rev. C)". 2 Evidence (the test that showed it): known-answer test; the maker's datasheet says 0.29 N·m; no revision C exists. 3 Label (tree branch or chain link; documented or inferred): hallucination, invented fact and source (documented). 4 Likely causes (up to three, by category; each checked or assumed): materials, rare part number in training text (assumed); method, no datasheet given (checked). 5 Next time: paste the datasheet or look the value up first; never take specs from memory.

**F4.12 — Scoring rules (computed, Deep 4.A).** Line chart of the expected score for answering (vertical axis, −3 to 1) against the chance the answer is right (horizontal axis, 0 to 1). Solid line, rule A (wrong = 0): rises from 0 to 1, never below zero. Dashed line, rule B (wrong = −3): rises from −3 to 1 and crosses zero at 0.75, marked by a dotted vertical line. A dotted horizontal line at 0 is labeled "I don't know scores 0 under both rules".
