## Long descriptions

**F22.2 (f22-02-storyboard).** Title: IMO 2025: the same score, three kinds of check. Three columns, each a rounded box naming who checked, an arrow down to a hexagon naming the AI result, and a status tag. Column 1: graded by the competition; Gemini Deep Think, 35 of 42, natural language, 4.5 hours; measured: IMO coordinators (as DeepMind's post states). Column 2: graded by people the developer chose; OpenAI experimental model, 35 of 42; reported: three former medalists (Reuters). Column 3: checked by a proof program; Seed Prover (30 of 42, IMO-certified) and Aristotle (gold-level); measured: the Lean proof checker, after problems were formalized. Below, a light box: what no check covered: how much computing was used, or whether people helped (the IMO president, reported by AFP). Bottom rounded box: human context (AFP): 641 students; about 10% won gold; five scored 42 of 42.

**F22.3 (f22-03-timeline), the Capability Timeline signature.** Nine boxes in date order along a horizontal axis (not to scale), alternating above and below, each joined to a dot on the axis. Each box gives a date, a short claim and a tag. In order: Jul 2025, IMO gold-level score from an experimental model, reported: the developer; Jul 2025, IMO gold-level score in natural language, measured: IMO coordinators; Sep 2025, reasoning trained with answer-based rewards, measured: Nature peer review; Apr 2026, agents: 50% horizon above 16 hours, measured: METR (flagged unreliable); Apr 2026, data-center electricity up 17% in 2025, measured: IEA estimate; May 2026, model disproves a 1946 geometry conjecture, reported: the developer; May 2026, open-weight models about four months behind, measured: Epoch AI; Jul 2026, two systems claim 42 of 42 at IMO 2026, reported: the developers (AFP); a thick bar marked now (October 2026); by 2030, data-center electricity doubles, forecast: IEA (light fill). Five of nine are measured, three reported, one forecast. Legend: filled corner square = measured; open square = reported; arrow and light fill = forecast; dashed outline = the book's inference; folded corner = aged.

**F22.4 (f22-04-evidence-status).** Top row: 1. As-of date (when it was made or measured) → 2. Evidence status (a diamond) → 3. Who could check it (the outside source that could). From the diamond, arrows to four boxes: measured (checked by an outside source; method you can read); reported (stated by the developer, or an outlet citing it); forecast (about the future, whoever says it; light fill); the book's inference (dashed). Below, a crosswalk: case tier asks whether a fact is in the primary record (confirmed, reported, inferred); evidence status asks who checked the capability. Example: DeepMind's post is the confirmed record of what DeepMind says; the score it reports was checked by the IMO's coordinators, so it is measured.

**F22.8 (f22-08-horizons).** Log-scale scatter chart. Horizontal axis: model release date, 2023 to mid-2026. Vertical axis: task length for skilled people, from 1 minute to 16 hours, each step a multiple. Filled markers: 50% horizons; open markers: 80% horizons; circle = OpenAI, square = Anthropic, triangle = Google. 50% horizons rise from about 4 minutes (GPT-4, March 2023) to about 6 hours (Gemini 3.1 Pro, February 2026) and 12 hours (Claude Opus 4.6, February 2026), with one point near 17 hours (Claude Mythos Preview, early, April 2026) above a line at 16 hours labeled as METR's limit of reliable estimates. 80% horizons sit several times lower, about one to three hours for the newest models. A dotted line labeled trend, not data, is this book's fit to METR's best results: doubling about every 129 days. Data table in Lab 22.3 and data/ch22_metr.csv.

**F22.11 (f22-11-open-closed).** Two bar panels. Left (AI Index 2025, Chatbot Arena): score gap between the best open-weight and best closed model, 8% in January 2024 and 1.7% in February 2025. Right (Epoch AI, Capabilities Index): months the best open model trails, 3.5 months for 2023 to October 2025 with a 90% range of 1.1 to 5.3, and about 4 months for January to May 2026.

**F22.12 (f22-12-compute).** Log-scale scatter of 478 notable AI models' training compute (FLOP) by publication date, 2010–2026, rising from about 10^14 to about 10^27. A dotted line labeled trend, not data: this book's fit, about 4.3 times a year. Source line: Epoch AI, CC BY 4.0; Epoch's own summary 4–5 times a year.

**F22.14 (f22-14-electricity).** Two bar panels. World (IEA, 2025 edition): 415 TWh in 2024 (filled, estimate) and 945 TWh forecast for 2030 (light box labeled forecast). United States (LBNL, 2024): 176 TWh in 2023 (filled) and a range of 325–580 TWh forecast for 2028 (light floating box). Note: the IEA's April 2026 update reports 17% growth in 2025 and a doubling by 2030, with no TWh in its release.

**F22.15 (f22-15-boundary).** Four stacked boxes widening upward: active AI chips (narrowest; labeled 0.10 Wh, Google's figure counting only these, 2025); plus host computer and memory; plus idle capacity kept ready; plus data-center overhead, cooling and power (widest; labeled 0.24 Wh, Google's full-stack figure, 2025). Notes: both figures are the developer's (reported) and its blog says they were not verified by an independent third party; an outside estimate for another provider's typical query is about 0.3 Wh (Epoch AI, 2025).

**F22.17 (f22-17-timeline-aged), signature reuse.** Six claims, 2014 to 2030, in date order. Sep 2014: machines that talk with users (Siri; Watson), reported: a 2014 overview, aged (thick: re-checked). Mar 2025: agents' 50% horizon about 50 minutes, measured: METR (first version), aged (thick). Oct 2025: 'ten open problems solved' post, reported: a developer's executive, aged. Jan 2026: Erdős problem 728 solved, proof checked in Lean, measured: proof checker and write-up. Sep 2026: METR stops updating its horizon page, measured: METR (thick). Thick bar: now. By 2030: data-center electricity doubles, forecast: IEA (2026 edition). Three of six marked aged with a folded corner.

**F22.19 (f22-19-chain-erdos), chain reuse.** The failure-to-harm chain: 1. the output is wrong (note: a literature search found existing papers; the output was useful); 2. checked before use? (highlighted; note: posted as 'solved' before anyone checked the problems' status, reported); 3. someone relies on it (readers and other researchers relied on the post, reported); 4. loss or harm (retraction and public correction; no lasting harm reported). Tag: tree none; the failure was a claim published before its check (link 2).
