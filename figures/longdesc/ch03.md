## Long descriptions (complex figures)

**F3.3 — Anatomy of an LLM Response (Signature 4), complete version; F3.3a–d are the same drawing with one part highlighted.** Title: "Anatomy of an LLM response". On the left, a tall shaded panel titled "Context window: everything the model receives this turn" holds four stacked boxes: System prompt (set by the tool's maker), Conversation so far (earlier prompts and responses), Pasted or retrieved text (documents, search results), and Your prompt (the newest message; a rounded box, meaning human input). Under them: "+ each token the model adds". An arrow leads right to box 1, a rectangle, "1. Text becomes tokens", showing the prompt split as A | good | name | for | a | coffee | shop | near | campus | is. An arrow leads right to step 2, a hexagon (AI component), "2. The model scores every possible next token", with the note "settings fixed after training". An arrow leads down to a box "Scores for the next token", with horizontal bars: The 30%, Bean 16%, Brew 12%, Campus 9%, Common 6%, then "… thousands more, most near 0%". An arrow leads left to step 3, a dashed rectangle (chance is involved), "3. Pick one token (sampling)", with "the temperature option sets how often less likely tokens win". An arrow leads from step 3 back into the context window, labeled "4. Add the token to the context; repeat until the response is finished". The four numbered steps match section 3.2. Footnotes: scores are illustrative; token pieces come from a newer OpenAI tokenizer (o200k_base). In the variants, the highlighted part has a dark fill or a thick border (dark band in print): F3.3a step 1; F3.3b step 2, the scores box and step 3; F3.3c the whole context-window panel; F3.3d only the "Pasted or retrieved text" box.

**F3.2 — Tokens across languages (computed).** Horizontal paired bars for nine languages, showing how many tokens the full text of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights needs relative to English (English = 1.0×). First bar of each pair: newer tokenizer (o200k_base); second, lighter (hatched in print): older tokenizer (cl100k_base). English 1.0× and 1.0×; Spanish 1.2× and 1.5×; Turkish 1.5× and 2.0×; Russian 1.4× and 2.6×; Chinese (simplified) 1.2× and 1.7×; Arabic 1.2× and 2.6×; Vietnamese 1.5× and 2.7×; Hindi 1.7× and 5.6×; Tamil 2.5× and 9.6×. A dotted vertical line marks 1.0×. Source: computed by the book in October 2026 with tiktoken 0.8.0 on the Unicode UDHR collection (data/ch03_tokens.csv).

**F3.4 — Temperature (illustrative).** Paired vertical bars for eight options for the next token after "A good name for a coffee shop near campus is", at a low temperature (0.5, dark bars) and a high temperature (1.5, light or hatched bars). The 61% vs. 18%; Bean 17% vs. 12%; Brew 10% vs. 10%; Campus 6% vs. 8%; Common 2% vs. 6%; Java 2% vs. 6%; Grounds 1% vs. 5%; 20 other tokens combined 1% vs. 35%. The scores were chosen by the book; they are not output from a real model.

**F3.5 — Training, then inference.** Top row, "Training (before you use the tool): the settings change": three boxes joined by arrows: Pretraining (predict the next token in huge amounts of text; self-supervised), Instruction tuning (examples of prompts and good responses), and Learning from human feedback (a rounded box: people rank responses; a scoring model is trained on them). A diagonal arrow, labeled "settings frozen when training ends", leads down to the middle row, "Inference (each time you use it): the settings only get used": Your prompt (rounded) → The model (hexagon; settings fixed, frozen) → Response (dashed rectangle; likely text; may be out of date). Bottom: a timeline. A solid segment is labeled "Text written before the cutoff: can shape the settings"; a dashed vertical line marks "Knowledge cutoff"; a dashed segment to the right, ending in an arrow toward "time", is labeled "Later events: not in the settings".

**F3.6 — Lost in the middle (published values).** Line chart. Horizontal axis: position of the document that contains the answer among 20 retrieved documents (1, 5, 10, 15, 20). Vertical axis: questions answered correctly (40% to 80%). GPT-3.5-Turbo (0613), solid line with filled circles: 75.8%, 57.2%, 53.8%, 55.4%, 63.2%. Claude-1.3, dashed line with hollow squares: 59.9%, 55.9%, 56.8%, 57.2%, 60.1%. Dotted horizontal lines show each model with no documents (closed book): 56.1% for GPT-3.5-Turbo and 48.3% for Claude-1.3. An arrow points to the GPT-3.5-Turbo value at position 10, "Answer in the middle: lowest accuracy". Source: Liu et al. (2024; arXiv:2307.03172v3, Appendix G.2, Table 6) and Table 1.

**F3.8 — Probe record.** Four boxes stacked top to bottom and joined by arrows, each followed by text. 1 Prediction (rounded): the model will miscount the letter r in "strawberry" because it receives tokens, not letters. 2 Prompt (rounded): "How many times does the letter r appear in the word strawberry? Answer with a number." 3 Result (dashed hexagon, the model's output): constructed example, "3", correct; asked to spell the word letter by letter first, also 3. 4 Conclusion (rounded): the prediction was wrong for this tool on this date; one correct answer does not show the tool counts letters reliably; test five more words. Footer: also record the tool, the model name if shown, the options used (search on or off) and the date. The same record is given in words in section 3.6. Chapter 4 reuses the drawing (`probe_record(rows=...)`) with other content.

**F3.9 — Chapter 3 terms linked to Chapter 2 terms.** Two columns of rounded boxes: Chapter 3 terms on the left (white), Chapter 2 terms on the right (shaded). Four arrows, each read as a sentence: Pretraining "is a kind of" Self-supervised learning; Next-token prediction "uses the real next token as its" Label; Knowledge cutoff "marks the end of the" Training data; Context window "is rebuilt at every step of" Inference.

**F3.10 — Attention (illustration).** Title: "Attention lets each token draw on the tokens before it". Two copies of the sentence "The trophy doesn't fit in the brown suitcase because it's too …" (Levesque et al., 2012), ending "big." in the first and "small." in the second. Arrows start at the last word and point back, because each token can draw only on the tokens before it. In the first, a thick arrow above the words runs from "big" back to "trophy" (strong link) and a thin dashed arrow below runs to "suitcase" (weak link). In the second, the thick arrow runs from "small" to "suitcase" and the dashed arrow to "trophy". Not measured values.

**F3.13 — Reversal curse, real celebrities (published values).** Two horizontal bars. "Celebrity → parent (mother and father questions)": 79% correct. "Parent → celebrity ('Name a child of [parent]')": 33% correct, counting a success if any of ten tries named the celebrity. Model: GPT-4, 2023. Source: Berglund et al. (2024), section 2.2; 1,573 celebrity–parent pairs.
