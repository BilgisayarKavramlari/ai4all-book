## Long descriptions (complex figures)

**F5.3 — The Delegation Ladder (Signature 7).** A vertical ladder with two rails. A thick arrow on the left points upward, labeled at the top "Harder to see and check what the AI contributed before it takes effect" and at the bottom "Easier to check." The bottom of the ladder is a wide rounded box, the floor: "No AI: you do this part yourself — always an option." Above it are three rungs, each made of a rounded box (what you do) and a hexagon (what the AI does). Rung 1, Automation: you define the task and the AI carries it out; the AI produces an output you can check before you use it. Rung 2, Augmentation: you and the AI shape the task together, turn by turn; the AI suggests, drafts and questions inside your thinking. Rung 3, Agency: you configure AI to carry out future tasks for you, often for others; the AI acts later, without you checking each output. A note under the ladder says the ladder, its floor and its axis are this book's arrangement of the AI Fluency framework's three modalities (Dakan & Feller, 2025), that the framework ranks none of them, that one task often mixes several, and that a higher rung is not a better use. The variant F5.3b adds Lucía's parts under each rung: floor, choose what first-year students need and the final check; automation, draft the handout text; augmentation, why the example budget confuses readers; agency, a study-group FAQ chatbot, not now.

**F5.5 — Klarna storyboard.** Three dated panels joined by arrows. February 2024, confirmed (press release): 2.3 million chats in month one, two-thirds of chats, work equal to 700 full-time staff, satisfaction about the same as with people, 25% fewer repeat questions; a person available on request. May 2025, reported (Bloomberg interview, via other outlets; thick border): the CEO said cost had been too dominant and quality was lower; customers must always be able to reach a human. November 2025, reported (CX Dive): the CEO said the assistant did the work of more than 853 full-time staff and had saved about $60 million. Footer: every figure is the company's own statement, none an independent measurement; sources Klarna (2024), Fisher (2025), Shibu (2025), Doerer (2025).

**F5.6 — Anatomy of a Prompt (Signature 6).** A large shaded box labeled "Your prompt" contains six rounded boxes. Top row: Role (voice and perspective), Context (what this task needs), In-context examples (sample input with the output you want). Middle, wide and with a thick border: Task (the verb and what it acts on: always needed). Bottom row: Constraints (limits and must-nots), Output format (a shape you can check). An arrow leads from the prompt box to a hexagon, "The AI tool — receives only what is written," and from there down to a rectangle, "Output — scored against your criteria." A legend says rounded boxes are parts you write, the thick border marks the part always needed, and the hexagon is an AI component.

**F5.7 — Lucía's first and final prompts.** Left, a shaded box "v1 (first prompt)": Write a one-page handout for first-year students on how to make a monthly budget. Include one worked example as a table. Use plain language. Under it, in italics: Missing: the figures that were in her notes. Right, a larger box "Final prompt, v5 (parts labeled)" with four labeled rows: Task (thick): write a one-page handout for first-year students on how to make a monthly budget. Context (highlighted): use only these monthly figures: income $1,150; rent $650; groceries $280; phone $45; transport $60. Constraints: plain language; refer to tools in general terms, no product, bank or app; explain every money term in everyday words the first time; if costs are higher than income, say so and show the gap. Output format: one worked example as a table. Notes: no role and no in-context examples, because this task did not need them; v2 to v5 each added one line.

**F5.8 — The iteration loop.** Seven rectangles arranged in a ring with arrows clockwise: 1 Diagnose the weak output; 2 Trace it to a prompt part (or the frame); 3 Change one thing; 4 Predict the effect; 5 Rerun in a fresh chat, twice; 6 Score against the criteria; 7 Log it, keep or revert; then back to 1. In the center, a dashed rounded box, "Back to Frame — when the trace ends at the task itself," is reached by a dashed arrow from step 2. A note says the loop is the Prompt ↔ Verify part of the AI Work Loop and that dashed means taken only when needed.

**F5.9 — A why-chain.** Five boxes stacked vertically and joined by downward arrows. Top, a thick-bordered risk box: "Weak output — the example budget's total is wrong; two numbers were invented" (observed in 2 runs). Then three "Why?" boxes, each marked "checked": no figures were in the prompt, so the output filled in plausible ones; the frame card said to use the association's figures, but they were never pasted; they were in Lucía's notes, not in the prompt. Bottom, a rounded box: "Fix (v2) — add the figures as context; predict: every number matches." To the right, a dashed box reached by a dashed arrow from the top box: "Or is the model just bad at sums? — assumed, not checked yet," with the note "v2 tests it." Legend: solid = a link you checked; dashed = a link you only assumed.

**F5.10 — Wording and the definition of success (data chart).** Grouped bar chart of published values (Meincke et al., 2025a, Supplementary Table 2): GPT-4o (gpt-4o-2024-08-06), 198 GPQA Diamond questions, each asked 100 times per prompt at temperature 0. Two prompts that differ only in their opening words: "Please answer the following question." and "I order you to answer the following question." The vertical axis is percentage points above random guessing (0 = 25% of questions); lines are 95% intervals. Left group, strict standard (right in all 100 of 100 runs): Please −1.5, I order you −3.0; both intervals include zero. Right group, majority standard (right in 51 or more runs): Please +20.2, I order you +23.7; both intervals are above zero and overlap. Finding: the two wordings did not differ, and whether either "worked" depended on the standard.

**F5.11 — Test grid.** A grid with three rows (test inputs: Typical, the association's figures; Different, income that changes month to month; Edge, costs higher than income) and two columns (Run 1, Run 2). Four cells read "All 5 met" (shaded). Different/Run 2 reads "Format partly met: no table" (light). Edge/Run 2 reads "Numbers not met: invented income line" (hatched, thick border). Below, a rounded box: "Verdict: not yet. It fails on the edge input, so v5 adds a rule for budgets that do not balance."

**F5.12 — Peer swap test.** A left-to-right flow: "Your final prompt — no explanation added" (rounded) → "A classmate runs it — fresh, temporary chat" (rounded) → "Output" (hexagon) → "Scored against your criteria" (rounded) → a diamond, "A gap?" If yes, an arrow leads left to "Write what was in your head into the prompt; rerun." If no, an arrow leads down to a shaded box, "Evidence the prompt carries its own context."

**F5.13 — Iteration log template.** A table with six columns: Version, Part changed, Prediction, Run 1, Run 2, Keep? Row v2: context, pasted the figures; prediction every number matches the figures; run 1 5 of 5 met; run 2 4 of 5, names a budgeting app; keep. Row v3: constraint, tools in general terms, no product, bank or app; prediction no product named, nothing else changes; run 1 5 of 5 met; run 2 4 met, 1 partly, a money term unexplained; keep. Row Frame: C3 rewritten after the peer swap; no new run, the v3 outputs rescored; C3 partly met in both; keep. Row v4: empty, for the reader. Notes: one change per row, a prediction before the run, scores tied to your criteria; constructed example.

**F5.14 — Delegation data (data chart).** Two horizontal bars of published values (Appel et al., 2025), August 2025, same month and classification method: chat conversations on Claude.ai, 49% showing automation patterns; first-party API (business) traffic, 77%. The source line says these are the provider's own definitions, not the AI Fluency modalities.
