## Long descriptions

**F8.1.** Chapter map in three columns: checking claims (8.1–8.3), before and after (8.4, 8.5, and Deep 8.A–8.B, dashed as optional), practice (8.6 Lab 8.1 and A5, labs 8.2–8.3, Case Study). Below, the AI Work Loop strip with Verify highlighted and Decide dashed.

**F8.2.** A horizontal timeline with six events, alternating above and below the line. March 1, 2023 (hexagon, AI-produced text): the filing cites cases ChatGPT made up (paras. 5, 36). March 15: Avianca's lawyers cannot find several cases (para. 7). April 11–12: the court orders copies (para. 13). April 25 (hatched in print, dark orange in color): an affidavit attaches excerpts of the fake decisions (paras. 20–22). May 25 (rounded): the lawyers begin to admit what happened (p. 2). June 22, 2023 (shaded): sanctions of $5,000 and letters to the client and judges. A note says the claim itself was dismissed as filed too late (ECF 55).

**F8.3.** A hexagon holds a constructed AI paragraph about the CPA exam. Below it, a six-row table lists the claims with type and stakes: 1, the exam has four sections AUD, BEC, FAR and REG (fact, high); 2, each section lasts four hours (number, medium); 3, a score of 75 passes each section (number, high); 4, all four must be passed within 18 months (number, high); 5, source: the official exam blueprint (citation, medium); 6, most candidates should start with the hardest section (judgment, not checkable; dashed row; low).

**F8.4.** Signature 8. From top to bottom: a hexagon labeled An AI output; a wide band, Claims (split the output: one statement you might rely on per claim); an arrow labeled every claim you rely on; a narrower band, 1 Independent check (a source no AI tool produced, with an author or publisher you can name, or redo the calculation yourself); an arrow labeled stakes not yet met; a narrower band, 2 Primary source (the original record: study, dataset, law, court record or official page); another arrow; the narrowest band, rounded because it is a person, 3 Expert (a person with the knowledge and access to judge it; ask for reasons). From the right edge of each of the three check bands, an arrow runs to a vertical side rail labeled Stop when the evidence matches the stakes. The rail leads down to a Verdict box listing supported, partly supported, unsupported, contradicted, cannot verify. A dashed arrow leads from Verdict to a dashed box, Remaining uncertainty: what you could not check, and why.

**F8.4d.** The Verification Funnel with the independent-check band and the verdict highlighted, tagged Case: Varghese v. China Southern Airlines, where the funnel would have stopped it. Notes beside the rail: at stage 1, a free case-law site, not found (para. 12); at stage 2, citation and docket belong to other cases (para. 28); at stage 3, the court's clerk: no such case (para. 26). The verdict box reads contradicted.

**F8.4s.** The complete Verification Funnel with the verdict box highlighted and the note: every claim you rely on gets at least the independent check. The verdict box lists the five-point scale.

**F8.5.** Three columns. Column 1, It does not exist; the check: search an index no AI tool produced; in Mata, the Eleventh Circuit's clerk confirmed no such case as Varghese (para. 26). Column 2, It points to another work; the check: match every detail to the record; in Mata, its docket number and reporter citation belong to two other cases (para. 28). Column 3, It does not say it; the check: open the source and find the passage; in Mata, real decisions cited inside it do not contain the quoted words (para. 29g).

**F8.6.** Grouped bar chart from Walters and Wilder (2023). For GPT-3.5, 55% of references did not exist and 43% of the real references had substantive errors. For GPT-4, 18% did not exist and 24% of the real ones had substantive errors. Source note: short literature reviews on 42 topics.

**F8.7.** A rounded box holds the question How many sections does the CPA exam have? Arrows lead to three hexagons. Run 1 and Run 2 each answer four sections, AUD, BEC, FAR, REG, 95% sure. Run 3, drawn with a thick border, answers three core sections plus one discipline section. Below runs 1 and 2, a dashed box: runs 1 and 2 agree, and both are outdated; agreement is not proof. Below run 3, a thick box: run 3 disagrees, a warning sign, check this claim first. Constructed example.

**F8.9.** Constructed calibration chart. The x-axis shows three stated-confidence groups with their mean confidence: 50–69% (mean 61%), 70–89% (mean 80%), 90–100% (mean 94%). Bars show the share correct: 2 of 5 (40%), 5 of 10 (50%), 9 of 15 (60%). A dashed diagonal marks perfect calibration; each bar sits below it, and an arrow runs from the bar's top up to a dot at the group's mean stated confidence, showing gaps of about 21, 30 and 34 points.

**F8.11.** The Failure Taxonomy Tree with two branches highlighted by thick, solid borders (documented): hallucination, whose leaves include invented facts or sources, and overconfidence, whose leaves include certainty beyond the evidence. Tag: Case Mata v. Avianca (S.D.N.Y. 2023), hallucination plus overconfidence, both documented. Note: invented cases (para. 36); asked if they were real, the replies said yes (para. 45).

**F8.12.** The failure-to-harm chain. Link 1, hexagon, the output is wrong: six decisions that do not exist (para. 36). Link 2, diamond with a thick border, checked before use? Note: not checked outside the tool; the signer read no authorities (paras. 6, 12). An arrow labeled no leads to link 3, rounded, someone relies on it: filed with the court and defended after orders. Link 4, loss or harm: $5,000 penalty, letters, time and trust lost. A yes branch from link 2 leads to caught, fixed before harm. Tag: the court held the lawyers and their firm responsible for the filing.

**F8.13.** Eight rounded field boxes, each with its description: claim; stakes; before checking (your confidence and the tool's stated confidence or signal); stage reached; source (for citations: exists? matches? says it?); verdict (shaded, with the five-point scale); remaining uncertainty (dashed); corrected statement.

**F8.14.** Two constructed reference checkers on 20 references, 5 of them fake. Checker A flags 8 and catches 4 of the 5 fakes: precision 4 of 8, 50%; recall 4 of 5, 80%. Checker B flags all 20 and catches all 5: precision 5 of 20, 25%; recall 100%.
