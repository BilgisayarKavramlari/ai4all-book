## Long descriptions (complex figures)

### F20.6 (f20-06-claim-anatomy)

Concept diagram. In the center, the claim "170 million new jobs by 2030". Seven question boxes point to it. Top: Who said it? the WEF, from employers' 2024 answers. Whom did they ask? over 1,000 employers, not workers. When? survey May–September 2024. What caused it? all trends combined, not AI alone. Bottom: What was measured? expectations and plans, not outcomes. Forecast or count? an estimate for 2025–2030. Gross or net? gross; 92 million displaced, net 78 million. Source: WEF (2025), pp. 5, 25, 97.

### F20.3 (f20-03-nace-gap)

Dot chart (dumbbell) of eight NACE competencies, sorted by gap. For each, a filled circle shows the percentage of students rating recent graduates "very" or "extremely" proficient and an open square the percentage of employers: leadership 66.0 vs 31.0 (+35.0 points); professionalism 79.7 vs 50.3 (+29.4); critical thinking 80.8 vs 55.9 (+24.9); communication 78.1 vs 53.5 (+24.6); career and self-development 61.5 vs 43.2 (+18.3); equity and inclusion 79.5 vs 63.3 (+16.2); teamwork 83.5 vs 81.5 (+2.0); technology 60.5 vs 72.0 (−11.5, employers higher). Source line: Gray (2025), NACE 2024 Student Survey (20,482 students; group shown not stated) and Job Outlook 2025 (237 employers); different people, not a matched comparison.

### F20.4 (f20-04-lo-nace-map)

Grid with eight NACE competencies as rows and LO1 to LO7 as columns. Cells name artifacts that can give evidence: career and self-development LO4 A4, LO7 A1 and A14; communication LO2 A3, LO4 A7, LO6 A13, LO7 A14; critical thinking LO1 A2, LO2 A3, LO3 A5, A6, A9; equity and inclusion LO5 A12; leadership LO5 A11, LO6 A13; professionalism LO4 A7, LO5 A12; teamwork LO2 A3 (peer swap), LO6 A13 (peer test); technology LO1 A2, LO2 A10, LO5 A10, A12. Note: the book's mapping, not NACE's; equity and inclusion under review as of October 2026.

### F20.5 (f20-05-wef-jobs)

Bar chart of the WEF's estimates for 2025–2030, made from employers' expectations and covering all trends, not AI alone: jobs created 170 million (14% of the about 1.18 billion jobs in covered roles), jobs displaced 92 million (8%), net growth 78 million (7%). The displaced bar is hatched in print. Source line: WEF (2025), pp. 5–6, 25, 97, 99; over 1,000 employers surveyed May–September 2024 (a p. 99 note says May–August); the report links about 11 million created and 9 million displaced to AI and information processing.

### F20.7 (f20-07-portfolio-anatomy)

Signature 12, Portfolio Anatomy, nine shapes top to bottom. A rounded box "Career narrative: three claims to one named reader" points down to a rectangle "Evidence map: artifact, outcome, competency, trail". A bracket labeled "each of at least six entries (one of them A13)" spans four rectangles: Artifact (as submitted, then revised); Revision trace (before to after, what changed and why); Verification trail (checks, verdicts, what stays uncertain); AI Use Declaration (the AI's part and your part). Arrows lead to two rounded boxes, Reflection (what changed in your practice since A1) and Code of practice (your commitments, Chapter 21), which both point to a rounded box "Oral defense: 3 + 2 minutes, live", with the note "live, no tool". Legend: rounded = your judgment in your own words; rectangle = a record a reader can check. Highlighted parts (in reuses) are drawn in the dark highlight fill with the label "this section".

### F20.13 (f20-13-privacy-check)

Decision flow. 'An item for your portfolio' leads to the diamond 'Whose information is in it?' Only yours: keep it, but check its data class anyway. Protected (patients', participants' or clients' confidential data): remove it (hatched, thick border). Classmates', an employer's, or a client's work: a second diamond, 'Written permission from its owner?' Yes: keep, permission noted. No: replace with placeholders, or describe the method only.

### F20.18 (f20-18-employer-rules)

Matrix of three employers by three hiring stages. Anthropic (company page, July 2025; solid borders): application materials, your own first draft, AI to refine, never invent experience; take-home tasks, no AI unless the company says it is allowed; interviews, no AI in live interviews unless indicated. Amazon (reported February 2025; dashed = reported): application and take-home, 'no rule reported' (light fill with label); interviews, generative AI may disqualify unless explicitly permitted. Goldman Sachs (reported June 2025; dashed): application and take-home, no rule reported; interviews (HireVue), no outside sources including ChatGPT, same language to all campus applicants. Note: three firms only, not evidence of what employers in general do.
