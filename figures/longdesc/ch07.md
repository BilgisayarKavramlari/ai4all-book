## Long descriptions (complex figures)

**F7.2 — Practice versus exam results (data chart).** Grouped bars show each AI group's difference from the control group, in points on a 100-point normalized grade, with error lines of one standard error. Left group, "Practice, with the tool": GPT Base +13.7 points (+48% relative to the control mean of 28.4); GPT Tutor +36.1 points (+127%). Right group, "Exam right after, without any tool": GPT Base −5.4 points (−17% relative to the control mean of 32.1); GPT Tutor −0.4 points (about −1%, not significant). Source line: Bastani et al. (2025), Table 1. In print, GPT Tutor bars are hatched.

**F7.3 — Session storyboard.** Three columns. Part 1: a rounded box, "Teacher review, same for every class." Part 2, headed "only this differed": three boxes stacked — Control (books and notes, no devices; rounded), GPT Base (hexagon: chat built like ChatGPT, asked to act as a tutor), GPT Tutor (hexagon with a thick border: hints, not answers; teachers' solutions in its instructions). Arrows lead from the review to each of the three and from each to Part 3: a thick-bordered rectangle, "Closed-book exam, same for all; each problem paired with a practice problem." Notes: four 90-minute sessions in fall 2023–24, classrooms randomly assigned, practice and exam both graded; source Bastani et al. (2025).

**F7.4 — Reread versus recall (data chart).** Left panel, "Recall": bars for three groups at two delays. After 5 minutes: read four times 83%, read three times and recall once 78%, read once and recall three times 71%. After one week: 40%, 56% and 61%. Right panel, "How well they expected to remember": predicted recall in a week on a 1–7 scale, 4.8, 4.2 and 4.0 for the same three groups. Source: Roediger and Karpicke (2006), Experiment 2, 180 students, recall tests without feedback.

**F7.7 — The Jagged Frontier Map from the learner's side (Signature 2 reuse).** The Chapter 1 map, unchanged: a jagged line separates "inside the frontier" (shaded, below) from "outside the frontier" (above), with six numbered example tasks, three usually inside and three usually outside, illustrative as of October 2026. Below the map, two boxes. Left, thick border: "Inside the frontier — the tool can do the task for you. If the skill is yours to build, this is where the crutch is easiest." Right, dashed border: "Outside the frontier — the tool may be wrong. Only your own skill catches the error."

**F7.8 — Scaffold versus crutch (new reusable diagram).** Two horizontal lanes, each starting from the same rectangle, "A practice question," and passing through the same hexagon, "Same reply: worked steps and the answer." Top lane, "Crutch: no attempt": a dashed arrow labeled "no attempt" leads straight to the reply, then to a rounded box "You copy it," then to a thick-bordered, hatched rectangle "Output done; you recalled and produced nothing." Bottom lane, "Scaffold: attempt first": the question leads to a dark rounded box "You attempt first," then to the same reply, then to a dark rounded box "You compare, redo and explain it back," then to a rectangle "You recalled and produced: skill built." Bold line below: "Test for every turn: who recalled and produced the answer, you or the tool?" The caption calls it Chapter 1's two paths (Figure 1.13), turn by turn.

**F7.10 — Tutor-mode setup on the Anatomy of a Prompt (Signature 6 reuse).** The Chapter 5 diagram: six rounded boxes inside "Your prompt" (role, context, in-context examples, task, constraints, output format), an arrow to the AI tool hexagon and then to the output. Role, constraints and output format are highlighted (dark fill, thick border). Note: tutor mode changes the role, the constraints and the output format; the task stays the same.

**F7.12 — A4 record sheet.** A table with rows for three keyed questions and columns "Before: confidence, answer," "Right after (closed-book)," and "2+ days later (closed-book)," where each later cell reads "predict ___% → right? Y / N." A line defines calibration gap as predicted score minus actual score. A second table, the turn log, has columns "Turn #," "Code S / C," "Reason: who recalled and produced it?" and "Error found?" Notes: add rows for at least six coded turns; score against your source's key, not the tool's verdict; your score does not affect your grade.

**F7.14 — The Delegation Ladder for a skill you are building (Signature 7 reuse).** The Chapter 5 ladder (floor "No AI", rungs automation, augmentation, agency; axis: harder to see and check what the AI contributed before it takes effect) with augmentation highlighted. Notes under the rungs: augmentation, "Tutor mode: you attempt; the tool questions and hints"; automation, "Answer mode on a skill you are building: the crutch"; floor, "Closed-book checks: the skill must show without the tool."
