## Long descriptions (complex figures)

**F18.2 (f18-02-storyboard).** Eight boxes in two rows. Top row: Ross asks to license Westlaw, refused because it was a competitor; training memos written using Westlaw headnotes; the search tool trained, returning passages of court decisions; Thomson Reuters sues, 2020. A curved arrow leads to the second row: Ross shuts down, December 2020 announcement (dashed: reported by a named outlet); 2023 ruling, fair use left for a jury; February 11, 2025, not fair use (appeal allowed April 2025); September 29, 2026, Third Circuit affirms (dark, thick border: the ruling in force as of October 2026). Legend: solid = court record; dashed = reported.

**F18.3 (f18-03-regulation-map).** The Regulation Map. A center box, "Your AI use and the people it affects." Above it, under "Law, by jurisdiction," five boxes: EU law (AI Act and other EU law); U.S. federal law (copyright, consumer, civil rights); U.S. executive (orders and policy; changes often); U.S. states (for example, Texas TRAIGA); cities (for example, NYC Local Law 144). Below it, under "What you agree to": terms and licenses, and contracts; and, under "Your organization's rules," a rounded box: your organization's policy (course, university, employer). Every box has an arrow to the center meaning "applies to." Note: as of October 2026; not legal advice; the boxes are kinds of rules, not a ranking.

**F18.4 (f18-04-map-hana), F18.13 (f18-14-map-us), F18.18 (f18-19-map-fakes).** The same map with some boxes dark and thick (they apply to the use shown) and a short note in each. Hana's menu job: U.S. federal law "copyright: who owns the art?", contracts "Hana's AI clause", organization "Hana's rule: tell the client first"; note: no Texas AI-specific rule found for this job (this book's reading, as of October 2026). U.S. rules: U.S. federal law "no comprehensive AI statute", executive "EO 14179; EO 14365 (Dec 2025)", states "TRAIGA from Jan 1, 2026", cities "LL144 since July 5, 2023"; note on Colorado's replaced act. Fakes and labels: EU "Art. 50 labels; new bans", U.S. federal "TAKE IT DOWN Act; FCC ruling", states "TRAIGA: sexual deepfakes"; note: these rules protect the people depicted and voters and do not decide whether training was lawful.

**F18.6 (f18-06-ross-factors).** A four-row comparison with columns for the trial court (February 2025) and the Third Circuit (September 2026). Purpose: against fair use in both. Nature: for fair use, and slightly for. Amount, boxed with a thick border: for fair use in the trial court (outputs did not show headnotes) but against in the Third Circuit (whole headnotes copied and copying not needed), the factor decided the other way. Market: against in both. Bottom: result in both courts, not fair use for the 2,243 headnotes. Note: the appeals court distinguished generative models in a footnote; that is not a holding.

**F18.7 (f18-07-rulings-scale).** A two-headed arrow from "Not fair use" to "Fair use" with four rounded boxes above it: Ross (appeals, 2026; dark, thick), a non-generative tool competing with Westlaw, at the far left; Bartz's pirated library, left of center; Kadrey (2025), fair use on this record because the wrong arguments were made, right of center; Bartz's training (training on books; scanning bought books), at the right. Note: Ross is an appeals-court ruling binding in the Third Circuit; Bartz and Kadrey are trial-court rulings binding only their own cases.

**F18.8 (f18-08-menu-layers).** Three stacked layers of Hana's menu cover and a side box: the owner's generated draft (hexagon; layout reference only; not protected, no human author); Hana's hand-drawn illustration (rounded, dark; Hana's own expression, protected); the owner's arrangement of text and pictures (at most thin protection); the bakery's logo as a brand (trademark protects the bakery's brand in trade; its artwork may also have copyright).

**F18.9 (f18-09-spectrum-copyright).** Chapter 10's voice and ownership spectrum (positions 1 to 5 by the share of wording a tool supplies) with three dashed brackets marking this book's inferred mapping: positions 1–2, "yours: protected"; positions 3–4, "only your own parts protected"; position 5, "no human author." Note: a reading of the U.S. Copyright Office's 2025 report, not a legal test.

**F18.11 (f18-11-chain-retail).** Chapter 4's failure-to-harm chain for the constructed return-window case: link 1, the output is wrong (a 60-day return window); link 2, checked before use? (thick: policy answers never checked); link 3, someone relies on it (the customer returns on day 45); link 4, loss (refund refused; dispute); a "caught" branch below. Tag: who is responsible, the shop, as deployer (this book's reading, from Moffatt, 2024).

**F18.12 (f18-12-ai-act-tiers).** Four stacked boxes widening downward: prohibited practices (thick, hatched in print; for example, inferring emotions from faces or voices at work or school; social scoring; applies from February 2, 2025); high-risk systems (admission, grading, test monitoring, CV screening; duties scheduled from December 2, 2027); transparency duties (tell people it is AI; mark generated content; label deepfakes; applies from August 2, 2026, with marking from December 2, 2026 for systems already on the market); minimal risk (spam filters, spell-checkers; no new duties beyond AI literacy; most uses). A hexagon: general-purpose AI models, duties from August 2, 2025. Source note: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 113, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.

**F18.14 (f18-15-flow-rules).** Chapter 15's data-flow map (you, input, connectors, AI tool, output, recipients, storage, seen by, reuse, with the trust boundary as a shaded band) annotated with the rules to look for: input, "whose work? which data class?"; storage, "retention terms; FERPA or HIPAA (18.1)"; reuse, "training terms"; output, "who owns it? a label?"; recipients, "hiring or notice rules". Note: at the tool, its terms and license; list rules at each box and write the highest-stakes one as a risk.

**F18.15 (f18-16-risk-sheet).** A nine-row sheet: 1 scratch list (prompts a–e on the data-flow map); 2 choose the highest-stakes rule and name its layer; 3 the risk template; 4 evidence (D: the rule's own text opened, section cited; I: give the reason); 5 who is harmed; 6 rating; 7 mitigation with the risk owner; 8 the rule's owner and your question; 9 the last line, not a legal opinion. Footer: drafted in Lab 15.1, step 6; finished at home.

**F18.16 (f18-17-heat-zine).** Signature 10, the Risk Heat Map, with one documented risk (D, solid outline): contributors losing their right to decide how copies of their drawings are used. Hollow marker at medium likelihood and medium impact; an arrow to a filled marker at low likelihood, medium impact. Note: reduce with a no-training tool and an AI line on the form; owner: Hana, as editor.

**F18.19 (f18-13-aiact-dates).** A dot chart of five EU AI Act duty groups against years 2025 to 2030. Prohibited practices and AI literacy (February 2, 2025), general-purpose AI models (August 2, 2025) and transparency (August 2, 2026): one filled circle each, unchanged. High-risk duties for listed uses move by an arrow from August 2026 (hollow circle) to December 2, 2027 (filled square); for AI in products, from August 2027 to August 2, 2028. A thin solid line marks October 2026.

**F18.20 (f18-20-flow-c15).** Chapter 15's data-flow map without the trust boundary, with input and reuse highlighted: you = Ross (and its memo writers); input = memos written from Westlaw headnotes; reuse = training the tool; output = passages of court decisions, not headnotes. Note: an input-side harm; no output was wrong, so the Failure Taxonomy Tree does not apply.
