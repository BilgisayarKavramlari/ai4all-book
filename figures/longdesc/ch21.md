## Long descriptions

**F21.2 (f21-02-storyboard).** Six boxes in two rows, all with solid outlines, read left to right and then the second row. Row 1: "1992: the code's last update before 2018"; "2016–2018: two-year update; drafts published for comment"; "June 22, 2018: ACM Council adopts the update". An arrow leads down to row 2: "Structure: principles + guidelines + compliance (Section 4)"; "Its own limit: not an algorithm; a basis for judgment"; "October 2026: still the current version (page archived Jul 9, 2026)". Source line: ACM's 2018 booklet and July 17, 2018 press release, and ACM's code page as archived July 9, 2026. Footer: about 26 years passed between the two versions.

**F21.3 (f21-03-two-codes).** A two-column table. Column headers: "A profession's code (e.g., ACM, 2018)" in a plain rectangle and "Your personal code" in a thick rounded box. Rows: Who writes it — a professional body with members' input / you. Whom it binds — its members (for ACM, every ACM member) / you, inside the rules that bind you. Who enforces it — the body (ACM's Enforcement Policy) / you, through your marks and your review. How it is updated — rarely, by the body (1992 to 2018) / at your review date or on a trigger. Footer: your code borrows a profession's habit of reasons and revision but cannot borrow its enforcement.

**F21.4 (f21-04-trigger-test).** A flow from left to right. A rectangle "A line in your draft" leads to three diamonds in turn: "Moment?" (would I notice it then, from something I can see?), "Action?" (can I or someone else trace it afterward?), "Reason?" (does it point to my own evidence?). "Yes" arrows link the diamonds and lead to a thick rounded green box "Commitment". From each diamond a "no" arrow points down to a wide thick box, hatched in print, "Rewrite: name a visible moment, a trace, or your evidence", from which an arrow returns to the starting rectangle. Note: a rewritten line takes the test again.

**F21.5 (f21-05-card-hana) and F21.8 (f21-08-card-marcus).** Both use the principles card (described under F21.11). Hana's card: scope "Hana, freelance illustrator who runs a small zine · constructed example"; five commitments, reasons shortened: (1) before any AI image generation on a client job, get written agreement, because of Hana's clause and the bakery job (Chapter 18); (2) before uploading a drawing to any tool, check its data terms and log the tool and date (two tools' terms differed); (3) before sending an invoice, list any AI use in its notes, because clients already ask about these tools; (4, a risk line) before contributors' scans go into any AI tool, check the risk and the submission form (Hana's Chapter 18 risk sheet: a tool's terms let uploads train models); (5) before an image or quotation goes into the zine, trace it and file the record (a fake "1890s photo" once came in). Boundaries: client contracts, Hana's course's AI rules and the rules of the zine collective Hana belongs to; the stricter wins; ask the client or the collective. Review by the first week of next term; triggers: a new contract, a change in a tool's terms, a change to the zine's form. Dated note: the terms of the tool Hana's school provides say prompts are not used to train its foundation models (as of October 2026). The commitments block is highlighted. Marcus's card: scope "nursing student, clinical placement and night shifts"; five commitments: (1) before pasting or uploading anything into an AI tool, check it holds no information about a patient, even unnamed; (2) build study material only from his textbook and his own notes, file marked "own sources"; (3) on catching himself typing "just tell me" in a study session, stop and note it in his study log; (4) before relying on a drug fact from an AI tool, check it in the course's drug reference and note the page; (5) when he submits coursework, his declaration lists every AI use, checked against his session history. Boundaries (highlighted, dark fill): his course's AI rules, his institution's data rules and his placement site's rules; the stricter wins; ask his clinical instructor. Review by the start of his next placement; triggers: a new site's rules, a new tool, a mistake he makes. Dated note: his university provides an enterprise chat assistant to all students (as of October 2026). The commitments header on both cards reads "Commitments (5–7, including the four required lines)".

**F21.6 (f21-06-ladder-code).** Signature 7, the Delegation Ladder, unchanged from Chapter 5: a floor (no AI) and three rungs (automation, augmentation, agency) with the axis "harder to see and check what the AI contributed before it takes effect". The agency rung is highlighted, with the note: "my trigger: before a tool I set up acts for others, a person sees each consequential action". Footer: a commitment for the top rung is an approval gate before consequential actions (Chapter 14).

**F21.7 (f21-07-map-code).** The Regulation Map from Chapter 18: a central rounded box "Your AI use and the people it affects" with arrows from five law boxes above (EU law; U.S. federal law; U.S. executive; U.S. states; cities) and three boxes below (Terms and licenses; Contracts; Your organization's policy). Highlighted for Hana's code: Terms and licenses ("tool terms (see Hana's dated note)"), Contracts ("client clause: written OK first") and Your organization's policy ("course AI rules + Hana's code"). Note: strictest rule wins; ask its owner (section 18.1); as of October 2026; not legal advice.

**F21.9 (f21-09-shelf-lives).** Three rows. Commitments (years, thick border): "Before I send anything others will rely on, I check every claim that carries a number, a name or a date." Practices (one to two years): a prompt template, a checklist, a test-input set, a review routine. Tool notes (months, light fill): which tool, which model, its price, its data terms, a setting, each "as of [month year]". A vertical arrow at the right marks faster change downward. Source: the book's own dating rule, not a measured result.

**F21.10 (f21-10-review-cycle).** A diamond "Review date reached, or a review trigger?" leads ("yes") to a rectangle "Reread your marks: which triggers fired, which never did", which branches to three rounded boxes: Keep (it fired and held), Revise (it failed, or a rule changed), Retire (it no longer applies). All three lead to a thick rounded box "Set the next date", from which a line returns along the bottom to the diamond ("the next date starts the cycle again"). Footer: review triggers are Chapter 12's re-test triggers plus a new rule from someone who binds you and a failure you met.

**F21.11 (f21-11-principles-card).** The principles card introduced in this chapter, as a blank template inside one outline: a rounded box at the top for scope (who I am in this code, and the date); a commitments block with five numbered ruled lines, each "When [a moment I will notice], I [an action I or someone else can trace], because [my evidence]", headed "Commitments (5–7, including the four required lines)"; a thick-bordered boundaries box; a review box marked with a calendar icon and a thick top rule ("by [date, ≤ 6 months] · review triggers: [two or more]"); and a dated-notes box. Legend: rounded = your choice; thick border = the rules around your code; calendar = a date you set. No dashed outline is used.

**F21.12 (f21-12-portfolio-code).** Signature 12, the Portfolio Anatomy, drawn by Chapter 20's function: career narrative at the top, the evidence map, then four parts of each entry side by side (artifact, revision trace, verification trail, AI Use Declaration), then the reflection and the code of practice side by side, then the oral defense ("live, no tool"). The code of practice is highlighted ("this section"). Note: your code sits beside your reflection, below the entries and their declarations; the defense may ask about one line.
