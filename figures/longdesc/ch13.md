## Long descriptions (complex figures)

**F13.2 (f13-02-storyboard).** A horizontal timeline with five events, alternating above and below the line. 1, Email: a phishing email from someone posing as the head office's chief financial officer; the employee first doubted it (the doubt is reported by CNN). 2, Video conference: pre-recorded (confirmed by the government's reply), made from public clips and voices, as the police believe. 3, Instant messages: the fraudster ended the meeting under a pretext and sent instructions by messaging (confirmed). 4, Transfers: to five local accounts, about HK$200 million (confirmed). 5, Later check with head office: the fraud was found (reported, CNN). Boxes 1–3 are orange (hatched with a thick border in print): channels the fraudsters controlled. Box 5 is rounded and green: a person's check. Footer: sources, Hong Kong SAR Government (2024) and Chen & Magramo (2024); the company later confirmed it was Arup (Magramo, 2024).

**F13.3b (f13-03b-modality-tokens).** Three input lanes on the left, each a rounded box (what you provide) followed by a processing rectangle: your words are split into text tokens; an image is cut into patches and each patch becomes an image token; a recording is cut into short slices and a codec turns each into an audio token. All three lanes point into one tall rectangle, one context window, holding tokens of every kind side by side, which points to a hexagon, the model, which produces output. A footnote says that file information such as date, place and camera is not part of the tokens in two providers' tools, but the file itself still reaches the provider (as of October 2026).

**F13.4 (f13-04-pipeline-image).** Six steps left to right: prompt (rounded; words describing the image) → text encoder (turns the words into numbers) → random noise (the starting point) → remove a little noise (hexagon; repeated many times, steered by the prompt; a curved arrow labeled loop: many steps) → decoder (turns the result into pixels) → new image (plausible for the prompt; no event behind it). Below, a gray box for the earlier training stage: very many images with captions, noise added, settings adjusted to predict and remove it, with an arrow up to the denoising step. Sources: Ho et al. (2020); Rombach et al. (2022).

**F13.5 (f13-05-pipeline-voice).** Five steps: text to speak (rounded) and a short voice sample of one person, three seconds in the 2023 study (rounded, highlighted) → predict audio tokens one after another (hexagon) → codec decoder → new speech in an imitation of that voice. Footnote: a 2023 research system (Wang et al., 2023), trained on 60,000 hours of English speech; not a product.

**F13.6 (f13-06-camera-vs-generator).** Two rows of four boxes. Camera row: an event (rounded) → light from it → camera sensor → photo file, a record of the event (green). Generator row: prompt and noise (rounded) → generator (hexagon) → image file, plausible for the prompt → no event (gray box: nothing behind the picture). Caption line: both files can look alike; only one has an event behind it.

**F13.7 (f13-07-who-checks).** Four boxes in a two-by-two grid. Alt text: relies, a screen-reader user; can check, rarely; checks, the author who posts it (orange). Captions: relies, a viewer who is deaf or hard of hearing; can check, rarely; checks, whoever publishes the video (orange). Transcript: relies, a reader of the record; can check, only with the audio; checks, whoever files it. Design draft: relies, a client; can check what they see, not how it was made; checks, the designer, who discloses. Below, a rounded green box: the check belongs to whoever publishes the output.

**F13.8 (f13-08-whisper-harms).** Horizontal bars, share of the 312 transcriptions that contained invented text: perpetuating violence 19%, inaccurate associations 13%, false authority 8%, at least one of these harms 38% (orange, hatched in print). Source note: 13,140 speech segments run twice in April and May 2023; 187 segments (about 1.4%) reliably produced invented text, and 312 transcriptions contained it (roughly 1% of transcriptions); categories overlap, so they add up to more than 38%; in December 2023, 12 of the 187 still did (Koenecke et al., 2024).

**F13.9 (f13-09-credential).** Left: an image file (the pixels) and a green credential box (signed statements: captured with, edited with, generated with) both feed a hash-binding box (a fingerprint of the pixels; changes show). Right: shared as the file, the credential is kept, a lead about origin (green); as a screenshot or re-upload (gray box), it is a new file whose credential is often stripped, unless the record was stored online and linked. Note: a credential states history as signed; it cannot say the scene is true (C2PA, 2026).

**F13.10 (f13-10-present-absent).** A three-by-three grid. Rows: content credential, watermark, platform label. Present and valid column (green): signed history of the file, not truth; the maker's tool produced it; the platform or uploader says AI was used. Missing column (light gray cells): proves nothing: stripped, never added, or removed.

**F13.11 (f13-11-human-detection).** Two panels. Left, faces (Nightingale & Farid, 2022), against a thin solid gray line at 50% (guessing): judged correctly without training 48.2% (315 participants), after training and feedback 59.0% (219 participants). Right, voices (Mai et al., 2023): 73% of speech deepfake clips correctly identified (529 participants; overall accuracy on real and fake clips 70.35%). Note: different studies, tasks and measures; compare each bar within its own panel.

**F13.12 (f13-12-detector-auc).** Paired bars for nine open-source detectors (three video, three audio, three image). Light bars, AUC on each detector's original test data: 0.87 to 1.00. Orange bars (hatched in print), AUC on real and fake media that users flagged in 2024: GenConViT 0.63, FTCN 0.50, Styleflow 0.51, AASIST 0.43, RawNet2 0.53, P3 0.58, UFD 0.56, DistilDIRE 0.52, NPR 0.53. A thin solid gray line marks 0.5, guessing. Note: the best of 22 anonymized commercial tools scored 0.79 (video), 0.93 (audio; partly credited by the authors to their audio labeling) and 0.90 (images); the benchmark was built with TrueMedia.org, a nonprofit detection service closed to the public in January 2025 (TrueMedia.org, 2025); source Chandra et al. (2025), version 5, Tables 3 and 5.

**F13.13 (f13-13-checklist).** A vertical check list. Stop (diamond; a trigger fired: pause before you act or share) → 1. Source and channel (who sent it? reach them another way) → 2. Earliest version (reverse image search) → 3. Outside sources (does anyone else report the event?) → 4. Provenance signals (present = lead; missing = proves nothing) → 5. Content clues (gray box: weak leads, checked last) → Verdict and remaining uncertainty (rounded, green), with Chapter 8's five verdicts.

**F13.15 (f13-15-protocol-card).** Six boxes in two rows: 1 triggers (highlighted: money, files, urgency, a new channel, proof offered, about to publish) → 2 pause (no action and no reply in the same channel) → 3 channel by role (the number on the contract; the directory); 4 checks by stakes (section 13.7) → 5 record (verdict and what remains uncertain) → 6 disclose (when media you share was generated or altered). Below, a green rounded box: test it on real items, then revise one rule.

**F13.17 (f13-17-chain-arup).** The failure-to-harm chain with four links and notes above: 1, the output (hexagon): a pre-recorded fake conference (police believe; confirmed); 2, checked before use? (diamond, thick border): no check with head office before the transfers (reported: CNN); 3, someone relies on it: instructions followed (confirmed); 4, loss: about HK$200 million (confirmed). A 'yes' branch leads to 'caught: fixed before harm'. Tag: confirmed = the government's reply; reported = CNN, citing police; inferred would be dashed.

**F13.18 (f13-18-pentagon).** Five hops: image posted by look-alike news accounts (maker and intent not documented) → spread on Twitter that morning → brief market dip, indices recovered → officials' statement: no explosion or incident (Pentagon Force Protection Agency and county fire department) → context check by an open-source investigator: no witnesses, no other images (Bond, 2023, NPR).
